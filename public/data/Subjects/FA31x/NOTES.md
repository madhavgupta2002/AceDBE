# Module 01: Introduction to Management Accounting

## 1.0 Welcome to Module 01
### Overview and Objectives
Management accounting is essentially "accounting for managers." It focuses on using accounting information for internal planning, controlling, and decision-making, rather than just technical rule-based reporting.

**Core Objectives:**
*   Understand how managers use accounting information in day-to-day operations.
*   Use accounting data for operating and strategic decision-making.
*   Perform managerial planning (budgeting) using cost data.
*   Control the business and measure performance via accounting metrics.

## 1.1 Financial Accounting & Cost Accounting
### Differences and Linkages
Management Accounting relies on data generated by both Financial and Cost Accounting systems. 

**Financial Accounting**
*   **Target Audience (Outsiders):** Investors, Lenders, Tax Authorities, Suppliers, Customers.
*   **Purpose:** To determine whether a profit was earned or a loss incurred, and to present the financial condition.
*   **Output:** Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement.

**Cost Accounting**
*   **Target Audience (Insiders):** Managers.
*   **Purpose:** To track the movement of all goods and services within the organization.
*   **Output:** Inventory Statement, Cost Sheet (shows cost per unit of goods produced/services delivered).

### The Role of Management Accounting
*   **Users:** Internal Managers.
*   **Purpose:** Computation of financial ratios (assessing fixed assets, inventory, receivables efficiency), pricing, outsourcing, product discontinuation, budgeting, and variance analysis.
*   **Four Broad Pillars:** 
    1.  Product Costing (Job & Process Costing)
    2.  Planning (Budgeting)
    3.  Decision Making (Operational & Strategic)
    4.  Controlling (Variance Analysis & Performance Measurement)

## 1.2 Basic Cost Terms and Concepts
### Definition of Cost
**Cost** refers to the monetary measure of resources given up in acquiring goods and services.

### Cost Classifications
Costs are grouped to aid managers in specific analyses:

| Classification Basis | Cost Type | Definition / Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time** | **Historical** | Past costs used for Financial Statements. |
| | **Replacement** | Present cost used for Decision Making and Insurance computation. |
| | **Budgeted** | Future cost used for Planning and Control. |
| **Volume** | **Variable Cost** | Changes proportionally with volume (e.g., Material, Labour). |
| | **Fixed Cost** | Unaffected by volume changes (e.g., Office Rent). |
| | **Mixed Cost** | Contains both fixed and variable elements (e.g., Base salary + Sales commission). |
| | **Step Cost** | A variation of mixed cost; remains fixed up to a capacity level, then jumps (e.g., Depreciation on a newly added machine when capacity is exceeded). |
| **Financial Statement**| **Unexpired Cost** | Resource not yet consumed (e.g., Value of a new machine on purchase day). |
| | **Expired Cost** | Consumed resource; moves to the Income Statement (e.g., Depreciation after one year). |
| | **Product Cost** | Inventoriable cost directly related to the product (e.g., Raw Material, Factory Wages). |
| | **Period Cost** | Non-inventoriable cost incurred during a period (e.g., Admin rent, Sales staff salary). |
| **Decision Making** | **Relevant Cost** | Incremental / Opportunity costs relevant to specific decisions (e.g., lost interest from extending credit periods). |
| | **Sunk Cost** | Past, irrecoverable costs not relevant for future decisions (e.g., past R&D spend). |

### Key Cost Formulas
*   $Prime\ Cost = Direct\ Material\ Cost + Direct\ Labour\ Cost$
*   $Conversion\ Cost = Direct\ Labour\ Cost + Manufacturing\ Overhead$
*   $Cost\ of\ Goods\ Manufactured\ (COGM) = Prime\ Cost + Manufacturing\ Overhead$
*   $Cost\ of\ Sales = COGM + Administrative\ Overhead + Selling\ \&\ Distribution\ Overhead$
    *   *Alternatively:* $Cost\ of\ Sales = Total\ Expenses - Closing\ Inventory$
*   $Profit = Sales - Cost\ of\ Sales$
*   $Profit\ Margin = \frac{Profit}{Sales}$
*   $Conversion\ Margin = \frac{Profit}{Conversion\ Cost}$

## 1.3 Costing Systems
### Three-Step Process of a Costing System
1.  **Establish Cost Objects and Cost Centers**
    *   **Cost Object:** The lowest point at which cost data is collected (e.g., material, salary, travel, specific customer, or job).
    *   **Cost Center:** A logical group of cost objects (e.g., Production Cost Center, Purchase Cost Center).
2.  **Cost Accumulation**
    *   Creating records to capture data as it is incurred (e.g., Material Requisition Slips, Employee Time Sheets, Machine Log Books).
3.  **Cost Assignment**
    *   Transferring accumulated common costs to revenue-generating products/services based on predetermined allocation rates (e.g., output quantity, material cost, labour cost, or machine hours). 

*Note: An alternative costing system designed to prevent the misallocation of complex common costs is called **Activity Based Costing (ABC)**.*

### Types of Costing Systems
| Feature | Job Costing | Process Costing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Firm Type** | Receives specific orders, then manufactures/delivers. | Mass manufacturing where output is identical. |
| **Examples** | IT companies, Construction, Automobile Service Stations. | Sugar, Cement, Fertilizer, Banks, Telecom. |
| **Cost Object** | The specific "Job" is the cost object. | Each "Process" is a cost center/object. |

## 1.4 Preparation of Cost Sheet
### Cost Sheet Flow and Allocation
A cost sheet traces the flow of costs from raw materials to the final cost of sales per unit. 
When allocating support service costs to production processes, predefined bases are used:
*   **Purchase Department / Stores:** Allocated based on *Cost of Raw Material*.
*   **Maintenance:** Allocated based on *Machine Hours*.
*   **Quality Control:** Allocated based on *Units Manufactured*.
*   **Personnel / Canteen:** Allocated based on *Labour Cost*.
*   **Accounts & Administration:** Allocated based on *Sales, Units Manufactured, or Number of Employees*.

### Handling Idle Capacity (Autocomp Example)
When charging fixed manufacturing overheads (like depreciation) to products, managers must decide how to handle unutilized (idle) machine hours:
*   **Charging Idle Time to Customers:** Using actual running hours as the denominator spikes the per-unit cost, penalizing the customer for the firm's idle capacity and making prices uncompetitive.
*   **Absorbing Idle Time (Relevant Approach):** Using total available capacity as the denominator keeps per-unit cost stable. It forces management to recognize unrecovered fixed costs as a loss, creating a behavioral incentive to aggressively improve machine utilization and win new orders.

## 1.5 Costing in the Service Industry
### Applicability
The service sector (Banking, Insurance, Healthcare, Tourism, IT, Not-for-profits like Churches/Temples) uses standard costing principles tailored to their operations.
*   **Job Costing:** Used for specific, customized service contracts (e.g., L&T constructing an airport, IT project).
*   **Process Costing:** Used for standardized, repetitive services (e.g., Insurance policy processing, Bank loan approvals).
*   **Operating Costing (Hybrid):** Used by entities like Indian Railways. Costs are traced to the train (cost object) and split into phases: origination, transit (distance/passenger km), halting, and termination.

### Hospital Costing Example (United Medicare)
Hospitals trace direct costs (resident doctors, specific medicines) directly to patient services. Indirect support costs (building depreciation, administrative salaries) must be allocated using logical bases—such as allocating hospital administration costs to surgical or hospitalization departments based on their proportional *revenue generation* or *floor space*. Pricing is then determined by adding a target markup (e.g., 20% margin) to the calculated total cost per service.

## 1.6 Summary
*   **Financial accounting** reports to outsiders via standard statements.
*   **Cost accounting** tracks internal resource movement.
*   **Management accounting** utilizes both to execute **four broad roles**: Product Costing, Planning, Decision Making, and Controlling.
*   Understanding detailed cost behaviour (variable vs. fixed, relevant vs. sunk) is crucial to solving pricing, product mix, and capacity utilization problems.

## 1.7 Practice Exercises
### Key Analytical Insights
*   **Cost Sheet Sensitivity:** The per-unit cost of a product is highly sensitive to the *basis of allocation*. (e.g., Allocating common costs by *Labour Cost* vs. *Units Produced* can drastically shift which product appears more profitable, even if total company costs remain identical).
*   **Conversion Effort:** By splitting total costs into Prime Cost and Conversion Cost, managers can assess operational efficiency. In industries where raw material prices fluctuate heavily, tracking the Conversion Margin isolates the factory's actual value-creation efficiency.
*   **Direct vs. Indirect Classification:** In project-based businesses (e.g., Real Estate), a cost is direct if it is exclusively consumed by that project (e.g., a dedicated site manager, specific licensing fees). It becomes indirect if shared across multiple projects (e.g., corporate design team).

## 1.8 Case Study
### Special Order Pricing (RK Forging & Mini-Bar Scenarios)
*   **Relevant Costing for Special Orders:** When operating below full capacity, quoting for a special one-off order should rely on *Relevant Costing*. Sunk costs and fixed production overheads (already recovered by regular orders) are ignored. Pricing needs only to cover direct material, direct labour, and any specific one-time fixed costs (e.g., special molds). This allows the firm to quote aggressively, utilize idle capacity, and generate pure incremental profit.
*   **High Markups in Service (Mini-Bar):** Items like hotel mini-bar drinks carry massive markups (e.g., 500% over market price) not for pure profit, but because the *indirect costs* (premium real estate rent, 24/7 staff, centralized AC) allocated to the Food & Beverage division are extraordinarily high compared to a standard retail shop.

# Module 02: Product Costing and Activity Based Costing

## 2.1 Methods of Product Costing
### Overview of Costing Methods
Organizations use different costing methods depending on the nature of their manufacturing or service delivery process. 

| Method | Application Context | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Job Costing** | Customized products or services based on specific customer orders. | IT projects, Construction, Tailoring. |
| **Process Costing** | Continuous mass production where output units are identical. | Cement, Steel, Sugar, Banking services. |

### Variations of Product Costing
*   **Customer Costing:** An extension of job costing used for firms with large B2B customers.
*   **Batch Costing:** A variation of process costing where products are processed in batches (e.g., Pharmaceuticals, FMCG).
*   **Operating Costing:** Used for continuous service industries (e.g., Railway services, Transport).

## 2.2 Job Costing
### Mechanics of Job Costing
*   **Cost Object:** The specific "Job" or "Order" is the cost object.
*   **Direct Costs:** Traced directly to the job using source documents:
    *   **Material Requisition Slip:** Captures direct material costs.
    *   **Time Sheets / Machine Log Books:** Captures direct labour and machine time.
*   **Prime Cost Formula:** 
    $Prime\ Cost = Direct\ Material + Direct\ Expenses$
*   **Indirect Costs (Overheads):** Common costs (e.g., manager salary, rent) allocated to jobs based on bases like units, labour hours, or machine hours.

### Types of Job Costing
| Costing Type | Direct Costs | Indirect Costs | Primary Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Costing** | Actual | Budgeted / Estimated | **Decision Making** (Pricing jobs immediately) |
| **Actual Costing** | Actual | Actual | **Controlling** (Accounting reconciliation) |
| **Standard Costing** | Budgeted | Budgeted | **Planning** (Quoting tenders/bids) |

## 2.3 Customer Costing
### Concept and Application
*   **Cost Object:** The individual **Customer** becomes the cost object.
*   **Purpose:** Evaluates profitability at the customer level by identifying hidden costs.
*   **Issue Addressed:** Different customers demand different service levels (e.g., frequent deliveries, multiple customized designs, smaller order sizes). 
*   **Impact:** A standard markup on variable costs ignores these demands. Without customer costing, low-maintenance customers (standard orders) end up subsidizing high-maintenance customers (custom orders).

## 2.4 Process Costing
### Mechanics of Process Costing
*   **Cost Object:** The **Process** or **Department**. Costs are accumulated per process and divided by output.
*   **Cost Flow:** Output of one process becomes the raw material (transferred-in cost) for the next process.
*   **Equivalent Units (EU):** Used to convert partially completed Work-In-Progress (WIP) into fully completed equivalent units for accurate per-unit costing.
    *   $Equivalent\ Units = [Completed\ Units \times 100\%] + [WIP\ Units \times \%\ of\ Completion]$
*   **Cost per Equivalent Unit:**
    *   $Cost\ per\ EU = \frac{Total\ Cost\ incurred}{Total\ Equivalent\ Units}$
*   **Valuation:** Total cost is split between **Completed Units** and **Closing WIP**.

### Methods of Process Valuation
*   **Weighted Average Method:** Merges opening WIP costs with current period costs. Does not distinguish between opening stock and new units introduced.
*   **FIFO (First-In-First-Out):** Separates opening WIP from new units. Assumes opening stock is completed first.
*   **LIFO (Last-In-First-Out):** Assumes the newest units are completed first (rarely used).

## 2.5 Treatment of Process Losses
### Types of Losses
Processes naturally incur losses, wastage, or defective units. The accounting treatment depends on the nature of the loss:

| Loss Type | Definition | Accounting Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Loss** | Expected, unavoidable loss inherent to the production process (e.g., evaporation). | **Absorbed by good units.** Cost is spread across completed units and WIP, increasing the cost per unit. |
| **Abnormal Loss** | Unexpected loss due to inefficiency, machine breakdown, or defective materials. | **Separated and charged to the P&L account.** Treated as a period cost to prevent distorting product costs. Enables root-cause investigation. |

## 2.6 Joint and By-products
### Product Classification by Economic Value
*   **Joint Products:** Multiple products of relatively high and equal sales value produced from a single process (e.g., Petrol, Diesel, LPG from an oil refinery).
*   **By-products:** Products with minor sales value generated incidentally (e.g., Molasses in a sugar industry).
*   **Scrap:** Residue with minimal or zero value (e.g., Sawdust). *Note: Scrap is not separately costed; sales revenue is credited to manufacturing overhead or treated as other income.*

### Methods of Splitting Joint Costs
1.  **Physical Quantity Method:** Split based on weight or volume (useful only if products share the same unit of measurement).
2.  **Sales Value at Split-off:** Split based on the market value of products at the separation point. (Most widely used).
3.  **Estimated Realizable Value:** Used when products require further processing before sale. Split based on final sales value minus post-split processing costs.
4.  **Estimated Gross Margin:** Split based on the profit margin of the products.

## 2.7 Introduction to Activity Based Costing
### Limitations of Traditional Costing
*   Traditional costing pools indirect costs broadly (e.g., factory overhead) and allocates them using volume-based drivers (e.g., machine hours, labour hours, units).
*   **The Problem:** It assumes high-volume products consume more indirect resources, which is false for complex or customized products.
*   **The Result:** Cross-subsidization. High-volume standard products are **over-costed**, and low-volume customized products are **under-costed**.

## 2.8 Indirect Cost
### The Growth of Overheads
*   Indirect costs (Overheads) include supervisor salaries, rent, depreciation, and facility maintenance.
*   **Automation Impact:** With modern flexible manufacturing systems, direct labour decreases while indirect costs (machine setup, software, maintenance) increase significantly. 
*   If indirect costs are large, arbitrary allocation severely distorts product costs.

## 2.9 Method of Indirect Cost Allocation
### Allocating Service Department Costs
Service departments (HR, Maintenance, Accounting) support Production departments. Their costs must be allocated to production before being charged to products.

| Allocation Method | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct Method** | Allocates service department costs directly to production departments, ignoring services provided to other service departments. |
| **Step-wise Allocation** | Allocates costs sequentially. Ranks service departments (usually starting with the one providing the most service to others or having the lowest cost) and allocates its cost to all subsequent departments. Once closed, a department receives no further allocations. |
| **Simultaneous Equation** | Uses mathematics to fully recognize reciprocal services between all service departments. Highly accurate but complex. |

## 2.10 Misallocation of Indirect Costs
### Consequences of Poor Allocation
*   **Distorted Decision Making:** Misallocated costs lead to incorrect pricing, faulty product-mix decisions, and misplaced marketing focus.
*   **Example:** A retail store allocating warehouse costs based purely on the purchase price of goods. A small, expensive bottle of perfume gets loaded with high warehouse costs, while a bulky, cheap sack of wheat flour gets minimal costs. In reality, the flour requires more physical handling, unpacking, and shelf space.

## 2.11 Activity Based Costing
### Core Philosophy
*   **Concept:** Organizations are a bundle of activities. Products consume activities, and activities consume resources.
*   **Cost Drivers:** Factors that cause activity costs to change (e.g., number of setups, number of purchase orders).

### Classification of Activities (Cost Hierarchy)
| Activity Level | Description | Examples | Cost Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unit-Level** | Performed for every single unit produced. | Machining a part, inspecting a unit, issuing a boarding pass. | Variable |
| **Batch-Level** | Performed for a group of products, regardless of batch size. | Machine setup, material handling, placing a purchase order. | Mixed |
| **Product-Level** | Performed to support an entire product line. | Product design, R&D, preparing service manuals. | Fixed |
| **Facility-Level** | Sustains general manufacturing/business operations. Not tied to specific products. | Security, building rent, factory lighting, payroll processing. | Fixed |

## 2.12 Implementation of Activity Based Costing
### Steps to Implement ABC
1.  **Form an ABC Team:** Involve line managers from all departments, ensuring decentralized ownership.
2.  **Identify Activities:** Managers list distinct departmental activities (e.g., tendering, vendor evaluation).
3.  **Create a Master List of Activities:** Compile all activities, resource requirements, and cost drivers.
4.  **Develop Bill of Activities (BOA):** Similar to a Bill of Materials, the BOA lists all activities required to produce a specific product or service.

### Value Analysis
*   **Core / Value-Added Activities:** Activities that directly increase the value of the product to the customer (e.g., manufacturing, designing). Focus on continuous improvement here for cost leadership.
*   **Non-Core / Non-Value-Added Activities:** Activities that do not add value from the customer's perspective (e.g., expediting delayed orders, excessive quality checking due to old machinery). ABC helps identify these for elimination or outsourcing.

## 2.13 Activity Based Management
### Concept and Application
**Activity Based Management (ABM)** is the use of ABC data for operational and strategic management.
*   **Activity Based Budgeting (ABB):** Preparing budgets at the activity level rather than the functional departmental level.
*   **Performance Measurement:** ABM evaluates activities across three dimensions:
    1.  **Cost** (Efficiency)
    2.  **Time** (Speed / Delivery)
    3.  **Quality** (Defect rate)
*   **Pricing Decisions:** Pricing is based on the actual effort/activities consumed by the product, rather than flat margins on raw materials. This corrects distorted pricing schemes where simple products cross-subsidize complex ones.

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Job vs. Process Costing:** Job = Unique/Custom orders. Process = Identical/Mass production.
*   **Normal Costing:** Uses actual direct costs + *budgeted* indirect costs. Essential for immediate pricing decisions.
*   **Equivalent Units (EU):** Used in process costing to value WIP. $EU = Completed\ Units + (WIP \times \% Complete)$.
*   **Process Losses:** Normal loss is absorbed by good units (increases per-unit cost). Abnormal loss is isolated and charged to P&L.
*   **Service Dept Allocation:** Step-wise method ranks service departments and allocates sequentially without looping back.
*   **Traditional Costing Flaw:** Allocates overheads on volume (units/hours), causing standard products to subsidize complex, low-volume custom products.
*   **ABC Cost Hierarchy:** Unit-level (per item), Batch-level (per group), Product-level (per product line), Facility-level (general overhead).
*   **ABM (Activity Based Management):** Uses ABC data to manage operations, optimize non-value-added activities, and evaluate performance based on Time, Quality, and Cost.

# Module 03: Cost Analysis for Decision-Making

## 3.0 Welcome to Module 03
### Overview
Managers rely on accounting information to make critical business decisions. This module covers the analytical frameworks used to determine pricing, evaluate internal production versus third-party procurement (make or buy), allocate scarce resources among multiple products, and decide whether to continue or shut down specific products and divisions.

## 3.1 Absorption vs. Marginal Costing
### Costing Frameworks for Decision-Making
There are two primary approaches to presenting cost data:

| Feature | Absorption Costing (Full Costing) | Marginal Costing (Variable Costing) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary User** | External stakeholders (Financial Statements). | Internal managers (Decision Making). |
| **Cost Treatment** | Pools all costs (Fixed + Variable) into the Cost of Goods Sold. | Separates costs based on behavior (Variable vs. Fixed). |
| **Fixed Costs** | Treated as a **Product Cost**. Allowed to move to the next period through closing stock valuation. | Treated as a **Period Cost**. Charged fully in the period it is incurred. |
| **Closing Stock** | Contains both fixed and variable costs. | Contains only variable costs. |
| **Profit Computation**| $Profit = Sales - Cost\ of\ Goods\ Sold$ | $Contribution = Sales - Variable\ Cost$<br>$Profit = Contribution - Fixed\ Cost$ |

*Key Insight:* Absorption costing can distort short-term decision-making because fixed costs per unit drop as production volume increases. Marginal costing isolates fixed costs, providing a clearer picture of true product profitability.

## 3.2 Behavior of Costs
### Understanding Cost Dynamics
To apply marginal costing, costs must be classified by how they react to volume changes:
*   **Variable Cost:** Changes linearly with volume (e.g., Raw Materials, Direct Labour). If there is no production, there is no variable cost.
*   **Fixed Cost:** Remains constant regardless of production volume (e.g., Rent, Depreciation, Managerial Salaries).
*   **Mixed Cost:** Contains both fixed and variable elements (e.g., Repairs & Maintenance, Electricity).

### Methods to Classify Mixed Costs into Fixed and Variable
1.  **Account Analysis:** A subjective method where managers use their insight to estimate the split (e.g., determining 30% of maintenance is fixed). Misses changing cost structures over time.
2.  **High-Low Method:** Objective method using the highest and lowest production volumes.
    *   $Variable\ Cost\ per\ unit = \frac{Cost\ at\ Highest\ Volume - Cost\ at\ Lowest\ Volume}{Highest\ Volume - Lowest\ Volume}$
    *   $Fixed\ Cost = Total\ Cost - (Variable\ Cost\ per\ unit \times Volume)$
3.  **Scatter Graph:** Plot total cost on the Y-axis and volume on the X-axis. Draw a line of best fit. The slope represents the variable cost per unit, and the Y-intercept represents the fixed cost.
4.  **Regression Method:** A statistical linear equation using all historical data points for maximum reliability.
    *   Equation: $Sales / Volume = a + b(Total\ Cost)$ where **a = Fixed Cost** and **b = Variable Cost**.
    *   *Note:* If the regression yields a negative fixed cost, the model must be rerun with the intercept forced to zero ($Constant = 0$).

## 3.3 Break-Even Analysis
### Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis
CVP Analysis determines how changes in volume affect costs and profit. 

**Assumptions of CVP Analysis:**
*   Revenue, variable cost, and contribution per unit are constant and linear.
*   Total fixed cost is constant within the relevant range.
*   Mixed costs can be perfectly separated into fixed and variable components.
*   Sales volume equals production volume (no major inventory fluctuations).
*   Sales mix remains constant in multi-product firms.
*   Inflation, labour productivity, and technology remain unchanged.

### Key Formulas
*   **Contribution Margin:** $CM = Sales - Variable\ Cost$
*   **Contribution Margin Ratio (CMR):** $CMR = \frac{Contribution\ Margin}{Sales}$
*   **Break-Even Point (BEP) in Units:** $BEP = \frac{Fixed\ Cost}{Contribution\ Margin\ per\ unit}$
*   **Break-Even Point (BEP) in Sales Value:** $BEP = \frac{Fixed\ Cost}{CMR}$
*   **Margin of Safety (MOS):** $MOS = Current\ Sales - BEP\ Sales$
*   **Profit:** $Profit = MOS\ (in\ units) \times Contribution\ per\ unit$

### BEP Analysis in a Multiproduct Situation
When a firm sells multiple products, BEP is calculated using a **Sales-Mix Bag**.
1.  Determine the sales mix ratio (e.g., Product A: 3 units, Product B: 2 units).
2.  Define one "Bag" based on this ratio.
3.  Calculate the **Contribution per Bag**: Sum of (Contribution per unit $\times$ units in the bag).
4.  Calculate **Break-Even Bags**: $BEP\ Bags = \frac{Fixed\ Cost}{Contribution\ per\ Bag}$.
5.  Multiply the BEP Bags by the unit ratio to find the exact BEP volume for each product.
*Note: Any change in the sales mix will alter the break-even point.*

## 3.4 Relevant Cost Analysis
### Pricing Decision
*   **Normal Pricing:** $Internal\ Price = Cost + Desired\ Profit$. In the long run, the price must yield a positive contribution and cover fixed costs.
*   **Special Order / Export Pricing:** Fixed costs already recovered by regular sales are irrelevant. The minimum acceptable price is the **Variable Cost**. 
*   **Rule:** $Special\ Order\ Price = Variable\ Cost + Normal\ Mark-up$. 

### Make or Buy Decision (Outsourcing)
*   **Objective:** Achieve cost competitiveness by comparing internal manufacturing costs against third-party quotes.
*   **Relevant Costs for Internal Manufacturing:** All variable costs + **Exclusive/Avoidable Fixed Costs** (e.g., machine rent specific to that product).
*   **Irrelevant Costs:** General allocated fixed costs (these remain even if outsourced).
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Must be added to the internal manufacturing cost (e.g., if outsourcing frees up machinery that can be sold, the interest/return on that capital is an opportunity cost of continuing internal production).

### Discontinuing Product or Closing Down Division
*   Products offering a **negative contribution** ($Sales < Variable\ Cost$) are primary targets for discontinuation.
*   **Relevant Costs Saved:** Variable costs and product-specific fixed costs.
*   **Irrelevant Costs:** Allocated corporate fixed costs (these will just shift to surviving products).
*   *Strategic Check:* A loss-making product under absorption costing might still generate a positive contribution margin. Discontinuing it would lower overall company profits.

## 3.5 Optimal Product Mix
### Handling Resource Constraints
When resources (machine hours, labour hours, raw materials) are limited, the goal is to maximize total profit using the constraint.
*   **Decision Rule:** Rank and select products based on the **Contribution per unit of the Limiting Factor**.
*   **Formulas:**
    *   Machine Hour Constraint: $\frac{Contribution\ per\ unit}{Machine\ Hours\ per\ unit}$
    *   Labour Hour Constraint: $\frac{Contribution\ per\ unit}{Labour\ Hours\ per\ unit}$
*   **Allocation Steps:** Satisfy minimum production requirements first. Then, allocate the remaining surplus constrained resource to the product with the highest rank until demand is met, followed by the next ranked product.

## 3.6 Operating Leverage
### Measuring Operating Risk
Business risk stems from fixed obligations. High fixed costs mean higher operating risk but faster profit growth once BEP is crossed.
*   **Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL):** Measures how sensitive profit is to a change in sales volume.
    *   $DOL = \frac{Contribution\ Margin}{Profit\ Before\ Interest\ and\ Taxes\ (PBIT)}$
*   **Degree of Financial Leverage (DFL):** Measures financial risk from borrowings/interest.
    *   $DFL = \frac{PBIT}{Profit\ Before\ Tax\ (PBT)}$
*   **Total Leverage:** Combines operating and financial risk.
    *   $Total\ Leverage = DOL \times DFL = \frac{Contribution\ Margin}{PBT}$
*   **CVP Index:** Evaluates the balance of profitability and risk.
    *   $CVP\ Index = Contribution\ Margin\ Ratio \times \frac{Current\ Sales}{BEP\ Sales}$

## 3.7 Summary
*   **Absorption vs. Marginal:** Marginal costing is essential for internal decision-making as it properly isolates fixed and variable behaviors.
*   **CVP Framework:** Allows managers to perform break-even calculations, optimize multiproduct sales mixes, and set target profits.
*   **Relevant Costing:** Crucial for Special Orders, Make vs. Buy, and Product Discontinuation. Ignore sunk costs and allocated overheads; focus only on incremental variable and avoidable fixed costs.
*   **Limiting Factors:** Always maximize contribution per unit of the constrained resource.

## 3.8 Practice Exercises & Case Study Insights
*   **Volatility Analysis:** High volatility in profit despite stable sales typically points to fluctuating raw material costs that the company is unable to pass on to customers via pricing.
*   **Competitor Cost Reconstruction:** If a competitor is consistently dropping prices, use CVP variables to reverse-engineer their cost structure. A steep drop in their variable cost ratio usually indicates heavy automation (converting variable labour costs into fixed depreciation costs).
*   **Opportunity Costs in Decisions:** When a new special order forces the rejection of regular sales, the lost contribution margin of the regular sales becomes a relevant opportunity cost that must be added to the minimum acceptable price of the special order.

***

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Absorption Costing:** Fixed costs = Product Cost (Inventory valuation).
*   **Marginal Costing:** Fixed costs = Period Cost (Written off immediately).
*   **Contribution Margin:** $Sales - Variable Cost$.
*   **BEP (Units):** $Fixed Cost / Contribution per unit$.
*   **Margin of Safety:** $Current Sales - BEP Sales$.
*   **Special Order Pricing:** Minimum price = Variable Cost + Avoidable Fixed Costs.
*   **Make or Buy:** Compare Vendor Price vs. (Internal Variable Cost + Avoidable Fixed Cost + Opportunity Cost).
*   **Optimal Product Mix:** Rank by $Contribution / Limiting Factor$.
*   **Operating Leverage (DOL):** $Contribution / PBIT$. Higher DOL = Higher Risk.


# Module 04: Budgeting and Variance Analysis

## 4.1 Introduction to Planning and Budgeting
### The Role of Budgeting
*   Accounting information serves as the foundation for organizational planning.
*   **Planning vs. Resources:** Needs generally exceed the availability of resources. To manage this deficit, managers have four options:
    1.  Reduce activities.
    2.  Defer activities to the next period.
    3.  Reallocate existing resources.
    4.  Borrow funds to meet the deficit.

## 4.2 Strategic Planning and Budgets
### Linkage Between Strategy and Budgets
*   **Vision Statement:** Discloses what the company aims to be in the long-run.
*   **Mission Statement:** Discloses how the company plans to achieve its vision.
*   **Long-Range Planning:** The next logical step to work towards achieving the vision statement (typically 5 to 10 years).
*   **Budgets:** The final step of the planning process. Budgets provide specific, quantitative, short-term targets (usually 1 year) for managers to execute the long-range plan.

## 4.3 Budgeting Process
### Steps and Master Budget Creation
*   Budgeting is a detailed process developed over a period of time, taking 2–3 months to complete.
*   It reduces conflicts of interest and aligns managerial actions with organizational goals.
*   **Process Flow:**
    1.  **Data Collection:** Gather historical data and market estimates.
    2.  **Key Factor Identification:** Identify the limiting factor (typically sales/demand, but could be machine hours or raw materials).
    3.  **Budget for the Key Factor:** Draft the primary budget (e.g., Sales Budget).
    4.  **Other Divisions Work on the Budget:** Functional heads negotiate and prepare subsequent budgets based on the key factor.
*   **Master Budget:** The final consolidated document consisting of:
    *   **Operating Budgets:** Sales, Production, Purchases, Direct Labour, Overhead, and Selling & Administrative Budgets (expressed in units and monetary value).
    *   **Financial Budgets:** Cash Budget, Capital Expenditure, Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement (aggregates monetary details).

## 4.4 Types of Budgets
### Common Budget Classifications
*   **Flexible Budget:** Drawn for different levels of capacity utilization. Ideal for commercial organizations with high fluctuations in demand.
*   **Zero-Based Budget (ZBB):** Requires critical examination and justification of every activity before funding is approved. Highly suitable for government agencies and R&D functions.
*   **Continuous or Rolling Budget:** Drawn every quarter to cover the subsequent twelve months, making budgeting an integrated, continuous exercise.
*   **Activity-Based Budgeting (ABB):** Relies on Activity-Based Costing (ABC).
    *   *Steps:* Determine cost per unit of activity $\rightarrow$ Determine activity demand based on production targets $\rightarrow$ Compute budgeted cost $\rightarrow$ Consolidate.
*   **Kaizen Budget:** Focuses on continuous improvement. Requires formal plans for improvement and specifies required resources (e.g., equipment, training).

## 4.5 Budget Benefits
### Advantages of Budgeting
*   Acts as a roadmap to achieve organizational goals and a guide to perform activities.
*   Brings discipline and makes cross-functional coordination easier.
*   Acts as a device for employee participation and establishes a foundation for cost control.
*   Identifies gaps in execution and ensures resources are acquired well in advance.
*   **Distributing Dissatisfaction:** Budgets manage the conflict between limited resources and high departmental demands in an orderly manner.

## 4.6 Approaches to Budget Preparation
### Imposed vs. Participatory
| Approach | Description | Best Suited For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Imposed Budget** | Top-down approach where the budget team prepares the plan and line managers execute it. | Start-ups, small businesses, times of economic crisis, or when managers lack budgetary skills. |
| **Participatory Budget** | Bottom-up approach prepared in consultation with the executing managers. | Well-established organizations, autonomous units, times of economic affluence, and managers with strong skills. |

## 4.7 Preparation of Master Budget
### Interdependency of Budgets
*   **Sales Budget:** The starting point (Quantity $\times$ Selling Price).
*   **Production Budget:** $Target\ Sales + Desired\ Closing\ Stock - Opening\ Stock = Production\ Quantity$.
*   **Purchase Budget:** $Material\ for\ Production + Desired\ Closing\ Material - Opening\ Material = Purchase\ Quantity$.
*   **Cash Budget:** Integrates credit policies to map exact cash inflows (collections) and outflows (payments to suppliers, expenses, capital expenditures).

## 4.8 Budget Slack
### Causes and Consequences
*   **Definition:** The intentional underestimation of revenues or overestimation of expenses by managers to create a "safe" buffer.
*   **Causes:** Fear of exposure, performance evaluation pressures, or fear of budget cuts in subsequent periods.
*   **Consequences:** Small slacks in every department add up to massive inefficiencies, defeating the planning objective. Leads to unnecessary year-end spending to exhaust allocated funds.
*   **Solution:** Implement Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB) or Activity-Based Budgeting (ABB) to eliminate arbitrary slacks.

## 4.9 Introduction to Variance Analysis
### Comparing Actuals to Budgets
*   **Macro Level:** Budgeted departmental costs are compared with actual costs to report overall differences.
*   **Micro Level:** Root causes of deviations are identified, and corrective control actions are initiated.

## 4.10 Setting Standards
### Establishing Baselines
*   Standards must be reasonably accurate and practically achievable (e.g., accounting for normal machine rest/maintenance times, not purely theoretical maximums).
*   **Material Standards:** Derived from R&D, engineering drawings, or bill of materials.
*   **Labour Standards:** Derived via time and motion studies or expert consultants.
*   **Overhead Standards:** Machine hour rates or Activity-Based Costing metrics.
*   Standards must be periodically reset whenever major changes occur in component costs or manufacturing processes.

## 4.11 Variance Analysis: General Framework
### The Mechanics of Variance
*   **Total Variance** splits into two core components: **Rate/Price Variance** and **Usage/Efficiency Variance**.
*   **Formula Structure:**
    *   $Actual\ Cost = Actual\ Price\ (AP) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (AQ)$
    *   $Standard\ for\ Actual = Standard\ Price\ (SP) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (AQ)$
    *   $Standard\ Cost = Standard\ Price\ (SP) \times Standard\ Quantity\ (SQ)$
*   **Price Variance** $= (AP - SP) \times AQ$ *(Assumes the department is efficient in usage).*
*   **Usage Variance** $= (AQ - SQ) \times SP$ *(Assumes the department is efficient on rate).*

## 4.12 Material and Labour Cost Variance
### Core Formulas
*   **Material Variances:**
    *   $Total\ Material\ Variance = Actual\ Material\ Cost - Standard\ Material\ Cost$
    *   $Material\ Price\ Variance = Actual\ Cost - Standard\ Cost\ for\ Actual\ Quantity$
    *   $Material\ Usage\ Variance = Standard\ Cost\ for\ Actual\ Quantity - Standard\ Cost$
*   **Labour Variances:**
    *   $Total\ Labour\ Variance = Actual\ Labour\ Cost - Budgeted\ Labour\ Cost$
    *   $Labour\ Rate\ Variance = (Actual\ Rate - Standard\ Rate) \times Actual\ Hours$
    *   $Labour\ Efficiency\ Variance = (Actual\ Hours - Standard\ Hours) \times Standard\ Rate$

## 4.13 Variable & Fixed Overhead Variance
### Overhead Variances
*   **Variable Overhead (VOH) Variance:**
    *   $VOH\ Spending\ Variance = (Actual\ Rate - Standard\ Rate) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (Hours)$
    *   $VOH\ Efficiency\ Variance = (Actual\ Quantity - Standard\ Quantity) \times Standard\ Rate$
*   **Fixed Overhead (FOH) Variance:**
    *   *Unlike VOH, total fixed costs do not change with volume.*
    *   $Total\ FOH\ Variance = Actual\ FOH - Applied\ FOH$
    *   $FOH\ Spending\ Variance = Actual\ FOH - Budgeted\ FOH$
    *   $FOH\ Volume\ Variance = Budgeted\ FOH - Applied\ FOH$ *(Represents over/under-absorption due to volume changes).*

## 4.14 Material Mix and Yield Variance
### Handling Multiple Inputs
When a product requires a mix of materials (e.g., chemicals, recipes), variances occur due to differing input proportions.
*   $Actual\ Cost = Actual\ Mix\ (AM) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (AQ) \times Actual\ Price\ (AP)$
*   $Standard\ Cost\ at\ Actual\ Mix = Actual\ Mix\ (AM) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (AQ) \times Standard\ Price\ (SP)$
*   $Standard\ Cost\ at\ Standard\ Mix = Standard\ Mix\ (SM) \times Actual\ Quantity\ (AQ) \times Standard\ Price\ (SP)$
*   $Target\ Standard\ Cost = Standard\ Mix\ (SM) \times Standard\ Quantity\ (SQ) \times Standard\ Price\ (SP)$
*   **Material Mix Variance:** Difference between actual mix and standard mix at actual total quantity.
*   **Material Yield Variance:** Difference between actual total quantity used and standard total quantity allowed.

## 4.15 Sales and Sales Mix Variance
### Revenue Side Variance
Evaluated using **Contribution Margin (CM)** instead of profit to isolate volume/mix effects from fixed costs.
*   **Sales Mix Variance:** Occurs when the proportion of high-margin vs. low-margin products sold differs from the budget. 
    *   *Example:* Selling more of a low-margin product creates an adverse mix variance, even if total unit volume increases.
*   **Sales Volume Variance:** The difference in contribution resulting purely from selling a different total quantity than budgeted.

## 4.16 Investigating Variance
### Control and Corrective Actions
*   Managers must investigate significant variances to find root causes.
*   **Interdepartmental Dynamics:** Favorable price variance in Purchasing might cause adverse usage variance in Production (e.g., buying cheaper, low-quality materials leads to higher scrap rates).
*   **Budget Committees:** Convene to discuss MIS reports, resolve interdepartmental conflicts, and authorize corrective actions.
*   Variances directly inform performance incentives and trigger standard resets if consistently highly favorable (indicating loose standards).

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Budgeting Process:** Links strategic long-term vision to daily operations via the Master Budget.
*   **Key Factor:** The bottleneck (usually sales) around which all other budgets (production, purchase, cash) are built.
*   **Budget Slack:** Hidden managerial buffers; eliminated by strict ZBB or ABB implementation.
*   **General Variance Formula:** $Total\ Variance = Price/Rate\ Variance + Usage/Efficiency\ Variance$.
*   **Price Variance:** $(Actual\ Price - Standard\ Price) \times Actual\ Quantity$.
*   **Usage Variance:** $(Actual\ Quantity - Standard\ Quantity) \times Standard\ Price$.
*   **Overhead Volume Variance:** Applies only to Fixed Overheads (Budgeted FOH - Applied FOH).
*   **Mix vs. Yield/Volume:** Mix measures the *proportion* of inputs/sales. Yield/Volume measures the *total quantity* of inputs/sales against standards.
