# Management Accounting — Formula Sheet with Worked Examples

## MODULE 1: Cost Concepts & Cost Sheet

---

### Prime Cost

$$\text{Prime Cost} = \text{Direct Material} + \text{Direct Labour} + \text{Direct Expenses}$$

**Q26:** Material Consumed Rs. 10000; Labour Rs. 6000; Direct Expenses Rs. 4000; Fuel Rs. 2000; Other factory Expenses Rs. 1000; Advertisement Rs. 5000; Distribution Rs. 2000; Admin Rs. 1000. Find Prime Cost.

> Prime Cost = 10000 + 6000 + 4000 = **Rs. 20000** ✓
> *(Fuel, factory expenses, advertisement, distribution, admin are all overheads — excluded)*

---

### Conversion Cost

$$\text{Conversion Cost} = \text{Direct Labour} + \text{Manufacturing (Factory) Overheads}$$

**Q1:** Material Rs. 10000; Labour Rs. 6000; Fuel Rs. 2000; Other factory Expenses Rs. 1000; Advertisement Rs. 5000; Distribution Rs. 2000; Admin Rs. 1000. Find Conversion Cost.

> Direct Labour = 6000
> Factory Overheads = Fuel (2000) + Other factory (1000) = 3000
> Conversion Cost = 6000 + 3000 = **Rs. 9000** ✓
> *(Material is excluded; Advertisement/Distribution/Admin are period costs)*

---

### Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM)

$$\text{RM Consumed} = \text{Opening RM} + \text{Purchases} - \text{Closing RM}$$

$$\text{Prime Cost} = \text{RM Consumed} + \text{Factory Labour}$$

$$\text{Gross Factory Cost} = \text{Prime Cost} + \text{Factory Overheads}$$

$$\text{COGM} = \text{Gross Factory Cost} + \text{Opening WIP} - \text{Closing WIP}$$

**Q28:** Opening RM Rs. 1000; Purchases Rs. 20000; Closing RM Rs. 1000; Labour Rs. 5000; Factory Exp Rs. 6000; Opening WIP Rs. 3000; Closing WIP Rs. 2000; S&D Rs. 5000; Admin Rs. 2000.

> RM Consumed = 1000 + 20000 − 1000 = 20000
> Prime Cost = 20000 + 5000 = 25000
> Gross Factory Cost = 25000 + 6000 = 31000
> COGM = 31000 + 3000 − 2000 = **Rs. 32000** ✓
> *(S&D and Admin excluded)*

---

### Cost Per Unit (Job Costing / Quotation)

$$\text{Cost per Unit} = \frac{\text{Total Estimated Cost}}{\text{Number of Units}}$$

**Q30:** Cloth Rs. 140000; Materials Rs. 10000; Labour Rs. 60000; Factory OH Rs. 20000; Admin OH Rs. 10000; Transport Rs. 10000. Units = 1000.

> Total Cost = 140000 + 10000 + 60000 + 20000 + 10000 + 10000 = 250000
> Cost/unit = 250000 / 1000 = **Rs. 250** ✓

## MODULE 2: Process Costing & Activity Based Costing

---

### Completed Units

$$\text{Completed Units} = \text{Opening WIP} + \text{Units Introduced} - \text{Closing WIP}$$

**Q6:** Opening WIP: 100 units; New Units: 3000; Closing WIP: 600 units.

> Completed = 100 + 3000 − 600 = **2500 units** ✓

---

### Cost per Equivalent Unit (Weighted Average — No Loss)

$$\text{EU} = (\text{Completed Units} \times 100\%) + (\text{Closing WIP} \times \%\text{ Completion})$$

$$\text{Cost per EU} = \frac{\text{Opening WIP Cost} + \text{Cost Incurred}}{\text{Total EU}}$$

**Q5:** Opening WIP: 100 units (60% complete); New: 3000; Closing WIP: 600 (40% complete). Conversion cost in Opening WIP: Rs. 3000; Incurred: Rs. 13440.

> Completed = 100 + 3000 − 600 = 2500
> EU = 2500 + (600 × 0.40) = 2500 + 240 = 2740
> Total Cost = 3000 + 13440 = 16440
> Cost/EU = 16440 / 2740 = **Rs. 6.00** ✓

---

### Cost per Equivalent Unit (With Normal Loss)

$$\text{EU} = (\text{Completed Units} \times 100\%) + (\text{Closing WIP} \times \%) + 0 \text{ (Normal Loss)}$$

**Q7:** Same as above but Completed = 2400 units; Normal Loss = 100 units.

> EU = 2400 + (600 × 0.40) + 0 = 2400 + 240 = 2640
> Total Cost = 3000 + 13440 = 16440
> Cost/EU = 16440 / 2640 = **Rs. 6.23** ✓
> *(Normal loss absorbs zero EU — its cost spreads over good units)*

---

### ABC — Cost Allocation

$$\text{Activity Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Cost Pool}}{\text{Total Driver Volume}}$$

$$\text{Allocated Cost} = \text{Activity Rate} \times \text{Driver Units for Product}$$

**Q9:** Design dept cost Rs. 5,00,000. Designs: X = 300, Y = 150, Z = 50. Allocate to X.

> Total Designs = 300 + 150 + 50 = 500
> Rate = 500000 / 500 = Rs. 1000/design
> Cost to X = 300 × 1000 = **Rs. 3,00,000** ✓

## MODULE 3: CVP Analysis & Decision Making

---

### Break-Even Units

$$\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}}$$

**Q37:** SP = Rs. 100; Material Rs. 30, Labour Rs. 5, Other mfg Rs. 35; FC = Rs. 30,00,000.

> VC/unit = 30 + 5 + 35 = 70
> Contribution/unit = 100 − 70 = 30
> BEP = 30,00,000 / 30 = **1,00,000 units** ✓

---

### Units for Target Profit

$$\text{Required Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Target Profit}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}}$$

**Q12:** SP Rs. 70; VC Rs. 30; FC Rs. 20,00,000; Target Profit Rs. 50,00,000.

> Contribution/unit = 70 − 30 = 40
> Units = (20,00,000 + 50,00,000) / 40 = 70,00,000 / 40 = **1,75,000 units** ✓

---

### Margin of Safety

$$\text{MOS} = \text{Current Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales}$$

**Q13:** BEP Sales Rs. 10 lakhs; Current Sales Rs. 120 lakhs.

> MOS = 120 − 10 = **Rs. 110 lakhs** ✓

---

### Profit from Margin of Safety

$$\text{Profit} = \text{MOS} \times \text{PV Ratio}$$

**Q41:** MOS = Rs. 100 lakhs; Contribution Margin = 30%.

> Profit = 100 × 30% = **Rs. 30 lakhs** ✓

---

### BEP from Two-Period Data

$$\text{PV Ratio} = \frac{\Delta \text{Profit}}{\Delta \text{Sales}}$$

$$\text{FC} = (\text{Sales} \times \text{PV Ratio}) - \text{Profit}$$

$$\text{BEP} = \frac{\text{FC}}{\text{PV Ratio}}$$

**Q40:** Year 1: Sales 100L, Profit 30L. Year 2: Sales 160L, Profit 60L.

> PV Ratio = (60 − 30)/(160 − 100) = 30/60 = 50%
> FC = (100 × 0.50) − 30 = 50 − 30 = 20 lakhs
> BEP = 20 / 0.50 = **Rs. 40 lakhs** ✓

---

### Pricing for Target Profit

$$\text{New SP} = \text{VC/unit} + \frac{\text{FC} + \text{Target Profit}}{\text{Estimated Qty}}$$

**Q14:** Current SP Rs. 10; VC Rs. 4; FC Rs. 20000; Target Profit Rs. 20000; Qty = 5000.

> Required Contribution = (20000 + 20000) / 5000 = Rs. 8
> New SP = 4 + 8 = **Rs. 12** ✓

---

### Price Reduction Decision

$$\text{Compare: Current Profit vs. Proposed Profit}$$

**Q42:** Current: SP 100, VC 50, Qty 10000, FC 2L. Proposed: SP 80, Qty 20000.

> Current Profit = (100−50)×10000 − 200000 = 500000 − 200000 = 3,00,000
> Proposed Profit = (80−50)×20000 − 200000 = 600000 − 200000 = 4,00,000
> **Yes, accept** — Profit increases from 3L to **4L** ✓

---

### Make or Buy Decision

$$\text{Max Purchase Price/Unit} = \frac{\text{Variable Cost} + \text{Avoidable Fixed Cost}}{\text{Units}}$$

**Q15:** VC Rs. 70000; FC Rs. 40000 (only Rs. 10000 avoidable); Units = 20000.

> Avoidable Cost = 70000 + 10000 = 80000
> Max price = 80000 / 20000 = **Rs. 4 per unit** ✓

---

### Limiting Factor Decision

$$\text{Contribution per unit of scarce resource} = \frac{\text{Contribution/unit}}{\text{Resource/unit}}$$

**Q44:** Product A: Contribution Rs. 10, requires 3 hrs. Product B: Contribution Rs. 15, requires 5 hrs.

> A = 10/3 = Rs. 3.33/hr
> B = 15/5 = Rs. 3.00/hr
> **Product A gets priority** (higher contribution per machine hour) ✓

---

### Degree of Operating Leverage

$$\%\Delta \text{Profit} = \text{DOL} \times \%\Delta \text{Sales}$$

**Q17:** DOL: Product A = 1.20, Product B = 2.00. Sales increase = 20%.

> A profit increase = 1.20 × 20% = 24%
> B profit increase = 2.00 × 20% = 40%
> **Product B's profit increases more** ✓

## MODULE 4: Budgeting & Standard Costing / Variance Analysis

---

### Production Budget

$$\text{Production} = \text{Budgeted Sales} + \text{Desired Closing Stock} - \text{Opening Stock}$$

**Q18:** Jan Sales = 30000; Feb Sales = 28000; Policy: 10% of next month as closing; Opening = 500.

> Closing Stock = 10% × 28000 = 2800
> Production = 30000 + 2800 − 500 = **32300 units** ✓

---

### Raw Material Purchase Budget

$$\text{RM Purchases} = (\text{Production} \times \text{Material/unit}) + \text{Closing RM Stock} - \text{Opening RM Stock}$$

**Q19:** Production = 32300 units; 2 KG/unit; Desired Closing RM = 2000 KG; Opening RM = 0.

> RM needed = 32300 × 2 = 64600 KG
> Purchases = 64600 + 2000 − 0 = **66600 KGs** ✓

---

### Cash Collection (Credit Period)

**15 days credit → 50% same month, 50% next month**

$$\text{Collection} = \text{Opening Receivables} + (\text{Current Month Sales} \times 50\%)$$

**Q20:** Jan Sales Rs. 5,00,000; Opening Receivables Rs. 2,00,000; Credit = 15 days.

> Collection = 2,00,000 + (5,00,000 × 50%) = 2,00,000 + 2,50,000 = **Rs. 4,50,000** ✓

---

### Cash Paid to Suppliers

$$\text{Cash Paid} = \text{Opening Payables} + \text{Credit Purchases} - \text{Closing Payables}$$

**Q47:** This is the correct formula for determining actual cash outflow to suppliers. ✓

---

### Overhead Recovery

$$\text{Recovery Rate} = \frac{\text{Budgeted OH}}{\text{Budgeted Hours}}$$

$$\text{Applied OH} = \text{Actual Hours} \times \text{Rate}$$

$$\text{Under/Over} = \text{Applied} - \text{Actual}$$

**Q29:** Budgeted OH = 100000; Budgeted Hrs = 1000; Actual Hrs = 1200; Actual OH = 110000.

> Rate = 100000/1000 = Rs. 100/hr
> Applied = 1200 × 100 = 120000
> Difference = 120000 − 110000 = +10000
> **Over-recovery of Rs. 10000** ✓

**Q3:** Same formula but Actual Hrs = 800; Actual OH = 120000.

> Applied = 800 × 100 = 80000
> Difference = 80000 − 120000 = −40000
> **Under-recovery of Rs. 40000** ✓

---

### Material Cost Variance (Total)

$$\text{MCV} = (\text{Actual Output} \times \text{Std Qty/unit} \times \text{Std Price}) - (\text{Actual Qty} \times \text{Actual Price})$$

**Q22:** Budgeted: 1000 units, 5 KG/unit, Rs. 10/KG. Actual: 800 units, 4500 KG, Rs. 12/KG.

> Std Cost = 800 × 5 × 10 = 40000
> Actual Cost = 4500 × 12 = 54000
> MCV = 40000 − 54000 = **Rs. 14000 Adverse** ✓

---

### Material Price (Rate) Variance

$$\text{MPV} = (\text{Std Price} - \text{Actual Price}) \times \text{Actual Qty}$$

**Q23:** SP = 10, AP = 12, AQ = 4500.

> MPV = (10 − 12) × 4500 = −2 × 4500 = **Rs. 9000 Adverse** ✓

---

### Material Usage (Efficiency) Variance

$$\text{MUV} = (\text{Std Qty for Actual Output} - \text{Actual Qty}) \times \text{Std Price}$$

**Q24:** Std Qty = 800 × 5 = 4000; AQ = 4500; SP = 10.

> MUV = (4000 − 4500) × 10 = −500 × 10 = **Rs. 5000 Adverse** ✓

$$\boxed{\text{MCV (14000 A)} = \text{MPV (9000 A)} + \text{MUV (5000 A)}}$$

---

### Sales Variances

$$\text{Total Sales Variance} = \text{Actual Revenue} - \text{Budgeted Revenue}$$

$$\text{Sales Price Variance} = (\text{AP} - \text{SP}) \times \text{AQ}$$

$$\text{Sales Volume Variance} = (\text{AQ} - \text{BQ}) \times \text{SP}$$

**Q25:** Budgeted: 10L units × Rs. 100. Actual: 12L units × Rs. 95.

> Total = (12L × 95) − (10L × 100) = 1140L − 1000L = **Rs. 140L Favourable**
> Price Variance = (95 − 100) × 12L = **Rs. 60L Adverse**
> Volume Variance = (12L − 10L) × 100 = **Rs. 200L Favourable** ✓

$$\boxed{140\text{L (F)} = -60\text{L (A)} + 200\text{L (F)}}$$

## Quick Reference: What to Exclude

| Calculating | Exclude |
|-------------|---------|
| Prime Cost | All overheads |
| Conversion Cost | Direct Material, Selling/Admin |
| COGM | Selling, Distribution & Admin |
| Break-Even / CVP | FC is separate, not per-unit |
| Make or Buy | Unavoidable fixed costs |
| Overhead Recovery | Use **budgeted** rate, not actual |

---

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Decision Type | Rule |
|--------------|------|
| Limiting Factor | Maximize **contribution per unit of scarce resource** |
| Make or Buy | Compare supplier price with **avoidable cost per unit** |
| Price Reduction | Accept if **new total profit > current total profit** |
| Operating Leverage | Higher DOL = **more profit sensitivity** to sales changes |
| Variance | Negative = Adverse; Positive = Favourable |
| Over/Under Recovery | Applied > Actual = Over; Applied < Actual = Under |
