# 📘 **MODULE 1: Management Accounting Basics + Costing Foundations**

---

## 🔹 1. What is Management Accounting?

Management Accounting = **“Accounting for internal decision-making”**

👉 Focus:

* Planning (Budgeting)
* Decision Making (Pricing, Make/Buy)
* Controlling (Variance Analysis)
* Product Costing

📌 Uses data from:

* Financial Accounting (external reporting)
* Cost Accounting (internal cost tracking) 

---

## 🔹 2. Cost Concepts (VERY IMPORTANT)

### ✅ Types of Costs

* **Product Cost** → Goes into inventory (Material, Labour, Factory OH)
* **Period Cost** → Expensed (Admin, Selling, Distribution)

### ✅ Key Classifications

* Variable Cost → changes with output
* Fixed Cost → constant
* Relevant Cost → future + incremental
* Sunk Cost → past, ignore in decisions

---

## 🔹 3. Core Cost Formulas (MUST MEMORIZE 💯)

### 🔸 Prime Cost

👉 Concept: Direct costs only
**Formula:**
Prime Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses

---

### 🔸 Conversion Cost

👉 Concept: Cost to convert material → finished goods
**Formula:**
Conversion Cost = Direct Labour + Factory Overhead

---

### 🔸 Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM)

👉 Concept: Total factory production cost
**Steps:**

1. RM Consumed = Opening + Purchase − Closing
2. Prime Cost = RM + Labour
3. Factory Cost = Prime + Factory OH
4. COGM = Factory Cost + Opening WIP − Closing WIP

---

## 🔹 4. Pattern 1: Prime Cost / Conversion Cost

### 📌 Concept Trick

* Direct → Include
* Indirect (Admin, Selling) → Exclude ❌

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q1. Find Conversion Cost:**
Labour = 6000
Fuel = 2000
Factory Expense = 1000

👉 Conversion Cost = Labour + Factory OH
= 6000 + (2000 + 1000)
= **9000 ✅**

---

## 🔹 5. Pattern 2: Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM)

### 📌 Concept Flow (VERY IMPORTANT)

RM → Prime → Factory → COGM

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q2. Find COGM:**

RM Consumed = 2000 + 10000 − 1000 = 11000
Prime Cost = 11000 + 5000 = 16000
Factory Cost = 16000 + 6000 = 22000
COGM = 22000 + 3000 − 2000

👉 **Answer = 23000 ✅**

---

## 🔹 6. Pattern 3: Over/Under Absorption of Overheads

### 📌 Concept

We compare:

* **Applied OH** vs **Actual OH**

---

### 📐 Formula

Standard Rate = Budgeted OH ÷ Budgeted Hours
Applied OH = Actual Hours × Rate

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q3. Find Under/Over recovery:**

Rate = 100000 ÷ 1000 = 100/hr
Applied = 800 × 100 = 80000
Actual = 120000

👉 Under-recovery = 120000 − 80000
= **40000 (Under) ✅**

---

## 🔹 7. Pattern 4: Cost per Unit (Quotation)

### 📌 Concept

👉 Add ALL relevant costs → divide by units

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q4. Cost per chair:**

Total Cost =
50000 + 20000 + 40000 + 10000 + 1000 + 10000
= 131000

Cost per unit = 131000 ÷ 100

👉 **1310 per unit ✅**

---

## 🔹 8. Pattern 5: Process Costing (Completed Units)

### 📌 Formula

Completed Units = Opening WIP + Input − Closing WIP

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q6. Completed Units:**

= 100 + 3000 − 600
= **2500 units ✅**

---

## 🔹 9. Pattern 6: Equivalent Units (EU)

### 📌 Concept

Convert incomplete units → equivalent full units

---

### 📐 Formula

EU = Completed Units + (Closing WIP × % completion)

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q5. Cost per EU:**

EU = 2500 + (600 × 40%) = 2740
Total Cost = 3000 + 13440 = 16440

Cost per EU = 16440 ÷ 2740

👉 **6.00 ✅**

---

### ⚠️ Special Case: Normal Loss (Q7)

👉 Normal loss = ignored in EU

Cost per EU = 16440 ÷ 2640 = **6.23 ✅**

---

## 🔹 10. Pattern 7: Activity Based Costing (ABC)

### 📌 Concept

👉 “Costs are driven by activities”

---

### 🔹 Activity Levels

* Unit Level → per unit
* Batch Level → per batch
* Product Level → per product
* Facility Level → overall

---

### 🧠 Questions

**Q8. Material handling driver →**
👉 **Number of Shipments ✅**

---

**Q10. Cleaning per batch →**
👉 **Batch-level activity ✅**

---

**Q11. Advertisement →**
👉 **Product-level activity ✅**

---

**Q36. Packing →**
👉 **Unit-level activity ✅**

---

## 🔹 11. Pattern 8: ABC Cost Allocation

### 📌 Concept

👉 Use **correct cost driver (NOT units always)**

---

### 🧠 Question

**Q9. Allocate design cost:**

Total designs = 300 + 150 + 50 = 500
Rate = 500000 ÷ 500 = 1000

For Product X = 300 × 1000

👉 **300000 ✅**

---

## 🔹 12. Golden Exam Summary 🚀

👉 Direct costs → Prime cost
👉 Labour + Factory OH → Conversion cost
👉 COGM → Factory cost + WIP adjustment

👉 Normal loss → ignore
👉 Overhead → compare applied vs actual

# 📘 **MODULE 2: Product Costing and Activity Based Costing**

---

## 🔹 1. Product Costing — Big Picture

Product costing is used to find the cost of producing goods or services. The method depends on the nature of production:

* **Job Costing** → customized / unique orders
* **Process Costing** → continuous mass production
* **Batch Costing** → production in batches
* **Operating Costing** → services like transport / railways
* **Customer Costing** → customer becomes the cost object 

---

## 🔹 2. Job Costing

### ✅ Concept

Job costing is used when every order is different. The **job** is the cost object. Direct material, direct labour, and direct expenses are traced to the job, while overheads are allocated using a suitable base. 

### 📐 Formula

**Prime Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses** 

### 🧠 Typical Exam Pattern

Questions usually ask:

* identify the cost object
* compute job cost / quoted price
* trace direct vs indirect costs

---

## 🔹 3. Customer Costing

### ✅ Concept

Here the **customer** becomes the cost object. This is useful when different customers demand different service levels, delivery patterns, or customization. It helps reveal hidden customer-level profitability. 

### 🧠 Key Idea

A simple margin on sales may hide the fact that some customers consume more support activities than others. 

---

## 🔹 4. Process Costing

### ✅ Concept

Used where output is identical and produced continuously, such as cement, sugar, steel, or banking-type processes. Costs are collected by process/department and spread over output. 

### 📐 Formula

**Equivalent Units (EU) = Completed Units + Closing WIP × % Completion** 

### 📐 Cost per Equivalent Unit

**Cost per EU = Total Cost Incurred ÷ Total Equivalent Units** 

### ✅ Important Methods

* **Weighted Average Method** → combines opening WIP cost and current cost
* **FIFO Method** → separates opening WIP from current production
* **LIFO Method** → rarely used 

---

## 🔹 5. Pattern 1: Completed Units in Process Costing

### 📌 Formula

**Completed Units = Opening WIP + Units Introduced − Closing WIP** 

### 🧠 Question

**Q6. Compute the number of completed units for the month...**
Opening WIP = 100
New units introduced = 3000
Closing WIP = 600

Completed Units = 100 + 3000 − 600 = **2500 units ✅**
Same pattern appears again in **Q32**. 

---

## 🔹 6. Pattern 2: Equivalent Unit Costing

### 📌 Formula Flow

1. Find completed units
2. Add closing WIP equivalent units
3. Add opening WIP cost + current cost
4. Divide total cost by total EU 

### 🧠 Question

**Q5. Compute cost per Equivalent Unit for conversion cost...**
Total conversion cost = 3000 + 13440 = 16440
EU = 2500 + (600 × 40%) = 2740
Cost per EU = 16440 ÷ 2740 = **Rs. 6.00 ✅**

### ⚠️ Same Pattern with Normal Loss

**Q7.** Normal loss is absorbed by good units, so it does not create extra equivalent units.
Cost per EU = 16440 ÷ 2640 = **Rs. 6.23 ✅**
This same structure repeats in **Q31**. 

---

## 🔹 7. Treatment of Losses in Process Costing

### ✅ Concept

* **Normal Loss** → expected and unavoidable; absorbed into good units
* **Abnormal Loss** → unexpected; charged to P&L separately 

### 📌 Exam Tip

If the question says **normal loss**, do not treat it like extra good output. It is absorbed by the process cost. 

---

## 🔹 8. Joint Products, By-products and Scrap

### ✅ Concept

* **Joint products** → multiple major products from a common process
* **By-products** → low-value incidental output
* **Scrap** → minimal or zero-value residue 

### 📐 Joint Cost Apportionment Methods

* Physical quantity method
* Sales value at split-off
* Estimated realizable value
* Estimated gross margin 

---

## 🔹 9. Activity Based Costing (ABC)

### ✅ Concept

ABC says: **products consume activities, and activities consume resources**. Traditional costing often distorts cost because it uses volume-based drivers even when overheads are caused by complexity. 

### 🔹 ABC Hierarchy

* **Unit-level** → happens for each unit
* **Batch-level** → happens for each batch
* **Product-level** → supports an entire product line
* **Facility-level** → sustains the whole organization 

---

## 🔹 10. Pattern 3: Cost Driver Identification

### 📌 Concept

Choose the driver that actually causes the activity cost.

### 🧠 Questions

**Q8. Cost driver for material handling department**
👉 **Number of Shipments ✅**

**Q33. Cost driver for inspection department**
👉 **Number of parts in a product ✅**
Because inspection effort rises with complexity. 

---

## 🔹 11. Pattern 4: Activity Level Identification

### 📌 Concept

Classify the activity correctly before solving the question.

### 🧠 Questions

**Q10. Cleaning and calibration before starting a new tablet batch**
👉 **Batch-level activity ✅**

**Q11. Advertisement for a multi-product company**
👉 **Product-level activity ✅**

**Q35. Setting up an automobile assembly line before a new car batch**
👉 **Batch-level activity ✅**

**Q36. Packing**
👉 **Unit-level activity ✅** 

---

## 🔹 12. Pattern 5: Design Cost Allocation in ABC

### 📌 Concept

When design cost is given, allocate it using **number of designs** if that is the cost driver. Ignore production quantity and machine hours unless the question specifically says so. 

### 🧠 Question

**Q9. Allocate design department cost to Product X**
Total designs = 300 + 150 + 50 = 500
Rate per design = 500000 ÷ 500 = 1000
Allocation to X = 300 × 1000 = **Rs. 300000 ✅**

### 🧠 Same Pattern

**Q34. Allocate design department cost to Product X**
Total designs = 16 + 10 + 4 = 30
Allocation to X = (16 ÷ 30) × 300000 = **Rs. 160000 ✅** 

---

## 🔹 13. Job Costing vs Process Costing vs ABC — Fast Revision

### ✅ Job Costing

Best for special/custom orders.
Cost object = job. 

### ✅ Process Costing

Best for identical mass production.
Cost object = process/department. 

### ✅ ABC

Best when overheads are high and volume-based costing distorts cost.
Cost object = activity-driven product/service. 

---

## 🔹 14. Module 2 One-Page Formula Sheet

**Prime Cost** = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses
**Completed Units** = Opening WIP + Units Introduced − Closing WIP
**Equivalent Units** = Completed Units + Closing WIP × % Completion
**Cost per EU** = Total Cost ÷ Total EU
**Normal Loss** = absorbed by good units
**Abnormal Loss** = charged to P&L
**ABC Driver Rule** = choose the factor that actually causes the cost 

---

## 🔹 15. What to remember for the exam

Module 2 is mostly about:

* identifying the correct costing system
* using the right formula
* selecting the correct cost driver
* handling WIP, equivalent units, and process loss correctly
* classifying ABC activities properly

# 📘 **MODULE 3: Cost Analysis for Decision-Making**

---

## 🔹 1. What this module is about

This module is about using accounting information for **managerial decisions** such as pricing, make-or-buy, product mix, discontinuing products, and understanding how profit changes when sales change. The main tools are **marginal costing, CVP analysis, relevant costing, limiting factor analysis, and leverage analysis**. 

---

## 🔹 2. Absorption Costing vs Marginal Costing

### ✅ Concept

* **Absorption costing** treats both fixed and variable costs as product costs.
* **Marginal costing** treats only variable costs as product costs; fixed costs are charged in full to the period.
  This is why marginal costing is better for short-term decisions. 

### 📐 Core formula

**Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost**
**Profit = Contribution − Fixed Cost** 

### 🧠 Exam pattern

Whenever the question is about pricing, BEP, target profit, or make/buy, use **variable cost + contribution thinking**, not full absorption cost. 

---

## 🔹 3. Pattern 1: Break-even and target profit questions

### ✅ Concept

Break-even analysis tells you how many units or how much sales value is needed to cover fixed costs. Once contribution covers fixed cost, profit starts. 

### 📐 Key formulas

**BEP (Units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit**
**BEP (Sales) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio**
**Profit = Margin of Safety × Contribution per unit** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q37. A tubelight manufacturer sells each unit for Rs. 100. Material = Rs. 30, Labour = Rs. 5, Other manufacturing expenses = Rs. 35, Fixed cost = Rs. 30,00,000. How many units must it sell to break even?**
Variable cost per unit = 30 + 5 + 35 = 70
Contribution per unit = 100 − 70 = 30
BEP units = 30,00,000 ÷ 30 = **100,000 units ✅** 

### 🧠 Same pattern

**Q38. Same data, target profit Rs. 30 lakhs**
Target units = (Fixed Cost + Target Profit) ÷ Contribution per unit
= (30,00,000 + 30,00,000) ÷ 30
= **200,000 units ✅** 

---

## 🔹 4. Pattern 2: Margin of safety

### ✅ Concept

Margin of safety shows how far actual sales are above break-even sales. It is a safety cushion against losses. 

### 📐 Formula

**Margin of Safety = Actual Sales − Break-even Sales** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q39. Find the margin of safety from the following information: Break-even Sales = Rs. 10 lakhs; Current Sales = Rs. 120 lakhs.**
MOS = 120 − 10 = **Rs. 110 lakhs ✅** 

---

## 🔹 5. Pattern 3: Target selling price / target profit

### ✅ Concept

If sales volume is fixed and management wants a target profit, first calculate the contribution required, then work backward to the selling price. 

### 📐 Formula

**Required Contribution = Fixed Cost + Target Profit**
**Contribution per unit = Required Contribution ÷ Units**
**Selling Price = Variable Cost per unit + Contribution per unit** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q14. Sun Corporation wants to double profit from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 20,000. Variable cost per unit = Rs. 4, Fixed Cost = Rs. 20,000, Estimated quantity = 5000 units. Find the price.**
Required contribution = 20,000 + 20,000 = 40,000
Contribution per unit = 40,000 ÷ 5000 = 8
Selling price = 4 + 8 = **Rs. 12 ✅** 

---

## 🔹 6. Pattern 4: Margin of safety to profit relationship

### ✅ Concept

A very useful shortcut in CVP is that profit can be found from margin of safety and contribution margin ratio. 

### 📐 Formula

**Profit = Margin of Safety × Contribution Margin Ratio** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q41. If MOS is 100 lakhs and contribution margin is 30%, profit is?**
Profit = 100 × 30% = **Rs. 30 lakhs ✅** 

---

## 🔹 7. Pattern 5: Break-even from two years’ data

### ✅ Concept

If you are given sales and profit for two periods, you can derive the PV ratio from change in profit and change in sales. Then find fixed cost and break-even sales. 

### 📐 Formula

**PV Ratio = Change in Profit ÷ Change in Sales**
**Fixed Cost = Contribution − Profit**
**BEP Sales = Fixed Cost ÷ PV Ratio** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q40. Year 1 Sales = Rs. 100 lakhs, Profit = Rs. 30 lakhs; Year 2 Sales = Rs. 160 lakhs, Profit = Rs. 60 lakhs. Find BEP.**
PV Ratio = (60 − 30) ÷ (160 − 100) = 30 ÷ 60 = 50%
Contribution in Year 1 = 100 × 50% = 50 lakhs
Fixed cost = 50 − 30 = 20 lakhs
BEP = 20 ÷ 0.50 = **Rs. 40 lakhs ✅** 

---

## 🔹 8. Relevant costing: special order pricing

### ✅ Concept

For special orders, only **relevant costs** matter. Fixed costs already covered by regular business are ignored unless they change because of the order. The minimum price should cover variable cost and any avoidable fixed cost. 

### 📐 Formula

**Minimum acceptable special order price = Variable Cost + Avoidable Fixed Cost + Opportunity Cost** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q15. Entity makes 20,000 units. Variable cost = Rs. 70,000. Total fixed cost = Rs. 40,000, but only Rs. 10,000 is avoidable if bought outside. Maximum offer to outside supplier?**
Relevant internal cost = 70,000 + 10,000 = 80,000
Per unit = 80,000 ÷ 20,000 = **Rs. 4 ✅** 

### 🧠 Same pattern

**Q43** is the same make-or-buy logic again, and the answer is also **Rs. 4 per unit**. 

---

## 🔹 9. Make or Buy / Outsourcing

### ✅ Concept

Compare the supplier price with the **avoidable internal cost** of making the item yourself. Ignore allocated fixed costs that will stay anyway. If outsourcing frees a resource with value, include that as opportunity cost. 

### 📐 Formula

**Relevant Make Cost = Variable Cost + Avoidable Fixed Cost + Opportunity Cost** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q15 / Q43. Same make-or-buy structure**
Avoidable internal cost = Variable cost + saved fixed cost
So maximum supplier price = **Rs. 4 per unit ✅** 

---

## 🔹 10. Limiting factor / scarce resource decisions

### ✅ Concept

When machine hours, labour hours, or another resource are limited, rank products by the contribution they earn per unit of the scarce resource. Choose the highest contribution per limiting factor first. 

### 📐 Formula

**Contribution per limiting factor = Contribution per unit ÷ Resource per unit** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q16. Mars Corporation has surplus machine hours and unlimited demand. On what basis should it allocate hours?**
Use **Contribution per machine hour ✅** 

### 🧠 Another same pattern

**Q44. Product A contributes 10 and needs 3 hours; Product B contributes 15 and needs 5 hours. Which has priority?**
A = 10/3 = 3.33 per hour
B = 15/5 = 3 per hour
So **Product A ✅** 

---

## 🔹 11. Operating leverage

### ✅ Concept

Operating leverage measures how strongly profit reacts to a change in sales. Higher operating leverage means higher business risk, but also faster profit growth when sales rise. 

### 📐 Formula

**DOL = Contribution Margin ÷ PBIT** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q17. DOL of Product A = 1.20 and Product B = 2.00. If sales increase by 20%, which profit increases more?**
Product A profit increase = 1.20 × 20% = 24%
Product B profit increase = 2.00 × 20% = 40%
So **Product B ✅** 

### 🧠 Same pattern

**Q45** is the same logic again, and the answer is still **Product B**. 

---

## 🔹 12. Price reduction decision

### ✅ Concept

Do not judge a price cut only by unit price. Compare total profit before and after the change using the new price and expected volume. 

### 📐 Formula

**Profit = (Selling Price − Variable Cost) × Units − Fixed Cost** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q42. Price drops from Rs. 100 to Rs. 80 and quantity doubles from 10,000 to 20,000. Variable cost = Rs. 50, Fixed Cost = Rs. 2 lakhs. Should price be cut?**
Current profit = (100 − 50) × 10,000 − 2,00,000 = 3 lakhs
New profit = (80 − 50) × 20,000 − 2,00,000 = 4 lakhs
So **yes, profit rises to Rs. 4 lakhs ✅** 

---

## 🔹 13. Quick formula sheet for Module 3

**Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost**
**Profit = Contribution − Fixed Cost**
**BEP (Units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit**
**BEP (Sales) = Fixed Cost ÷ CMR**
**MOS = Actual Sales − BEP Sales**
**Profit = MOS × CMR**
**DOL = Contribution ÷ PBIT**
**Limiting factor ranking = Contribution per scarce resource unit**
**Make or Buy = Compare vendor price with avoidable internal cost** 

---

## 🔹 14. What to revise hardest from this module

The high-scoring areas are:

* BEP and target profit
* Margin of safety
* Special order / make-or-buy
* Limiting factor decisions
* Operating leverage
* Price-change impact on profit

# 📘 **MODULE 4: Budgeting and Variance Analysis**

---

## 🔹 1. What this module is about

This module covers two big areas:

1. **Budgeting** — preparing plans for sales, production, purchases, cash, and other functions.
2. **Variance analysis** — comparing actual results with standards/budgets to find the reasons for differences. 

---

## 🔹 2. Budgeting — the core idea

### ✅ Concept

Budgeting is a planning tool. It helps management convert strategy into numbers and coordinate sales, production, purchases, labour, overheads, and cash. The **sales budget** is usually the starting point, and other budgets are built around it. 

### 📐 Basic budget relationships

**Production Budget = Sales + Desired Closing Stock − Opening Stock**
**Purchase Budget = Material Required for Production + Desired Closing Material − Opening Material** 

---

## 🔹 3. Pattern 1: Production budget

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q18. Budgeted Sales for January = 30000 units, February = 28000 units, opening stock = 500 units, and desired closing stock = 10% of February sales. Find production for January.**

Desired closing stock = 10% × 28000 = 2800 units
Production = 30000 + 2800 − 500
= **32300 units ✅** 

### ✅ Formula to remember

**Production = Budgeted Sales + Desired Closing Stock − Opening Stock** 

---

## 🔹 4. Pattern 2: Raw material purchase budget

### ✅ Concept

First calculate how many finished units must be produced, then convert that into material requirement, then add closing material stock and subtract opening material stock. 

### 📐 Formula

**Purchases = Material for Production + Desired Closing RM − Opening RM** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q19. Each unit needs 2 kg of raw material. Find January raw material purchases.**

From Q18, production = 32300 units
Material for production = 32300 × 2 = 64600 kg
Closing RM desired = 2000 kg
Opening RM assumed = 0

Purchases = 64600 + 2000
= **66600 kg ✅** 

---

## 🔹 5. Pattern 3: Cash collection from credit sales

### ✅ Concept

If the question gives credit period, convert sales into collections by timing. For 15 days credit, roughly half the month’s sales are collected in the current month and half in the next month. 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q20. January sales = Rs. 5,00,000, opening receivables = Rs. 2,00,000, credit period = 15 days. Find cash collection in January.**

Opening receivables collected = 2,00,000
Collection from January sales = 50% × 5,00,000 = 2,50,000

Total cash collection = 2,00,000 + 2,50,000
= **Rs. 4,50,000 ✅** 

---

## 🔹 6. Pattern 4: Cash budget concept

### ✅ Concept

The cash budget collects all cash inflows and outflows to show whether the business will have a surplus or deficit. It is one of the most important financial budgets. 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q21. Which statement is correct about cash budget?**
Correct answer: **Cash budget integrates all operating budgets and finds out surplus and deficit cash position of the period. ✅** 

### ✅ Quick point

Cash budget is prepared from:

* cash collections
* cash payments to suppliers
* wages/salary payments
* overhead payments
* capital expenditure
* loan/interest payments 

---

## 🔹 7. Variance analysis — the core idea

### ✅ Concept

Variance analysis compares:

* **Standard / Budgeted cost**
* **Actual cost**

The difference is called a variance. It may be:

* **Favourable** if actual performance is better than standard
* **Adverse** if actual performance is worse than standard 

### 📐 General structure

**Total Variance = Price/Rate Variance + Usage/Efficiency Variance** 

---

## 🔹 8. Pattern 5: Material cost variance

### ✅ Concept

Material variance is one of the most common exam patterns. First find standard cost for actual output, then compare with actual cost. 

### 📐 Formula

**Total Material Variance = Standard Material Cost − Actual Material Cost** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q22. Find the material cost variance.**
Budgeted production = 1000 units
Standard quantity per unit = 5 kg
Standard price = Rs. 10/kg
Actual production = 800 units
Actual quantity purchased = 4500 kg
Actual price = Rs. 12/kg

Standard quantity for actual output = 800 × 5 = 4000 kg
Standard cost for actual output = 4000 × 10 = 40000
Actual cost = 4500 × 12 = 54000

Material cost variance = 40000 − 54000
= **Rs. 14000 Adverse ✅** 

---

## 🔹 9. Pattern 6: Material price / rate variance

### 📐 Formula

**Material Price Variance = (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Actual Quantity** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q23. Find material price variance.**

(10 − 12) × 4500
= −9000

So the variance is **Rs. 9000 Adverse ✅** 

---

## 🔹 10. Pattern 7: Material usage / efficiency variance

### 📐 Formula

**Material Usage Variance = (Standard Quantity − Actual Quantity) × Standard Price** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q24. Find material usage variance.**

Standard quantity for actual output = 800 × 5 = 4000 kg
Actual quantity = 4500 kg
Standard price = 10

(4000 − 4500) × 10
= −5000

So the variance is **Rs. 5000 Adverse ✅** 

---

## 🔹 11. Pattern 8: Sales variance

### ✅ Concept

Sales variance compares actual revenue with budgeted revenue. It can be split into:

* **Sales rate / price variance**
* **Sales quantity / volume variance** 

### 📐 Formula

**Total Sales Variance = Actual Sales − Budgeted Sales**
**Rate Variance = (Actual Price − Standard Price) × Actual Quantity**
**Quantity Variance = (Actual Quantity − Budgeted Quantity) × Standard Price** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q25. Budgeted sales = 10,00,000 units at Rs. 100, actual sales = 12,00,000 units at Rs. 95. Find sales variance and components.**

Budgeted revenue = 10,00,000 × 100 = 1000 lakhs
Actual revenue = 12,00,000 × 95 = 1140 lakhs

Total sales variance = 1140 − 1000
= **Rs. 140 lakhs Favourable ✅**

Rate variance = (95 − 100) × 12,00,000
= **Rs. 60 lakhs Adverse ✅**

Quantity variance = (12,00,000 − 10,00,000) × 100
= **Rs. 200 lakhs Favourable ✅** 

### 🧠 Same pattern

**Q50. Budgeted sales 1000 units at Rs. 20, actual sales 1200 units at Rs. 18**
Actual revenue = 1200 × 18 = 21600
Budgeted revenue = 1000 × 20 = 20000
Total sales variance = 1600 Favourable
So answer = **Rs. 1600 Favourable ✅** 

---

## 🔹 12. Pattern 9: Cash paid to suppliers

### ✅ Concept

Cash paid to suppliers is derived from purchases and payables movement. Opening payables are paid during the period, new credit purchases are added, and closing payables are deducted because they are not yet paid. 

### 📐 Formula

**Cash Paid to Suppliers = Opening Payables + Credit Purchases − Closing Payables** 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q47. Which one of the following is used to find the cash paid to suppliers?**
Correct formula: **Opening Payables + Credit Purchases − Closing Payables ✅** 

---

## 🔹 13. Pattern 10: Total material cost variance again

### ✅ Concept

Sometimes the paper asks total material cost variance again with different numbers. Use the same logic every time: standard cost for actual output minus actual cost. 

### 🧠 Representative question

**Q48. Find total material cost variance.**

Actual output = 600 units
Standard quantity per unit = 5 kg
Standard price = 10
Actual quantity = 4500 kg
Actual price = 12

Standard cost for actual output = 600 × 5 × 10 = 30000
Actual cost = 4500 × 12 = 54000

Variance = 30000 − 54000
= **Rs. 24000 Adverse ✅**

## 🔹 15. Quick revision sheet for Module 4

### ✅ Budgeting

**Production = Sales + Desired Closing Stock − Opening Stock**
**Purchase = Material Required + Desired Closing RM − Opening RM**
**Cash Budget = all cash inflows − all cash outflows**

**Total Variance = Standard Cost − Actual Cost**
**Price Variance = (SP − AP) × AQ**
**Usage Variance = (SQ − AQ) × SP** 

### ✅ Sales Variance

**Total Sales Variance = Actual Sales − Budgeted Sales**
**Rate Variance = (AP − SP) × AQ**
**Quantity Variance = (AQ − SQ) × SP**
