## MODULE 1: Introduction to Family Businesses

---

### Defining a Family Business

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Shareholding + **control** over strategy, values, culture (more than a % number) |
| **Management** | How the firm is run: family members (owner or not) or outside professionals |
| **Intent to succession** | Intent to last across generations; **predecessor intends AND successor willing** |

- Related founders alone **≠** family business; **listed/unlisted is immaterial**.
- Zerodha, EaseMyTrip = **NOT** (yet) family businesses (no second generation / intent not evident).
- Byju's: intent can exist even if survival fails.

> **CLA:** Which three characteristics together determine whether a business qualifies as a family business?
> - **Ownership, management, and intent to succession** ✓

> **CLA:** A technology startup founded by two siblings three years ago has not clarified whether they intend to pass the business to the next generation. How should it be classified?
> - **Not a family business until intent to succession is evident** ✓

### Ownership-Management Matrix

| | Family managed | Professionally managed |
|---|---|---|
| **High family ownership** | Reliance, Wipro | TCS (Tata Trusts ~70%), Mahindra, Marico |
| **Low family ownership** | Apollo Hospitals (Reddy family < 51%, runs every arm) | Max Financial, Suzlon (control via board nominees) |

- All four quadrants are possible; **control and influence > shareholding %**.
- ~20% stake is a typical benchmark, linked to a **board seat**.

> **CLA:** A listed IT company is majority owned by a founding trust holding about 70% equity, while day-to-day operations are run entirely by non-family professionals. Which quadrant fits?

> - High family ownership + professionally managed = **Marico, Wipro, TCS** ✓
> - Low family ownership + family managed = **Mahindra** ✓
> - Low family ownership + professionally managed = **Max Financial** ✓

### Degrees of Family Control

| Control | Focus | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Who owns | Purpose, values, culture; capital protection; wealth succession; exercised via **equity, involvement, emotional identity** |
| **Strategic** | Long-term direction and scope | Shaped by market + family goals + family strengths (Reliance: refining → retail, telecom, tech) |

### Weaknesses of Family Business

| Weakness | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Harmony over efficiency** | Family first; "dining room in the boardroom" |

| **Favouritism/nepotism** | Lowers morale |
| **Emotional decisions** | Short-term, conflict-prone |

### Agency and Stewardship Theory

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Agency theory** | Principal-agent: **goal incongruence, information asymmetry, agency cost** |
| **Family agent** | Lower agency cost (trust, shared values) |
| **Outside professional** | Agency cost can rise |
| **Primary agency cost** | Principal vs agent |
| **Secondary agency cost** | **Principal vs principal** (e.g. equal-stake brothers) |
| **Behavioural agency** | Agency + prospect theory (risk-seeking in loss domain) |
| **Stewardship** | Manager = steward: long-term, committed, aligned with family and firm; may decline over generations |

> **CLA:** Two brothers with equal ownership stakes disagree over strategic direction. Which type of agency cost does this primarily represent?
> - **Secondary agency cost between two principals** ✓

> **Q:** Which best reflects Stewardship Theory in a family business?
> - **A manager acts in the long-term interests of the business and aligns with the family's goals** ✓

- **Open system** interacts with environment; **closed** only internally.

> **Q:** In Systems Theory, which best describes a family business?

> **CLA:** Why is the family subsystem typically described as more closed while the business subsystem is more open?

### Alignment-Entrenchment Trade-off

| Effect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Alignment** | Family wealth tied to firm; low monitoring cost; long horizon |
| **Entrenchment** (Villalonga and Amit 2006) | Private benefits of control: related-party transactions, under-qualified relatives, resisting dilution |

- Alignment dominates on average; relationship **nonlinear**: best at **moderate ownership**.

## MODULE 2: Dynamics of Family Firm

---

### Family System vs Business System

| Aspect | Family system | Business system |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Nature** | Self-contained; own rules, values, culture, history; **emotional** ("socialist") | Rules, policies, procedures; **logical** ("capitalist") |
| **Key currency** | Trust, loyalty, support | Competence, merit |
| **Expectations** | Stable lifestyle income, emotional needs, **identity** | Customers, shareholder value, revenue, profit, growth |
| **Talent / skills** | Not important ("accept me as I am") | Important (talent, qualifications, performance mgmt) |
| **Pay / titles** | **Equal** across family members (harmony) | Vary with role and performance |
| **Hierarchy** | **Generation, then age** | Authority structure and capability |
| **Employment** | Unconditional default, two-sided | Based on talent |
| **Rules** | Unwritten, informal (informality a plus) | Formal |

- **Family business = family system + business system + interconnection**; boundaries are **blurred** (unlike non-family firms)
- **Family harmony** is sought so the business **lasts 2-3 generations**; disharmony splits the business
- **Informal apprenticeship:** next generation learns by sitting in the shop/father's cabin
- Common criterion for a family business: **significant family ownership + involvement in management**

> **CLA:** What is the most commonly used criterion for classifying a business as a "family business"?
> - **Significant family ownership and involvement in management** ✓

---

### Family First vs Business First vs Family Enterprise Spirit

| Decision area | Business First | Family First | Balanced / Family Enterprise Spirit |
|---------------|----------------|--------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Entry** | Skills and qualification for the role | Being a **relative** | Family members by business need + some skills |
| **Compensation** | JD and market | **Equal** within generation; varies by generation/age | Family-agreed standard by level; skills count |
| **Stock** | By contribution/performance; non-family too | **Equal by branch** | Equal value for all (stock + other opportunities) |
| **Titles / authority** | Merit; one boss | Same titles; **patriarch** has final say | Competence-based roles |
| **Governance** | Board, chairman, independent directors | Patriarch; at best family consensus | Multiple forums; **family council** |
| **Community role** | Leadership | Voluntary | Active where interests align |

- **Family first:** income/dividends **sacrosanct**; appraisal meaningless; promotion by age; patriarch **never questioned**; risk = harms business
- **Business first:** merit, results, industry-standard norms; risk = **family conflict** (favouritism, lower lifestyle in bad year)
- **No-gos are clearer than do's** (never create disharmony); **top line solves many problems**

### Ownership Dimension

- **Hidden inverted pyramid:** CEO reports to board; board answerable to **owners**
- Ownership = **power + rights + responsibility**; family ownership: **65%+, up to 90%** of businesses in some countries

| Ownership type | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Legal** | Shareholding |
| **Financial** | Capital allocation decisions |
| **Emotional** | Family name, identity, reputation at stake |

| Distinction | Types |
|-------------|-------|
| **Role** | **Custodian** (longevity/stability) vs **Entrepreneur** (drives growth) |
| **Involvement** | **Passive investor** ("sleeping partner") vs **Active investor** |

| Share class | Feature |
|-------------|---------|
| Class A / B | Differ in voting vs dividend rights |
| One vote per branch | Separates voting from cash flow rights |
| **Promoter / founder shares** | Recognise founder's contribution |
| **Special voting shares** | Decision-based higher voting |

- **Owner power dilutes** each generation (voting %), so **cohesion and bonding matter more**
- Owners serve: **family** (first), employees, other stakeholders (suppliers, customers), non-owning family
- Patriarch's role can be **lonely**; trusts early loyal employees

**Mehta Foods caselet:** third-generation family enterprise built on traditional recipes and family name. A multinational offers a substantial premium to acquire it. The patriarch refuses (selling erases the identity his grandfather created). His daughter (finance) wants the sale for wealth security and reduced risk (core market shrinking). A nephew (shareholder, uninvolved in operations) supports the sale for liquidity. No formal charter exists; disagreements are resolved informally at the dinner table; concerns arise as the family and shareholding base expand.

> **CLA:** (Mehta Foods) What concept best explains the patriarch's reluctance to sell despite a lucrative offer?
> - **Socioemotional wealth** (identity and legacy valued over financial offer; emotional ownership) ✓

### Three Powers of Owners

| Power | Meaning | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Destroy** | Owners set rules, so can break them; no approval needed; conflict deepens in later generations | **Ambani** (died intestate, mother intervened), **Ranbaxy** (power misused), **Murugappa** (exclusion in succession); refusal to let go; wrong successor |

- **Three-generation rule:** 1st builds, 2nd stabilises, 3rd takes lifestyle for granted; research shows it is **not universal**
- The three powers span **all three circles**

---

### Right to Design (Ownership Structure)

| Structure | Control / criteria | Who owns | Key challenge |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|---------------|
| **Sole owner** | Unified + exclusive | One per generation | Choosing the one owner; what others get |
| **Partnership** | Shared + exclusive | Only active family members | Entry/exit rules; recapitalisation each generation |
| **Concentrated** | Unified + inclusive | Any descendant, **subset holds control** | Who controls each generation; engaging non-voting owners |
| **Distributed** | Shared + inclusive | **Any descendant eligible** | Dilution; consensus nearly impossible |

- Usually starts as **sole owner**; **no fixed path**; changes with generations, investors, market forces
- Examples: **Godrej** split into two groups; **TVS** cross-holding to branch-level; **Wipro** 65% to philanthropy

---

### Right to Decide

- Covers **who decides** and **how** decisions are made; how much to delegate to family / non-family
- **Marico:** professionally run, owner keeps board oversight; **Tata Sons:** decentralised, nominee on each board; **Dr. Reddy's:** family council rules; **Dabur:** family not in day-to-day; **GMR:** family constitution; **Mahindra:** professional run, owner strategic oversight

| Governance tool | Content |
|-----------------|---------|
| **Family constitution** | Written document of shared values, policies, rules: who decides, who joins, qualifications, dispute resolution |
| **Family council** | Forum that codifies and discusses family/business decisions |

> **CLA:** What is a "family constitution" in family business governance?
> - **A written document outlining shared values, policies and rules guiding the family's relationship with the business** ✓

### Right to Define Value (Value Triangle)

| Choose (side) | Give up | Why | Example |
|---------------|---------|-----|---------|

## MODULE 3: Succession in Family Firms

---

### Succession: Definition and Actors

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Incumbent** | Person at the top currently running the family business; expected to hand over |
| **Successor / Ascender** | Family member **or** professional who takes over management and operations |
| **Succession** | A **gradual process (often decades)**, not an event; control, decision authority, management and often ownership move across |
| **Management transfer** | Who runs day-to-day operations |
| **Ownership transfer** | Who holds shares, voting power, ultimate say; usually the **second and last** transfer |
| **Complete succession** | **Both** ownership control **and** management control transferred |
| **Partial transfer** | Only one of the two transferred |

- Succession is **taboo** because it is read as "too old / frail / cannot manage".
- Must happen in all **three circles**; successor becomes **de facto head of family**.

> **Q:** What do we call the person who currently leads the family business and is expected to transfer control?
> - **Incumbent** ✓
>
> **Q:** Which of the following best describes a complete succession in a family business?
> - **Transfer of both ownership control and management control** ✓

### Ownership vs Management Transfer (Cases)

| Firm | Management | Ownership |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Wipro** | Rishad Premji runs operations | Azim Premji holds far more voting power; bulk of shares to foundation |
| **Walmart** | Professionals | Walton family majority |
| **Tata** | Professionals | Tata Sons (owned by Tata Trusts) |
| **Mars** | Professionals + family CEOs of divisions | 100% family; family board and council |
| **BMW** | Professionals | Family > 40% with voting rights |
| **Marico** | Professional CEO (Saugata Gupta, 2014) | Mariwala family ~60% |
| **Dabur** | Professionals, no Burman at CXO | Burmans > 60% |

- **Real power moves with ownership**; management control is given slowly.

### Three-Generation Rule

| Point | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Rule** | Family businesses do not survive beyond three generations ("shirtsleeves to shirtsleeves") |
| **Statistics quoted** | ~30% reach gen 2, ~13% gen 3, ~2-3% beyond |
| **Causes** | Ownership fragmentation ($100\% \to 50\% \to 25\%$); third generation joins an established business with its own aspirations |
| **Why cautious** | "Survival" defined as family running/controlling; **ignores changes in ownership, management or structure** |
| **Behavioural effect** | May push third generation to sell/exit unnecessarily |
| **Counter-examples** | Hermes (6th gen, resisted LVMH), Cargill (private, 5-6 gen), Ford (~40% ownership, Class B voting shares), Tata, Godrej |

> **Q:** Which of the following best explains why the three-generation rule should be interpreted with caution?
> - **Definitions of survival may ignore changes in ownership, management, or business structure** ✓

### Types of Succession

| Type | Who succeeds | Features | Examples |
|------|--------------|----------|----------|
| **Primogeniture** | **Eldest** child (traditionally male) | Groomed early; siblings follow; conserves capital; Japan: son-in-law if daughter | Shapoorji Pallonji, News Corp (Lachlan), Ford |
| **Coparcenary** | **Multiple** members share ownership | HUF in India (karta = father); equal rights; management still needs role clarity; fragments wealth | Rothschild, Hinduja, RP Goenka, Henkel (150+ owners, unified voting pool) |
| **Ultimogeniture** | **Youngest** child (broadly: beyond first child) | Elders superseded; favourite child or elders unwilling/unable | JCB (younger son) |

- Giving each child a separate company (Bajaj, Adani) is **NOT** pure primogeniture/ultimogeniture.
- 2005 Succession Act brought women in; Murugappa case (Valli Arunachalam) is a landmark.
- **Any** model needs broader **family governance** to keep influence and wealth across generations; model can change between generations.

> **Q:** What type of succession passes the business to the eldest child?
> - **Primogeniture** ✓

> **Q:** What type of succession involves ownership being shared among multiple family members?
> - **Coparcenary** ✓

### Succession as a Process

### Impact and Pathways in a Transfer

| Pathway | Description | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Sell to outsider** | Outside buyer takes over | Last choice; used when next gen uninterested, competition unmanageable, attractive offer, partner conflict; seller needs new identity |

| **Transfer intact** | Undivided, joint ownership and management | Less autonomy; conflict a matter of time; **formal governance essential** |

### Fairness and Justice

### Related-Party Transactions

| | Direction | Hurts |
|---|-----------|-------|

### Professionalisation: Definition, Driver and What It Changes

| Item | Key point |
|------|-----------|
| **Definition** | Deliberate shift from informal, person-driven decisions to formal, **rule-based, criteria-based** systems across markets, resources, operations, management systems and culture |
| **Changes** | **How** decisions are made, **not who** makes them; family member can be a professional |
| **Nature** | A **process/journey**, not an event or checklist |
| **Entrepreneurial mgmt** | One person's judgment; scales by founder working harder; breaks when business outgrows the person |
| **Professional mgmt** | Specialised roles and delegated decisions; scales by adding capacity and systems; breaks when **systems outrun trust** |
| **Why needed** | Decision bandwidth, succession readiness, external credibility, specialised judgment |
| **Urgency drivers** | Competitive complexity, external capital expectations (banks, PE), fair cross-generation transition, merit-based talent (incl. women) |
| **Evidence** | Only about **15%** of board seats independent/non-family; board common, **real outside voice is not** |

> **CLA:** What is the primary driver for family businesses to pursue professionalisation as they grow in size and complexity?
> - **The need for formal systems and specialised expertise beyond family capacity** ✓

> **Q:** What does professionalisation primarily change in a family business?
> - **How decisions are made** ✓

### Benefits of Professionalisation (9 Benefits, 3 Clusters)

| Cluster | Benefits |
|---------|----------|
| **People and talent** | Merit-based selection/pay/promotion (high-commitment systems); path for women and outside talent (no glass ceiling); stronger **non-family commitment** |
| **Governance and discipline** | Independent evaluation and incentives (same appraisal for all); institutional pressure for transparency and disclosure; rules bind family and non-family alike (**one-sided rule = double standard**) |
| **Strategy and capital** | Handles competitive complexity; diverse viewpoints; easier **access to external capital** (lower perceived risk) |

- **Interdependence:** people systems make governance credible; credible governance unlocks external resources
- **Example:** Godrej Consumer Products non-family CEO (Gambhir): market cap about USD 700 million to about USD 9 billion; then Nisaba Godrej succeeded

> **Q:** Which of the following is a key benefit of professionalisation?
> - **Merit-based talent management** ✓

### Resistance to Professionalisation (SEW, FIBER, Agency)

| Item | Key point |
|------|-----------|
| **Why resistance is rational** | Protects **socio-emotional wealth**; olive-mill cooperative: families chose lower economic outcome to avoid surrendering control |
| **FIBER threatened** | **F**amily control, **I**dentity, **B**inding social ties, **E**motional attachment, **R**enewal (dynastic succession) |
| **Type 1 agency** | Owner vs hired manager; fix by monitoring, incentives, independent board |
| **Type 2 agency** | **Altruism**, **entrenchment**, **self-control** (family-specific) |
| **When resistance wins** | Loss of family culture/speed, trust deficit with outsiders, career/status loss of family member |
| **Example** | Tata Sons / Cyrus Mistry (reached Supreme Court) |

### Professional Manager: Five Criteria

| # | Criterion | Professional |
|---|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | Decision basis | **General principles**, not case-specific intuition |
| 2 | Client relation | Client **trusts** expert judgment |
| 3 | Relationships | **Objective**, not personal/emotional |
| 4 | Status | From **achievement**, not family ties |
| 5 | Community | Member of a **voluntary professional community** |

| | Non-professional | Professional |
|---|------------------|--------------|
| **Family** | Birthright; defers to intuition | Earns role; data-based; same standard for all (**Harsh Mariwala, Marico**) |
| **Non-family** | Placed for loyalty; defers to family | Track record/expertise (**Vivek Gambhir**) |

- **Professional ≠ non-family**

> **Q:** Which of the following best defines a professional manager?
> - **A manager who uses expertise, objective criteria, and professional standards** ✓ (**NOT** simply a non-family manager, a relationship-based manager, or a shareholder)

### Professionalism Inside the Firm: Four Rooms and Governance Roles

| Room | Basis | Role |
|------|-------|------|
| **Family room** | Love, unity, development | Family relationships and values; manager authority **cannot** come from here |
| **Owner room** | Power, influence of shareholding | Sets way forward; not management authority |
| **Board room** | **Wisdom and judgment** | Hires/evaluates management; business strategy and oversight |
| **Management room** | **Competence and execution** | Where the professional sits |

- **Failure:** authority granted on family/owner standing; **Fix:** match authority to competence (five criteria)
- Owners' voice belongs in an owners' forum, **not** the board (mixing rooms creates conflict of interest)

> **CLA:** How does a family council differ functionally from a board of directors in a family business?
> - **The family council addresses family relationships and values while the board focuses on business strategy and oversight** ✓

### Three Paths to Professionalisation

| Path | Best when | Risk / safeguard |
|------|-----------|------------------|

### Innovation Paradox and Tensions

| Family tendency | Innovation needs |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Concentrated decisions | Outside expertise |
| Multi-decade horizon | Risk, quick turnaround, failure tolerance |
| SEW, continuity, identity | Willingness to **cannibalise** legacy product |
| Deep single-business knowledge | Openness to outside knowledge |

- **Pressures:** technology pace, multi-generation overlap, global competition, talent expectations
- Innovation and succession linked: no renewal gives the next generation less reason to join
- **Concepts:** creative destruction (Schumpeter); incremental vs radical; exploration vs exploitation; disruptive (Christensen: overlooked segments first)

> **Q:** Why is innovation described as a paradox in family businesses?
> - **Family control can both support and restrict innovation** ✓

### Theories of Internationalisation

| Theory | Core idea |
|--------|-----------|
| **Uppsala (process)** | Gradual **learning** raises commitment: sporadic export, independent agent, sales subsidiary, production subsidiary/FDI |
| **OLI (Dunning)** | O only = **export**; O+L = **licensing**; O+L+I = **FDI** |
| **TCE** | Own the operation when protecting knowledge from opportunism is costly |
| **Network model** | Follow existing partner/relationships |
| **Born globals** | International from inception (about first 3 years) |
| **Springboard** | Acquire strategic assets to overcome home constraints |
| **Institutional** | Support (facilitative home) vs **escapism** (weak home institutions) |

- Dimensions: **speed, intensity/degree, scope**; **liability of origin** for emerging to developed

> **Q:** Which theory views internationalisation as a gradual process of learning and increasing commitment?
> - **Uppsala Model** ✓

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Professional ≠ non-family | Defined by how decisions are made and authority exercised |
| How, not who | Professionalisation changes how decisions are made |

| One standard for all | A rule binding only non-family is a **double standard** |
| Resistance is rational | SEW (FIBER) loss outweighs economic gain |
| Ownership stays, operations delegated | Ownership control remains; strategic shared; operational delegated first |
| Control needs accountability | Value gains flatten without it |
| Ability ≠ willingness | Family ability high; willingness inconstant (SEW) |
| Willingness does not inherit | Ability resources transfer; willingness must be built |
| Governance outlives the leader | Innovation committee, budget and stage gates survive succession |
| Patient capital enables, SEW suppresses | Same control can help or hurt innovation |
| Ambidexterity | Exploit existing while exploring new |
| Mixed gamble | Domestic survival threat flips family to accept international risk |
| Commitment ladder | More control and return come with more commitment and risk |
| Family prefers low commitment | Joint venture bridges knowledge gaps with partial control |
| Governance structure matters | Family chair lowers, independents and cross-generational involvement raise internationalisation |
