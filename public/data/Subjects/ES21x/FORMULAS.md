## MODULE 1: Forces of Demand & Supply

---

### Opportunity Cost

$$\text{Opportunity Cost} = \text{Explicit Cost} + \text{Implicit Cost (value of next best alternative forgone)}$$

**Q41:** Rishav can earn Rs 100/hr. Bus costs Rs 30, takes 4 hrs total. Metro costs Rs 50, takes 2 hrs. Opportunity cost of bus?

> OC of bus = Direct cost (30) + Extra time vs. metro (2 hrs × 100) = 30 + 200 = **Rs 230** ✓
> *(Compare against the next best alternative — metro takes 2 hrs, bus takes 4 hrs, so 2 extra hours lost)*

---

### Sunk Cost

> **Rule:** Already spent, cannot be recovered → **IGNORE in decisions.** Only future costs/benefits matter.

**Q62:** Spent Rs 1.5 Cr on foundation. Recession cuts revenue to Rs 5 Cr. Remaining cost = Rs 4.5 Cr. Complete?

> Ignore sunk 1.5 Cr. Remaining benefit (5.0) > Remaining cost (4.5) → **Yes, complete** ✓

---

### Marginal Principle

$$\text{Optimal: } MB = MC$$

- If $MB > MC$ → increase activity
- If $MB < MC$ → decrease activity

---

### Law of Demand & Supply

| Law | Relationship | Curve Slope |
|-----|-------------|-------------|
| **Demand** | Price ↑ → Qty Demanded ↓ | Downward |
| **Supply** | Price ↑ → Qty Supplied ↑ | Upward |

---

### Shifts vs. Movements

| | Movement Along Curve | Shift of Curve |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause** | Change in own price | Change in other factors |

**Key Shift Factors:**

| Factor | Demand Effect | Supply Effect |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **Expect future price ↑** | Current D ↑ (Shift Right) | Current S ↓ (Shift Left) |
| Price of substitute ↑ | D for other good ↑ | — |
| Price of complement ↑ | D for paired good ↓ | — |
| Input costs ↑ | — | S ↓ (Shift Left) |
| Technology improves | — | S ↑ (Shift Right) |

**Q19:** Growers expect wheat price to rise next month →

> Sellers hoard → **Current supply decreases (shifts left)** ✓

**Q45–48 (Flats):** Expect prices to rise → Demand ↑ + Supply ↓ → **Price rises, shortage** ✓

---

### Demand/Supply Function Interpretation

$$Q_{sx} = 2000 + 3P_x - 4P_y - P_i$$

- Coefficient of $P_y$ negative → X and Y are **complements in production**
- In demand function: coefficient of $P_y$ positive → **Substitutes**; negative → **Complements**
- Coefficient of Income: negative → **Inferior good**; positive → **Normal good**

**Q20:** Price of substitute ↑ AND price of complement ↑ → Effects are **opposite** → Net result **depends on magnitudes** ✓

## MODULE 2: Equilibrium, Surplus & Elasticity

---

### Market Equilibrium

$$\text{Set } Q_D = Q_S \text{ and solve for } P^* \text{ and } Q^*$$

**Q31:** $Q_D = 5000 - 250P$; $Q_S = 600 + 150P$

> $5000 - 250P = 600 + 150P → 4400 = 400P → P^* = 11$ (thousands) = **Rs 11,000** ✓

---

### Disequilibrium

| Condition | Result | Pressure |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| $P > P^*$ | Excess Supply (Surplus) | Price falls |
| $P < P^*$ | Excess Demand (Shortage) | Price rises |

**Q49:** Price below equilibrium → $Q_D > Q_S$ → **Shortage** ✓

---

### Consumer Surplus & Producer Surplus

$$CS = \frac{1}{2} \times Q \times (P_{max} - P_{market})$$

$$PS = \frac{1}{2} \times Q \times (P_{market} - P_{min})$$

$$\text{Total Surplus} = CS + PS$$

**Q30:** $P = 50 - 0.5Q$; Market price = 30.

> $Q = 40$; $P_{max} = 50$
> $CS = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \times (50 - 30) = $ **Rs 400** ✓

**Q10:** $Q_S = -4/3 + (4/3)P$; $P = 4$

> $P_{min} = 1$ (set $Q_S = 0$); $Q = 4$
> $PS = \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times (4-1) = $ **6** ✓

**Q1:** In equilibrium, buyers have **highest WTP**, sellers have **lowest costs** ✓

---

### Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

$$PED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_D}{\%\Delta P} \quad \text{(use absolute value)}$$

**On a linear curve** $P = A - BQ$:

$$|PED| = \frac{1}{B} \times \frac{P}{Q}$$

| Category | \|PED\| | Revenue effect of price ↑ |
|----------|---------|--------------------------|
| Elastic | > 1 | TR ↓ |
| Inelastic | < 1 | TR ↑ |
| Unitary | = 1 | TR unchanged (maximized) |
| Perfectly Inelastic | = 0 | Vertical curve |
| Perfectly Elastic | = ∞ | Horizontal curve |

**Q18:** TR is maximized where $|PED| = 1$ (midpoint of linear demand) ✓

**Q23:** Lower the price on linear curve → **less elastic** (P/Q ratio falls) ✓

**Q53:** %ΔQ < %ΔP → $|PED| < 1$ → **Inelastic** ✓

**Q58:** Price ↑ 15%, Revenue ↑ → **Inelastic demand** ✓

**Q51:** Vertical demand curve → **Perfectly inelastic** ✓

---

### Elasticity & Revenue Rule

**Q2:** Supply shifts right (price falls). If demand is **inelastic** → TR **decreases** ✓

> *(Price effect dominates quantity effect when demand is inelastic)*

---

### Cross-Price & Income Elasticity

$$E_{XY} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_X}{\%\Delta P_Y}$$

| Sign | Relationship |
|------|-------------|
| Positive | **Substitutes** |
| Negative | **Complements** |

$$E_I = \frac{\%\Delta Q_D}{\%\Delta \text{Income}}$$

| Sign | Good Type |
|------|-----------|
| Positive | Normal |
| Negative | Inferior |

**Q38/Q50:** Positive cross-price elasticity → **Substitutes** ✓

---

### Tax Incidence

$$\text{Buyer's share} = \frac{E_S}{E_S + |E_D|}$$

$$\text{Seller's share} = \frac{|E_D|}{E_S + |E_D|}$$

> **Rule:** The MORE INELASTIC side bears MORE tax burden.

**Q3:** Cigarettes — demand inelastic (0.5) → **Consumers bear larger burden** ✓

**Q40:** Water — highly inelastic demand → **Consumers bear most burden** ✓

**Q12:** Supply more inelastic → **Sellers bear larger burden** ✓

**Q57:** To maximize tax revenue → tax goods with **perfectly inelastic demand** ✓

---

### Tax Equilibrium Calculation

$$P_{buyer} - P_{seller} = \text{Tax}$$

**New supply (tax on producers):** Replace $P$ with $(P_b - T)$ in supply equation.

**Q33–35:** $Q_D = 5000 - 250P$; $Q_S = 600 + 150P$; Tax = 2 (thousands)

> New equilibrium: $5000 - 250P_b = 600 + 150(P_b - 2)$
> $P_b = 11.75$ = **Rs 11,750** ✓
> $P_s = 11.75 - 2 = 9.75$ = **Rs 9,750** ✓
> Buyer's fraction = $750/2000$ = **3/8** ✓

---

### Deadweight Loss from Tax

$$DWL = \frac{1}{2} \times T \times \Delta Q$$

> More elastic → larger DWL; larger quantity reduction

**Q11:** Both supply AND demand elastic → **highest quantity reduction** ✓

---

### Price Controls

| Control | Binding If | Creates |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Ceiling** (max price) | $P_C < P^*$ | Shortage (excess demand) |
| **Floor** (min price) | $P_F > P^*$ | Surplus (excess supply) |

**Q24:** "Effective price ceiling creates excess supply" → **FALSE** (it creates shortage) ✓

**Q55:** Minimum wage Rs 6 > equilibrium Rs 4 → employment = quantity demanded at Rs 6 = **24 million** ✓

---

### Market Demand (Horizontal Summation)

$$Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2 \quad \text{(only include segments where } Q > 0\text{)}$$

**Q4:** $Q_m = 10 - P$; $Q_a = 20 - 4P$; at P = 7:

> $Q_m = 3$ (thousands); $Q_a = 20 - 28 = -8$ → set to 0
> Total = **3000** ✓

## MODULE 3: Firm Behavior — Perfect Competition

---

### Cost Definitions

| Cost | Formula |
|------|---------|
| $TC$ | $FC + VC$ |
| $ATC$ | $TC/Q = AFC + AVC$ |
| $AFC$ | $FC/Q$ (always declining) |
| $AVC$ | $VC/Q$ |
| $MC$ | $\Delta TC / \Delta Q$ |

**Q66:** $TC = 200 + 55q$ → **FC = 200**, VC = 55q, **AVC = 55** ✓

**Q13:** ATC at 100 = $0.25; MC = $0.10 (constant). FC = TC - VC = 25 - 10 = $15.

> TC(50) = 15 + 0.10(50) = **$20** ✓

---

### Key Cost Curve Relationships

- MC crosses ATC at **ATC's minimum** (efficient scale)
- MC crosses AVC at **AVC's minimum**
- When $MC < ATC$ → ATC is **falling**
- When $MC > ATC$ → ATC is **rising**
- ATC minimum occurs at **higher Q** than AVC minimum

**Q14:** MC ($0.10) < ATC at both Q=50 and Q=100 → ATC **falling at both** ✓

**Q15:** ATC min ≠ AVC min; ATC min ≠ MC min → **Both statements false** ✓

**Q68:** License fee (fixed cost) ↑ → shifts **AFC and ATC** upward (not MC or AVC) ✓

---

### Fixed vs. Variable Cost Classification

**Q21:** Contract workers (guaranteed pay) = **Fixed**; Dismissible workers = **Variable** → **Partly fixed, partly variable** ✓

---

### Profit Maximization (Perfect Competition)

$$\text{Profit Max: } P = MR = MC$$

$$\text{Profit} = (P - ATC) \times Q = TR - TC$$

**Q27:** $P = 120 - 2Q$; $MC = 4Q$. Set $P = MC$:

> $120 - 2Q = 4Q → Q = 20, P = 80$ ✓
> Profit = $80(20) - [100 + 2(400)] = 1600 - 900 =$ **$700** ✓

---

### Shutdown & Exit Rules

| Decision | Condition | Timeframe |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Keep producing** | $P ≥ AVC$ | Short run |
| **Shut down** | $P < AVC_{min}$ | Short run |
| **Exit market** | $P < ATC_{min}$ | Long run |
| **Enter market** | $P > ATC_{min}$ | Long run |

**Short-run supply curve** = MC curve above AVC

**Q22/Q37:** Firm covers VC but not TC → **Produce short run, exit long run** ✓

**Q64–65:** ATC = $8, AVC = $6, P = $7. Since $P > AVC$ but $P < ATC$:

> **Keep producing short run, exit long run** ✓

---

### Long-Run Equilibrium

$$P = \text{min } ATC \quad (\text{Zero economic profit})$$

**Practice:** $TC = 50 + 0.5Q^2$; min ATC at $Q = 10$, $P = 10$.

## MODULE 4: Monopoly

---

### Monopoly Key Relationships

$$\text{If } P = A - BQ \text{ (Demand)}$$
$$\text{Then } MR = A - 2BQ$$

$$\text{Profit Max: } MR = MC, \text{ then price from demand curve}$$

> **Critical:** For monopolist, $P > MR = MC$

**Q25:** $P = 120 - 2Q$; $MC = 4Q$; $MR = 120 - 4Q$

> $120 - 4Q = 4Q → Q_M = 15$; $P_M = 120 - 30 = $ **90** ✓

**Q26:** Profit = TR - TC = $90(15) - [100 + 2(225)] = 1350 - 550 =$ **$800** ✓

**Q16:** $P > MR$ and $MR = MC$ → "P = MC" is **NOT true** for monopoly ✓

---

### Monopoly vs. Competition (Same Industry)

| | Perfect Competition | Monopoly |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule** | $P = MC$ | $MR = MC$, $P > MC$ |
| **Q27 vs Q25** | Q = 20, P = 80 | Q = 15, P = 90 |
| **Output** | Higher | Lower |
| **Price** | Lower | Higher |

---

### Deadweight Loss of Monopoly

$$DWL = \frac{1}{2} \times (P_M - P^*) \times (Q^* - Q_M)$$

**Q29:** $DWL = \frac{1}{2} \times (90 - 80) \times (20 - 15) = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 5 =$ **$25** ✓

---

### Effect of Fixed Cost Change on Monopolist

> FC ↑ does NOT change MC → does NOT change optimal Q or P → **Price stays same, Profit decreases**

**Q36/Q70:** Fixed costs increase → Price **unchanged**; Profit **decreases** ✓

---

### Price Discrimination

**Requirements:**
1. Market power (price maker)
2. Ability to segment consumers (identify WTP)
3. No arbitrage (prevent resale)

> **NOT required:** Zero marginal cost (**Q7** ✓)

**Strategy:** Charge each segment their maximum WTP (or as close as possible).

**Q6:** 50 High (WTP=120), 30 Medium (WTP=60), 20 Low (WTP=20). MC = 0. Best uniform price?

> At P=120: TR = 120×50 = 6000
> At P=60: TR = 60×80 = 4800
> At P=20: TR = 20×100 = 2000
> **Uniform price = 120, TR = 6000** ✓

**Q8:** Can identify High but not Medium vs Low. Revenue-maximizing prices?

> High → charge 120 (full WTP)
> Medium+Low group: P=60 gives 60×30=1800 > P=20 gives 20×50=1000
> **Charge 120 to High, 60 to Medium and Low** ✓

---

### Natural Monopoly

$$\text{ATC continuously decreasing} → \text{One firm serves market cheaper than multiple}$$

> Caused by: Huge fixed costs + negligible marginal costs

**Q72:** Natural monopoly = one firm supplies entire market at **lower cost** than multiple firms ✓

## QUICK DECISION RULES SUMMARY

| Concept | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Opportunity Cost | Include ALL forgone alternatives (explicit + implicit) |
| Sunk Cost | **Always ignore** in current decisions |
| Equilibrium | $Q_D = Q_S$; self-correcting via price signals |
| Tax burden | Falls on **more inelastic** side |
| Revenue & Elasticity | Inelastic: P↑→TR↑; Elastic: P↑→TR↓ |
| Firm produces if | $P ≥ AVC$ (short run); $P ≥ ATC$ (long run) |
| Supply curve | MC above AVC |
| Perfect Competition | $P = MC$ |
| Monopoly | $MR = MC$; $P > MC$ |
| Price Discrimination | Need: market power + no arbitrage |
| FC change (monopoly) | Price unchanged, profit changes |

## ELASTICITY CHEAT SHEET

| Type | Formula | Positive → | Negative → |
|------|---------|-----------|------------|
| Own-Price (PED) | %ΔQ / %ΔP | — (always negative, use abs value) | — |
| Cross-Price | %ΔQ_X / %ΔP_Y | Substitutes | Complements |
| Income | %ΔQ / %ΔI | Normal good | Inferior good |

| Demand Type | Curve Shape | PED Value |
|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| Perfectly Inelastic | Vertical | 0 |
| Inelastic | Steep | < 1 |
| Unitary | — | = 1 |
| Elastic | Flat | > 1 |
| Perfectly Elastic | Horizontal | ∞ |
