## MODULE 1: Introduction to Persuasive Communication

---

### Core Concepts

| Concept | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Persuasion** | Combining logic (data) + emotion to influence beliefs or secure approval |
| **Glossophobia** | Clinical term for fear of public speaking |
| **Curse of Knowledge** | Knowledgeable people assume the audience knows what they know |
| **Communication Failures** | Often due to assumptions; sender bears responsibility |

> **Q:** Curse of knowledge = **tendency of knowledgeable people to assume audience knows what they know** ✓
> **Q:** Fear of public speaking = **Glossophobia** ✓
> **Q:** Lacks creative thinking → presentation becomes **predictable and less engaging** ✓
> **Q:** Core purpose of professional communication = **deliver info that is clear, relevant, and actionable** ✓
> **Q:** Data + emotion to convince leadership = best illustration of **persuasion in practice** ✓
> **Q:** Glossophobia = feeling of overwhelming nervousness/fear when speaking publicly ✓

---

### Building Confidence (Steps)

1. **Acceptance** — Accept the fear exists
2. **Preparation** — Thorough research and rehearsal
3. **Practice** — Repetition builds muscle memory
4. **Positive Visualization** — Imagine successful delivery
5. **Incremental Exposure** — Start small, build up

> **Q:** Building confidence starts with **acceptance of fear** ✓

---

### Mehrabian's Rule (7-38-55)

| Component | % of Communication Impact |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Words (Verbal)** | **7%** |
| **Vocal Tone (Paralanguage)** | **38%** |
| **Facial Expression / Body Language** | **55%** |
| **Total Non-verbal** | **93%** |

> **Q:** Non-verbal portion = **93%** ✓
> **Q:** Non-verbal in leadership = **reinforces message and builds trust** ✓
> **Q:** Mehrabian = only 7% impact from words; most from non-verbal ✓

---

### Non-verbal Communication & Delivery

| Element | Best Practice |
|---------|--------------|
| **Eye Contact** | Look into eyes of every audience member; builds personal connection |
| **Facial Expressions** | Coordinate with words to enhance message |
| **Posture & Gestures** | Complement and reinforce verbal message |

> **Q:** Engaging audience = **looking into eyes of every member** ✓
> **Q:** Expressions, posture, gestures = **coordinate with words to enhance message** ✓
> **Q:** Non-verbal cues should **complement verbal communication** ✓

---

### Authenticity

- **Definition:** Being genuine, transparent, and consistent in communication
- A speaker loses trust when words don't match tone/body language
- Must **not** be faked — audiences detect inauthenticity quickly

> **Q:** Authenticity = being **genuine + transparent + consistent** ✓

---

### Effective Listening (3 Steps)

1. **Shutting up** — Silence to receive fully
2. **Absorbing information** — Mental processing
3. **Paraphrasing thoughtfully** — Active confirmation

> **Q:** Three key listening steps = **shutting up, absorbing, paraphrasing thoughtfully** ✓

### OBC Structure & Burger Analogy (15-70-15)

| Part | % Time | Content |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Opening (Top Bun)** | **15%** | Hook, context, objective |
| **Body (Patty)** | **70%** | Arguments, evidence, narrative |
| **Closing (Bottom Bun)** | **15%** | Summary, restated objective, emotional lift, strong close |

> **Q:** Buns and patty time split = **15% intro, 70% body, 15% conclusion** ✓
> **Q:** Strong conclusion = **concise summary + restated objective + emotional lift + strong close** ✓
> **Q:** Opening principle = **keep concise, set context, prepare audience** ✓
> **Q:** Opening = **bun 1**; Body = **patty**; Close = **bun 2** ✓
> **Q:** 15% opening, 70% body, 15% closing ✓

## MODULE 3: AEIOUxE Framework & Aristotle's Rhetoric

---

### Aristotle's Three Pillars of Rhetoric

| Pillar | Appeal | Method |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **Ethos** | Credibility | Speaker's expertise, integrity, track record, appearance, confidence |
| **Pathos** | Emotion | Stories, personal connection, emotional engagement |
| **Logos** | Logic | Facts, data, reasoning, evidence |

> **Q:** Audience trusts speaker based on = **familiarity with speaker's credibility** ✓
> **Q:** Credibility → **significantly increases likelihood of trust and action** ✓
> **Q:** Logos = **provides facts, data, and reasoning** ✓
> **Q:** Establish credibility = **speak confidently, cite credible sources, demonstrate expertise** ✓
> **Q:** Presentation should evoke emotion because = **emotion binds message and maintains engagement** ✓
> **Q:** Trust determined by = **familiarity with speaker's credibility** (repeat tested) ✓
> **Q:** Ethos + Pathos + Logos = Aristotle's three modes of persuasion ✓

---

### Dimensions of Credibility (Ethos)

| Dimension | What It Covers |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Expertise** | Domain knowledge and authority |
| **Integrity** | Honesty and ethical conduct |
| **Character** | Personal values and moral standing |
| **Track Record** | Past performance and achievements |
| **Appearance** | Professional dress and grooming |
| **Confidence** | Poise and self-assurance |
| **Demographics** | Background context (age, role, etc.) |

> **Q:** ALL dimensions shape trust in different ways ✓
> **Q:** Establish credibility early = **relatable story + highlight relevant expertise** ✓

---

### The Four Key Audience Questions

| # | Question | What It Tests | AEIOUxE Element |
|---|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Who are you?** | Credibility (Ethos) | A-factor |
| 2 | **What do you want from me?** | Objective clarity | O-factor |
| 3 | **What's in it for me?** | Personal benefit | U-factor |
| 4 | **Why should I agree with you?** | Logical proof | E-factor |

> **Q:** "Why should I agree with you?" = need for **logical reasoning and evidence** ✓
> **Q:** Feynman's emphasis → "Why should I agree?" pushes for **sound reasoning + credibility** ✓
> **Q:** "What do you want from me?" = **What's in it for me?** (U-factor) ✓
> **Q:** Four questions map to AEIOUxE elements ✓

---

### AEIOUxE Framework

| Letter | Element | Meaning |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **A** | Audience/Authority | Who are you? Establish credibility |
| **E** | Emotion | Engage feelings (Pathos) |
| **I** | Interactivity | Involve audience; ask questions |
| **O** | Objective | Clear goal/intended outcome; restate at start, middle, end |
| **U** | U-factor (You) | Personal connection; audience's self-interest (WIIFM) |
| **xE** | Evidence | Facts, data, reasoning (Logos) |

> **Q:** 'O' in AEIOUxE = **Objective** ✓
> **Q:** 'U-factor' = **establishing personal connection with audience** ✓
> **Q:** Restate objective at start/middle/end so audience **knows what to expect and what action to take** ✓
> **Q:** Make audience feel involved = **ask thought-provoking questions, encourage participation** ✓
> **Q:** A-factor corresponds to "Who are you?" ✓
> **Q:** E-factor (evidence) answers "Why should I agree?" ✓

---

## MODULE 4: Slides & Delivery

---

### Three Types of Slides

| Type | Characteristic | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Document Slides** | Information-heavy, text-dense | Reports, handouts |
| **Bullet-point Slides** | Key phrases in bullet format | Traditional corporate decks |
| **Presentation Slides** | Highly visual, complement speech | Steve Jobs-style launches |

> **Q:** Presentation slides = **complement speaker's speech, help audience grasp message** ✓

---

### t/2 Rule

- For a talk of **t** minutes, you need approximately **t/2** slides
- *Example:* 20-minute talk → ~10 slides

> **Q:** t/2 rule for slide count ✓

---

### Slide Design Dos & Don'ts

| Do ✓ | Don't ✗ |
|------|---------|
| Proper citations for images/content | Too many colors and fonts |
| Spell checks | Overloading slides with information |
| Clean, balanced design | Neglecting references for external content |
| Manage time to avoid clutter | Long paragraphs and dense text |
| One or two strong points per slide | Excessive animations and sounds |
| Storyboarding for flow | Experimental colors/palettes |

> **Q:** Visuals + spoken word = **speech delivers verbal, visual aids support and enrich** ✓
> **Q:** Undermines presentation = **excessive data without insight or context** ✓
> **Q:** Best slides = **minimal text, relevant visuals, highlight key points** ✓
> **Q:** Excessive colors/fonts = **distract and overwhelm audience** ✓
> **Q:** Storyboarding = **helps audience anticipate next slide** ✓
> **Q:** Slide "do" = **citations, spell checks, clean design, manage time** ✓
> **Q:** Slide "don'ts" = **too many colors/fonts, overloading, no references** ✓
> **Q:** Choose **one or two strong points** per slide + credible support ✓
> **Q:** Key visual element = **simple and balanced design that avoids clutter** ✓
> **Q:** "**You are the presentation**"; slides are only supporting tool ✓

---

### Two-Version Deck Strategy

- **Presentation version:** Minimal text, highly visual (for live talk)
- **Leave-behind version:** Detailed, document-style (for post-meeting reference)

> **Q:** Two versions ensure audience gets both impact (live) and detail (reference) ✓

---

### Online Presentations

| Adaptation | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| **Slow down** to ~100 wpm | "No gravity" environment; harder to hold attention |
| **Ask for audience reactions** | Chat window, thumbs up, polls |
| **Simpler slides** | Screen fatigue; competing distractions |

> **Q:** Online = **slow down speaking + ask for audience reactions** ✓

---

### Dress Code & Professionalism

- Choose clothing that **conveys professionalism and respects the context**
- "In Rome, be a Roman" — match the setting
- **NOT:** Always suit & tie, match slide colors, or overdress

> **Q:** Professional dress = **respects context of presentation** ✓

---

### Practice & Improvement

- **10,000-hour rule:** Skills improve through repetitive practice
- Process: Practice → Feedback → Rework → Dry Run
- **Key:** Practice in front of others, receive feedback, rework, then do a dry run

> **Q:** Key to improving = **practice in front of others + feedback + rework + dry run** ✓

---

### Boardroom Presentations

- Balance **accuracy with engagement** to support executive decisions
- Don't dump raw data without narrative or context

> **Q:** Major boardroom challenge = **balancing accuracy with engagement to support decisions** ✓
