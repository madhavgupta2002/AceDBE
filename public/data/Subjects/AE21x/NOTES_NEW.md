***Foundations of Business Communication - II***
# Module 1: Essentials of Business Communication

## 1.0 Welcome to the Module
*   Understand the crucial role of communication in organizational success.
*   Analyze the communication process, including enablers and inhibitors.
*   Learn to deliver messages conveying **Total Meaning**.

## 1.1 Evolution of Communication Technology
Human communication has evolved from prehistoric visual signals to sophisticated digital tools.

| Era | Milestone | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prehistoric** | Cave Paintings (**30,000 BCE**) | Earliest visual communication for ritual/ceremony. |
| **4000–3000 BCE** | Cuneiform & Hieroglyphs | Transition from pictograms to formal writing systems. |
| **Media (2500 BCE)** | Papyrus & Clay Tablets | Transition to portable media; later replaced by parchment. |
| **Printing (1436)** | Gutenberg Press | Significant milestone; allowed mass reproduction of text. |
| **19th Century** | Telegraph & Telephone | Enabled data transmission and replication of human voice. |
| **Digital Age** | Internet, WWW, Mobile | Transformed information access and daily routines. |
| **Modern** | Emojis | The "new language" to fill emotional cues in text. |

## 1.2 Communication in the Digital Global Economy
*   **Flat World Concept:** Coined by **Nandan Nilekani**; the world is a level playing field due to decreased differentiation in commerce.
*   **Source of Competitive Advantage:** In an undifferentiated market, the ability to communicate effectively distinguishes a startup.
*   **Professionalism vs. Jugaad:** While *jugaad* (innovation) is useful, **professionalism** is the key determinant for scaling and gaining investor trust.

## 1.3 Stakeholder Relationship Management
Stakeholders are individuals or groups who affect or are affected by a project's outcome.
*   **Internal Stakeholders:** Employees, Managers, Shareholders.
*   **External Stakeholders:** Customers, Suppliers, Regulators, Media, Public.
*   **Primary vs. Secondary vs. Tertiary:** Categorized by the directness of interest in the organization's success.

### Mendelow's Matrix for Prioritizing Stakeholders
Organizations use this to focus limited resources on critical groups based on **Interest** and **Power (Influence)**.

| Quadrant | Stakeholder Type | Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low Interest / Low Power** | Apathetics | Minimal effort/communication. |
| **High Interest / Low Power** | Defenders | Keep informed and satisfied. |
| **Low Interest / High Power** | Latents | Keep satisfied; engage occasionally. |
| **High Interest / High Power** | Promoters | **Priority**; manage closely and communicate regularly. |

## 1.4 The Communication Process
Communication is a **closed-loop cybernetic system** derived from the Latin *communicare* (to share).

1.  **Ideation:** The sender transforms an idea into a purpose.
2.  **Synthesis & Encoding:** The sender packages the idea into a **Message** (the unit of information) using a tool like language.
3.  **Transmission:** Moving the message via a **Medium** (Channel/Vehicle).
4.  **Decoding:** The receiver captures the message and performs **Sense Making** to interpret meaning.
5.  **Feedback:** The response is fed back to the sender, completing the loop.

### Defining Effectiveness
*   **Effectiveness** = The extent to which the desired outcome, purpose, or objective is achieved.
*   **Agreement ≠ Action:** High-quality speaking (Aeschines) is less effective than speaking that moves people to action (Demosthenes).
*   **Responsibility:** The **Sender** is 100% responsible for the effectiveness of the communication.

### Direction of Communication Flow

| Direction | Flow | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Downward** | Manager → Staff | Policy announcements, task assignments. |
| **Upward** | Employee → Manager | Reports, feedback, suggestions. |
| **Lateral** | Colleague → Colleague | Peer coordination, cross-functional updates. |
| **Grapevine** | Informal / Unofficial | Rumors, social chatter; not part of the formal hierarchy. |

## 1.5 Types of Messages
1.  **Spoken:** Language-based; uses voices and ears as primary input/output. *Disadvantage:* No permanent record.
2.  **Written:** Text-based; more permanent, efficient, and reliable. Ensures **repeated access**.
3.  **Visual:** Uses images, symbols (**semiotics**), and charts to pack information efficiently; helps communicate complex data and make trends understandable.
4.  **Multimedia:** Combines text, audio, and video; provides immersive, interactive experiences.
5.  **Non-verbal:** Signals from sentient beings (body language) or inanimate objects (e.g., traffic lights).

## 1.6 Metacommunication
**Metacommunication** is "communication about communication." It consists of the underlying cues beyond literal words.
*   **Paralanguage (Vocalics):** Secondary information like **Accent, Emphasis, Speed, Volume, Pitch,** and **Silence**.
    *   *Example:* Changing emphasis in "I did not tell Tara you were late" changes the meaning entirely.
    *   *Example:* A sarcastic tone when giving feedback influences how the employee interprets the content, regardless of the words used.
*   **Kinesics (Body Language):** Involuntary movements, gestures, posture, and facial expressions.
*   **Micro-expressions:** Involuntary facial twitches that reveal true emotions before they can be masked.
*   **Proxemics:** The study of personal space in communication.
*   **Haptics:** Touch-based communication.
*   **Chronemics:** The role of time as a communication signal.

## 1.7 Context and Barriers
*   **High-Context Cultures:** (India, Japan, China) Assume shared knowledge; messages may be ambiguous to outsiders.
*   **Low-Context Cultures:** (USA, Germany) Assume little shared knowledge; messages are explicit and detailed.

### Common Barriers to Communication
*   **Physical:** Noise, distance, technical issues, or lack of line-of-sight.
*   **Physiological:** Hearing impairment, illness, or age.
*   **Psychological:** Bias, prejudice, lack of motivation, stress, emotions, preconceived notions, or **Information Overload**.
*   **Cultural:** Differences in beliefs, traits, and social forms (**LESCANT** model). Cultural stereotyping can lead to misinterpretations and hinder collaboration.
*   **Temporal/Place:** **Ambush Timing** (sending messages without considering the recipient's schedule) or inappropriate venues (e.g., noisy cafes for negotiations).

## 1.8 The 7 C's of Business Communication
These interconnected attributes ensure a message is "managerially appropriate":
1.  **Clear:** Simple language and sentence construction; not a "cryptic puzzle."
2.  **Concrete:** Specific, definite, and palpable (e.g., "10% return" instead of "large returns"). *Example:* "We will review the proposal next Monday and send you our response at 1700 hrs that day" is minimally ambiguous.
3.  **Concise:** Frugal with words; eliminate fluff and redundancy.
4.  **Complete:** Provides all necessary background and context to prevent the receiver from filling in gaps with assumptions.
5.  **Correct:** Accuracy in facts, figures, and grammar.
6.  **Coherent:** Logical flow where all parts fit together smoothly.
7.  **Courteous:** Respectful tonality; assertive but not aggressive.

## 1.9 Structure, Logic, and Flow
The three most basic characteristics of a business message are **structure, logic, and flow**.
*   **Structure:** The arrangement of elements. It aids comprehension, retention, and recall.
*   **Inductive Reasoning:** Specific cases → Generalized statement (Hypothesis).
    *   *Example:* "Every quiz so far was easy; therefore, the next one will be easy."
*   **Deductive Reasoning:** General principle → Specific instance (Axiom).
    *   *Example:* "All men are mortal (Major Premise); Socrates is a man (Minor Premise); therefore, Socrates is mortal."

## 1.10 Audience Orientation (AIM Model)
Effective communication requires an "Everyday Audience Flex" where the message is tailored to the recipient's "Hot Buttons" (triggers for action).
*   **A (Audience):** Identify initial (gatekeeper), primary, secondary, and watchdog audiences.
*   **I (Intent):** Define what you want the audience to do.
*   **M (Message):** Synthesize the content based on A and I.
*   **WIIFT:** "What's In It For Them?" — Addressing audience needs ensures a win-win outcome.

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Key Definitions:**
    *   **Metacommunication:** Beyond words; the underlying cues (paralanguage + kinesics).
    *   **Kinesics:** Study of body language and non-verbal communication.
    *   **Proxemics:** Study of personal space in communication.
    *   **Total Meaning:** Tangible Content + Paralingual Cues + Non-verbal Cues.
*   **Important Numbers/Metrics:**
    *   **7 C's:** Clear, Concrete, Concise, Complete, Correct, Coherent, Courteous.
    *   **5-step** communication process: Ideation → Encoding → Transmission → Decoding → Feedback.
*   **Must-remember Points:**
    *   The **Sender** is 100% responsible for effectiveness.
    *   **Feedback** closes the communication loop and ensures the message is received as intended.
    *   **Psychological barriers** (stress, bias, preconceived notions) are the most commonly tested barrier type.
    *   **Mendelow's Matrix:** High Interest / High Power stakeholders = **Promoters** = top priority.

# Module 2: Fundamentals of Business Writing

## 2.0 Welcome to the Module
*   Master structural models: **OBC** and the **Inverted Pyramid**.
*   Differentiate between **Direct** and **Indirect** approaches.
*   Execute the **Four-Step Writing Process** and apply best practices to emails, letters, and digital media.

## 2.1 Novel vs. Business Writing

| Feature | Novel/Fiction (Pyramid) | Business Communication (Inverted Pyramid) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | Nonlinear; meandering; subplots. | Linear; direct; rigid formats. |
| **Flow** | Detail → Information → Conclusion. | **Core Message** → Supporting Details. |
| **Goal** | To entertain and immerse the reader. | To inform, persuade, or instruct. |
| **Outcome** | Denouement/Climax at the end. | Recommendation/Main idea up-front. |

## 2.2 The Inverted Pyramid Format
A journalistic technique where information is presented in **descending order of importance**.
*   **The Lead:** Concise first paragraph conveying the main message.
*   **The Body:** Supporting details, argument, data, and context.
*   **The Conclusion:** Summary or call to action.
*   **Benefits:** Immediate impact, easy skimming for busy managers, and clarity in crisis communication.

## 2.3 The OBC Model
A physical and content structure compared to the three-headed **Trimurti** sculpture:
1.  **Opening (Introduction):** Defines background, context, purpose, and the **Core Message**. It includes necessary statutory info and greetings.
2.  **Body:** Organized into sections or paragraphs. Contains arguments, evidence, and logical flow.
3.  **Closing (Conclusion):** Restates recommendations and provides a **Call to Action** (next steps).

## 2.4 Specialized Structural Frameworks

| Framework | Structure | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **STAR** | Situation → Task → Action → Result | Behavioral interviews, performance reviews. |
| **SBO** | Situation → Behavior → Outcome | Simplified narrative responses. |
| **PACADI** | Problem → Alternatives → Criteria → Analysis → Decision → Implementation | Systematic problem-solving and decision-making. |
| **Minto Pyramid** | Apex (core message) → Arguments → Evidence | Hierarchical recommendation structure (McKinsey). |

*   **Minto Pyramid Principle:** Pioneered by **Barbara Minto** (McKinsey). The recommendation or conclusion is positioned at the start, followed by arguments, each backed by evidentiary points.

## 2.5 The Four-Step Writing Process
1.  **Planning & Pre-writing:** Analyze the situation, define the target audience, and outline headings.
2.  **Drafting:** Continuous writing without self-judgment. "Chip the marble" to let the sculpture emerge. *At the drafting stage, more is better.*
3.  **Revising:** Reviewing for appropriateness, objective alignment, and logical flow.
4.  **Editing & Proofreading:** Painstaking analysis of grammar, punctuation, and mechanics. *Proofreading is mainly concerned with language errors.*

### Time Management (The 40-20-40 Formula)
*   **40% Planning:** Sharpening the knife before cutting the tree.
*   **20% Drafting:** The actual writing of the text.
*   **40% Revising & Editing:** Essentially "rewriting" to ensure a bulletproof document.
*   *Therefore, **80%** of available time is for Planning, Review, and Editing.*

### Direct vs. Indirect Approach

| Approach | When to Use | Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direct** | Good news, routine information, positive requests. | State the main point first. |
| **Indirect** | Bad news, persuasion, sensitive topics. | Start with a **Buffer** to mitigate negative reaction. |

## 2.6 Using Generative AI
AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) is a tool for **augmentation**, not replacement.
*   **Benefits:** Automating routine tasks, drafting iterations, and summarizing data.
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out):** Output quality depends entirely on the **Prompt**. Best results come from clear, specific instructions.
    *   **Hallucinations:** AI can invent fictitious citations, facts, or legal cases.
    *   **Lack of Creativity:** AI relies on existing patterns and lacks critical thinking/originality.
*   **Safety Measures:** Managers must perform 100% human review, fact-checking, and bias awareness.

## 2.7 Business Emails
*   **Addressing:**
    *   **To:** Essential people only (Primary audience).
    *   **CC:** For information; do not use for "CYA" (Cover Your Backside) by over-copying.
    *   **BCC:** Use for privacy when emailing large groups or when a party needs to see a message without others knowing.
*   **Subject Lines:** Must be specific, professional, and descriptive (e.g., "Request for a Meeting to Discuss Q1 Marketing Strategy"). Subject lines have a **limited shelf life**; update them as the conversation topic changes.
*   **Etiquette:** Avoid "Reply All" unless essential. No continuous caps (shouting), no emoticons in formal mail, and no "pithy/prophetic" sayings in signatures.

## 2.8 Professional Letters
*   **Physical Structure:**
    1.  **Prefatory:** Letterhead, dateline, address block.
    2.  **Message:** Salutation, body text.
    3.  **Trailing:** Signature block, title block, postscripts (PS).
*   **Format:** **Full Block Format** (all text justified to the extreme left).
*   **Naming Conventions:** Always use the last name (e.g., Dear Mr. Sharma). Use **Mx.** for gender-neutrality.
*   **Postscripts (PS):** Highly effective attention-getters often read before the body.

## 2.9 Writing for Digital Media
*   **Characteristics:** Nonlinear, interactive, and very low shelf life.
*   **Consumer Mentality:** Readers are "scanners" with low attention spans (first **15 seconds** are critical).
*   **Approaches:**
    *   **Sniper Approach:** Accurate targeting using data/algorithms.
    *   **Spray and Pray:** Broad broadcasting, hoping for a result (less effective).
*   **KISS:** **Keep It Short and Simple**.
*   **Chunking:** Breaking text into small, digestible pieces with white space and visual hierarchies (headings, bullet points).

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Key Definitions:**
    *   **OBC:** Opening, Body, Closing.
    *   **KISS:** Keep It Short and Simple.
    *   **GIGO:** Garbage In, Garbage Out (applies to AI Prompts).
    *   **Full Block Format:** All text aligned to the left margin.
*   **Important Numbers/Metrics:**
    *   **40-20-40** time split: Planning (40%), Drafting (20%), Revising & Editing (40%).
    *   **80%** of writing time goes to non-drafting activities.
*   **Must-remember Points:**
    *   **Direct Approach** = main point first (routine/positive). **Indirect Approach** = buffer first (bad news).
    *   **Inverted Pyramid** = most important info at the top, supporting details follow.
    *   **Minto Pyramid** = core message at apex, hierarchically supported by arguments and evidence.
    *   Written communication ensures **repeated access** (permanent record).

# Module 3: Interpersonal Communication

## 3.0 Welcome to the Module
*   Build strong professional relationships through feedback, active listening, and effective questioning.
*   Understand group dynamics, meeting facilitation, and documentation.

## 3.1 Active Listening
Listening is distinct from the physical act of hearing; it is a **psychological process** of truly understanding the message.
*   **Definition:** The active intent to understand, interpret, and evaluate what is heard.
*   **Responding immediately** without pausing or clarifying does **not** demonstrate better comprehension.
*   **Paraphrasing:** Restating the speaker's message in one's own words to confirm understanding.

## 3.2 Giving and Receiving Feedback
Feedback should be viewed as a "cheat code" for professional growth rather than a "roasting session."
*   **Giving Feedback:** Must be constructive, specific, and focused on improvement rather than personal criticism.
*   **Receiving Feedback:** Requires an open mindset to identify areas for skill elevation without stress or defensiveness.

### The Hamburger Method
A feedback technique that sandwiches constructive criticism between two layers of positive reinforcement:
1.  **Top Bun:** Praise a strength.
2.  **Filling:** Deliver the specific area of improvement.
3.  **Bottom Bun:** Offer encouragement and express confidence.

## 3.3 Effective Questioning

| Question Type | Characteristics | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Open-ended** | Cannot be answered with "yes" or "no." | Encourage elaboration; create a non-threatening, supportive environment. |
| **Closed** | Answered with "yes," "no," or a specific fact. | Verify or clarify what is already known. |
| **Rhetorical** | Not intended to elicit a verbal response. | Stimulate reflection, emotional response, or emphasize a perspective. |

### The Funnel Technique
A questioning method that starts with broad, open-ended questions and **sequentially narrows** into specific, closed-ended questions.
*   *Example:* "Can you tell me about your experience?" → "What specific tools do you use?" → "How did you handle tight deadlines?" → "Did you find it stressful?"

## 3.4 The Johari Window Model
A framework for understanding self-awareness and disclosure in interpersonal communication.

| Quadrant | Known to Self | Known to Others | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arena (Open)** | ✓ | ✓ | Shared knowledge; builds trust. |
| **Facade (Hidden)** | ✓ | ✗ | Information consciously kept private. |
| **Blind Spot** | ✗ | ✓ | Others see it, but you don't. |
| **Unknown** | ✗ | ✗ | Unknown to both parties. |

## 3.5 Communication Styles and Emotional Regulation
*   **Assertive Communication:** Direct, honest, and respectful while taking others' feelings into account. The ideal balance between aggression and passivity.
*   **Fight Response:** When feeling attacked, individuals may become simultaneously aggressive and defensive. *Example:* Sarita retaliating when Jagan raises his voice.
*   **Emotional Regulation:** In high-pressure situations, take a deep breath, count to ten, and then respond calmly, considering all perspectives before reacting.

### Factors Influencing Communication Behaviour
*   **Emotional Intelligence:** Managing and understanding one's own and others' emotions.
*   **Emotional State:** Mood and psychological condition at the time of communication.
*   **Language Proficiency:** Command over the language being used.
*   **Spoken Accent:** *Not* a significant influence on underlying communication behaviour (it is a paralinguistic variance).

## 3.6 Small Group Interaction
### Group Dynamics and Pitfalls
*   **Common Pitfalls:** Lack of clear goals, dominant participants, and fear of differing perspectives.
*   **Improvement Strategies:** Encouraging participation from all members and managing diverse viewpoints to reach actionable outcomes.

### Types of Small Group Interactions

| Type | Purpose | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Discussion Groups** | Exchange of information. | Explores ideas without necessarily needing a final decision. |
| **Brainstorming Sessions** | Creative problem-solving. | Focuses on quantity of ideas and "out-of-the-box" thinking. |
| **Operational Meetings** | Tactical coordination. | Focuses on day-to-day tasks, updates, and immediate actions. |

### Informal Communication
*   **Grapevine:** The term for informal communication networks in organizations. Operates outside official hierarchies, driven by social interaction and rumors.

## 3.7 Formal Meetings and Documentation
*   **Preparation:** Establishing a clear objective and a structured agenda.
*   **Execution:** Facilitating the discussion to ensure engagement and time management. A good facilitator actively guides conversation back on track and ensures equal participation.
*   **Minutes of the Meeting (MoM):** The formal record of what was discussed, decided, and assigned. They provide a roadmap for accountability and ensure outcomes are actionable.

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Key Definitions:**
    *   **Active Listening:** A psychological act of understanding, not just hearing sounds.
    *   **Johari Window:** Arena (open), Facade (hidden), Blind Spot, Unknown.
    *   **Grapevine:** Informal communication network in organizations.
    *   **Assertive Communication:** Direct + honest + respectful.
*   **Important Numbers/Metrics:**
    *   **4 quadrants** of Johari Window.
    *   **3 question types:** Open, Closed, Rhetorical.
*   **Must-remember Points:**
    *   **Hamburger Method** = Praise → Critique → Encouragement.
    *   **Funnel Technique** = Broad questions narrowing to specific.
    *   **Paraphrasing** confirms comprehension in active listening.
    *   **MoM** is essential documentation for accountability in formal settings.

# Module 4: Business Presentations

## 4.0 Welcome to the Module
*   Develop and deliver impactful business presentations with confidence.
*   Master preparation, visual design, delivery techniques, and Q&A interaction.

## 4.1 The Four Stages of Presentations
1.  **Preparation:** The foundation and planning phase.
2.  **Creating Visual Aids:** Syncing visuals with the spoken word.
3.  **Delivering:** The actual performance and engagement.
4.  **Q&A and Interaction:** Handling the "presentation after the presentation."

## 4.2 Preparation (The Planning Phase)
### The Five Key Questions for Strategy

| Question | Purpose/Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **What is the purpose?** | Determines strategy: To inform, persuade, or problem-solve. |
| **Who is the audience?** | Influences tone and content level (age, knowledge, relationship). |
| **Where is the venue?** | Identifies physical needs (acoustics, lighting, power, projection). |
| **How long is it?** | Based on speech rate (**120 words per minute**). Plan for time-crunches. |
| **When is it?** | Determines the time available for preparation. |

### Message Formulation and Content
*   **Central Message:** State the core idea precisely in one sentence.
*   **Main Points:** Limit to **3–5 ideas**.
*   **Attention Span:** Research shows audiences have a **45-minute limit** and can handle a maximum of **7 concepts**.

### Structure and Sequencing Options
*   **Cause → Effect → Solution:** *Example:* High employee turnover (Cause) → Production down 15% (Effect) → Increase salaries and improve conditions (Solution).
*   **Problem → Cause → Possible Solutions → Proposal.**
*   **Chronological:** Time-based implementations.
*   **Logical Groupings / Order of Importance / Evaluatory Criteria.**

## 4.3 Creating the Script and Flow
### The Three Commandments
1.  **Tell them what you're going to tell them** (Opening).
2.  **Tell them** (Body).
3.  **Tell them what you've just told them** (Conclusion).

### Elements of a Strong Opening
*   **Opening Courtesies:** Brief, graceful address to the audience.
*   **Attention Getter:** Questions, statistics, anecdotes, or quotes to "hook" listeners. A thought-provoking question is the most effective technique.
*   **Purpose/Background:** Contextual relevance; why the topic matters now.
*   **Outline:** Defining the scope and agenda.

### Developing the Main Body
*   **Interpret Facts:** Don't let data speak for itself; explain implications.
*   **Examples/Anecdotes:** Transition from impersonal to personal to hold interest.
*   **Transitions / Interim Summaries:** Signal movement between ideas and refresh complex points.
*   **Humor:** Use with caution; self-deprecating humor is safest.

### Effective Conclusions
*   **Summary:** Reiterate the main message. *"Tell them what you've told them."*
*   **Recommendation:** Restate the appeal or required action.
*   **Persuasive Appeal:** Reinforce benefits to the audience.
*   **Exit Line:** A crisp, final concluding statement.

## 4.4 Overcoming Glossophobia
**Glossophobia** is the fear of public speaking or facing an audience.
*   **Characteristics:** Anticipatory anxiety, intense self-doubt, rapid heartbeat, dry mouth, nausea.
*   **What does NOT help:** Focusing meticulously on getting every word or movement perfectly.
*   **What helps:** Rehearsing in front of a supportive audience, deep breathing and mindfulness, visualizing confident delivery.

## 4.5 Delivery and Defense
*   **Vocal Modulation:** Adjusting pitch, volume, and pacing to maintain attention. **Monotone delivery** is the least effective.
*   **Body Language:** Open stance projects credibility and trust. **Crossed arms** project defensiveness, not confidence.
*   **Emotional Intelligence in Q&A:** Acknowledge both positive and negative feedback without becoming defensive; use it to improve future presentations.

### Register in Language
Register changes based on the formality of the setting, the social roles of participants, and the relationship between speaker and audience. **Extreme emotional state** does *not* determine linguistic register.

## 4.6 Presentation Rules and Heuristics

| Rule | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **10-20-30 Rule** (Guy Kawasaki) | **10** slides, **20** minutes, **30**-point minimum font size. Keeps the presentation concise and focused. |
| **Rule of Seven** | Maximum **7** lines per slide and **7** words per line. Promotes minimalism. |
| **Storytelling Trinity** | A presentation needs all three: the **Story** (structure), the **Visuals** (design), and the **Telling** (delivery). Removing any one weakens effectiveness. |

## 4.7 Visual Aids
*   **Main Aim:** To **support the message**, not replace the speaker.
*   **Poor Practices:** Overloading slides with text, reading directly from slides, excessive use of visuals causing audience confusion.
*   **Best Practices:** Clean design, legible fonts (≥**30**pt), relevant images, and consistent formatting.

## Quick Revision Summary
*   **Key Definitions:**
    *   **Glossophobia:** Fear of public speaking.
    *   **Register:** Variation in speech depending on context, purpose, and social dynamics.
    *   **Three Commandments:** Tell them what you'll tell them → Tell them → Tell them what you told them.
*   **Important Numbers/Metrics:**
    *   **120** words per minute (average speech rate).
    *   **45** minutes (audience attention span limit); **3–5** main points max; **7** concepts max.
    *   **10-20-30:** 10 slides / 20 min / 30pt font.
    *   **Rule of Seven:** 7 lines × 7 words per slide.
    *   **30pt** minimum font size (not 16pt).
*   **Must-remember Points:**
    *   **Cause → Effect → Solution** is a key tested sequencing pattern.
    *   **Open stance** = credibility; **Crossed arms** = defensiveness.
    *   Visual aids **support** the message; slides should never **replace** the speaker.
    *   **Emotional intelligence** in Q&A: acknowledge all feedback without becoming defensive.
