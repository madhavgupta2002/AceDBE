## MODULE 1: Founding or Joining?

---

### Framing the Decision (1.2.1)

| Concept | Key Point |
|---------|-----------|
| **Wrong question** | "Entrepreneur or employee?" turns career into an **identity test** |
| **Right question** | **Best next move** given what you know, want and can access **now** |
| **Routes** | Founding and joining are **both** entrepreneurial; differ in responsibility, learning, risk, ownership, control |
| **Careers** | **Sequences, not labels** (join, found, close, join, found again) |
| **Nature of decision** | Under uncertainty; **no formula**; evidence + experts + others' lives inform, **own interpretation** needed |
| **Claim types** | "Research suggests" = systematic study; "X argues" = practitioner opinion; named founders = **illustration, NOT proof** |

---

### Wanting vs Readiness (1.2.1)

- **Wanting the founder title** is **NOT** a reason to found now (identity signals ambition, independence, creativity, courage)
- **Faulty sequence:** "I want to be a founder, so I need an idea"
- **Startup** = temporary organisation seeking a sustainable business model; starting **≠** model in place
- **Venture survives only if** it creates enough value for customers, users, stakeholders

| Paul Graham (Y Combinator founder) | Detail |
|------------------------------------|--------|
| **Status of essay** | One experienced person's view, **NOT** empirical research |
| **Warning** | Knowing startup mechanics but weakly tied to a **real problem** = **"playing house"** |
| **Core advice** | Make **something people want**; fall in love with the **problem**, **NOT** the solution |
| **Failure rate** | About **90%** fail, often building what founders want |
| **Choose** | Problems that genuinely interest you + people you like and respect |
| **Idea source** | Sustained contact with important problems, **NOT** forced "find an idea" brainstorming (Maggi: brainstormed **problems**) |
| **Timing** | Around **20**: broad exploration; **exception:** side project that takes off |

---

### Youth, Reasons and Readiness (1.2.2)

| Advantages of Youth | Detail |
|---------------------|--------|
| Fewer fixed commitments | **Lower opportunity cost** |
| Closeness to new tech and behaviour | Peers see problems early |
| Energy for experimentation | Experience also comes from building and serving users |
| **Risk of delay** | Credible opportunity may be **destroyed** by waiting |

| Valid Reason to Found Now | Example |
|---------------------------|---------|
| Repeated painful problem | Skin condition solved via micronutrients led to a nutraceutical firm |
| Complementary co-founder with trust | Capabilities fit |
| Small solution with users | Early traction |
| Tech or regulatory shift | Temporary opening |
| Room in life to experiment | Low fixed obligations |

- **Readiness ≠ complete knowledge** (no founder is fully ready; Uniphore began without answers)
- **Readiness** = opportunity + people + motivation + capacity strong enough to justify **learning through action**
- **Task:** decide if the uncertainty is worth entering now and if founding is the best place to learn
- **Test:** attracted to the **problem** or to **founderhood**? Would you stay if the title and admiration vanished?

---

### What Are You Optimising For? (1.2.2)

| Priority | Founding | Joining |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Direction** | Greater scope, but **burden on you** | Less control |
| **Ownership** | Real, but **NOT liquid wealth** | Less ownership |
| **Autonomy** | Yet dependent on customers, staff, investors, regulation, cash | Focus, seniors, salary, running system |
| **Learning** | Fast; agenda set by **survival** | Good role **compresses years**; poor role = repetitive execution |
| **Stability / family** | Open-ended risk | Predictable income |

- Four optimisation goals: **autonomy, learning, financial upside, stability**; none is noble or ignoble

### Wealth vs Control (1.2.2, 1.3.1)

| Wasserman, *The Founder's Dilemmas* | Detail |
|-------------------------------------|--------|
| **Chapter 2** | Career decision to found; personal circumstances and motivations |
| **Rich vs King** | **Wealth** vs **control**; recurring tension, not a rule |
| **Control-insistent founder** | Limited resources |
| **Resource-seeking founder** | Bigger kingdom, **surrenders part of the throne** |

| Central Motive | Implication |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Control** | High-growth founding forces trading control for resources |
| **Wealth / scale** | Accept **shared authority** |
| **Learning** | Strong team may give more learning, **less existential pressure** |

- **Fantasy corrected:** founders are NOT free: customers refuse, co-founders disagree, staff leave, investors/board demand changes, growth adds obligations
- **Bikhchandani (1990):** bootstrapped for years; preserving control = patience, constraint, different growth path

| Benefit Sought | Test Question |
|----------------|---------------|
| Autonomy | Ready for **responsibility without boundaries**? |
| Wealth | Ready for **dilution and illiquid equity**? |
| Learning | Compared with a **strong startup role**? |

### Passion and Biases (1.3.1)

| Passion: Helps | Passion: Distorts |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Energy, persistence, attention | Becomes identity; acts as a **filter** on evidence |

| Bias | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Confirmation** | Seek information supporting desired belief |
| **Overconfidence** | Overestimate judgment/control |
| **Planning fallacy** | Underestimate time and effort |
| **Escalation of commitment** | Invest more because already invested |
| **Survivorship** | Hear only winners; shut-down founders unseen |
| **Hindsight** | "Would have joined Flipkart/Amagi"; at the time few did and many peers failed |

**Countermeasures**
- State beliefs about **user, problem, solution** (not slogans)
- Seek **disconfirming evidence**; define stop/change triggers
- Talk to people who **disagree**
- Decide on **evidence**, **NOT** hunch, identity or passion

### What Experience Gives You (1.3.2)

| Study | Finding | Caveat |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **Gupta et al.** (US immigrant first-time founders; green-card waits) | Experienced founders do better on **funding, patents, workforce growth**; via **team formation** | Specific setting; does **NOT** prove everyone should wait |
| **Azoulay et al.** | Successful founders **NOT very young**; mean age of fastest-growing **1 in 1,000** = **45**; **industry-specific experience** matters | 45 is **NOT** an ideal age |

- **Assets experience builds:** domain knowledge, problem exposure, networks, credibility, pattern recognition, self-knowledge
- **Downside:** inertia; assumptions harden; large firms teach **scale (1 to N)**, **NOT discovery (0 to 1)**
- **Years worked ≠ years learned**

| Founder | Path | Capital Built |
|---------|------|---------------|
| **Falguni Nayar** | 18 years at Kotak Mahindra | Business understanding, networks, credibility, managerial exposure |
| **Kunal Shah** | Freelance, programming, repeated ventures, pivots | Varied venture experience |

### Joining First as Strategy (1.4.1)

- **Learning gap:** what must I learn (technical depth, customer access, regulation, sales, operations)? Where fastest and most transparently? **Waiting is NOT preparation**
- **Joining = deliberate investment**, **NOT** fallback; learn inside someone else's experiment **with a salary**

| Joining Risk | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| Chaos without learning | Startup can be unstructured |
| Narrow role | Little exposure |
| Opaque founders | Limited context |
| Bad practices | Poor organisational habits |
| Worthless equity | If the startup folds |

| Founder | Joinee |
|---------|--------|
| Responsibility, identity, ownership in **one venture** | Gets **cash**; no control; **can leave** |

- **Choose the role, NOT the company:** ask which role lets me **learn, contribute, grow**
- **Company success ≠ individual learning**
- **Flipkart lesson:** assess how people judged it then (founder seriousness, traction, scale of experimentation); joined **with purpose**, not for equity
- **Seed / Series A** startup = some investor buy-in; still check the role

### Decision Tools (1.4.1)

| Choice | Entrepreneurial When |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Found now** | Based on **opportunity, NOT identity** (customers, users, traction; leaving would waste learning) |
| **Join first** | Chosen **intentionally** for learning |

| Affordable Loss Test | Detail |
|----------------------|--------|
| **Found test** | Try 3 or 6 months; if nothing, join a startup |
| **Join test** | Join 6 months; check if you are learning |
| **Applies to** | **Both** paths |

| Evidence | Question |
|----------|----------|
| **Opportunity** | Problem **real, frequent, costly or urgent**? |
| **Capability** | Access to what the next stage needs; can you win customers and market? |
| **Commitment** | Commitments from **team and self**? |
| **Life-context** | Affordable loss, family situation, responsibilities |

- **Family:** do NOT ignore; "family said no and I still won" = **survivorship**; seek **buy-in** for a time-boxed trial
- **Final question:** Who am I? What do I know? What must I learn? What resources today? **Best next move?**

## Quick Reference: Founding vs Joining Decision Guide

| Situation | Action / Answer |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Want the title but no problem | **Do NOT found**; explore or join |
| Real problem, traction, customers | **Found now** (with affordable loss) |
| Lack capability (tech, sales, regulation, operations) | **Join first** in a role that builds it |
| Control is the main motive | Expect trade-off; bootstrapping preserves control but slows growth |
| Wealth or scale is the main motive | Accept shared authority and dilution |
| Learning is the main motive | Compare a **strong startup role** vs founding |
| Need predictable income / family obligations | Weigh life-context evidence; prefer joining or time-boxed trial |
| Joining a startup | Judge the **role**, **NOT** company hype |
| Cold outreach to a founder | Insight(s) + ask, authentic, proof of concept |
| Starting with others | Choose a co-founder you enjoy; document equity |

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Identity rule** | Ask "best next move", **NOT** "founder or employee?" |
| **Reason rule** | Wanting the title is **NOT** a reason to found |
| **Problem-first rule** | Fall in love with the problem; make something people want |
| **Readiness rule** | Readiness = opportunity + people + motivation + capacity, **NOT** full knowledge |
| **Optimisation rule** | Decide what you optimise for: autonomy, learning, upside or stability |
| **Rich vs King rule** | Resources cost control; know which you value |
| **Bias rule** | Seek disconfirming evidence; discount survivor and hindsight stories |
| **Experience rule** | Years worked **≠** years learned; domain experience helps |
| **Role rule** | Company success **≠** your learning; choose the role |
| **Evidence rule** | Opportunity, capability, commitment, life-context |
| **Affordable-loss rule** | Time-box and cap loss for **either** path |
| **Co-founder rule** | Do **NOT** start solo; put equity and decision rights on paper |
| **Outreach rule** | Proof of concept + authentic, top-down insight-and-ask |

## MODULE 2: Who Should You Found With?

---

### Module Question and Approach

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Core question** | Who should I build with, and how do I tell **before it is too late**? |
| **Founding seat** | Choose co-founders **deliberately** |
| **Joining seat** | **Inherit** the team; you cannot pick, but can **evaluate whether they picked well** |
| **Method** | **Heuristics, NOT a checklist**; team formation is hard to predict (investors doubted Infosys co-founders would stick) |
| **Risk** | Seeing risk **≠** eliminating it; it means you do not walk in blind |
| **Also covered** | Couple co-founders: **double-edged sword** (tailwind or headwind) |

---

### Zepto / KiranaKart Case

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Founders** | Palicha and Vohra: childhood friends, Stanford dropouts, unicorn in record time |
| **Third person** | **Ansh Nanda**: product/tech work, early retail partnerships |
| **Promise** | **Verbal 20% stake + co-founder title**, conditional on staying until a date; equity **not** received |
| **Aftermath** | Legal dispute, police complaint, private settlement |
| **Lesson** | **Structural default, NOT malice**; cannot screen for "dishonest founders" |
| **Why trust failed** | Original pair's trust **did not extend** to the skill-based outsider |
| **Predictability** | Co-founder failures are **predictable**, with signals **visible from outside** |

---

### Solo vs. Team Founding

| Factor | Team | Solo |
|--------|------|------|
| **Average outcome** | **Higher survival and scale** | Lower |
| **Risk** | **Distributed** | **Single point of failure** |
| **Judgment** | **Distributed**; unlikely all wrong together | One view, no one to correct |
| **Resilience** | Covers burnout, illness | Everything stops |

- **A bad team is worse than a good solo founder** (energy goes into friction)
- **Team premium appears only when the team functions**
- Right advice: **right co-founder, or start alone**; **NOT** "always get a co-founder"

---

### Homophily

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Definition** | Bonding with people like you ("birds of a feather"); same college, hostel, job, city, language |
| **Appeal** | Familiar = trusted; already tested together; efficient |
| **Core flaw** | Optimizes **comfort, NOT complementary skill** |
| **Skill overlap** | Overlapping strengths and **blind spots**; "decisions with a twin" |
| **Network** | **Compressed** network; same circles |
| **Survivorship bias** | Only successes visible (Flipkart, Meesho); blow-ups unseen |
| **Research** | Teams of **friends have higher turnover** than strangers |
| **Why** | Reluctance for blunt conversations ("elephant in the room") |
| **Rule** | "Known each other forever" **lowers** confidence **unless** roles and final-decision rights are discussed |
| **Net effect** | Early speed and comfort; **NOT** reliable breadth of skills or stability under pressure |

---

### Trust vs. Complementarity

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Trust** | Good faith; will not cheat; safe to be vulnerable |
| **Complementarity** | Skills, networks, judgment you lack; more ground covered together |

| Combination | Result |
|-------------|--------|
| **High trust, zero complementarity** | Identical skills and blind spots |
| **Complementarity, limited trust** | Cover gaps but **functional strangers** under pressure; **NOT** automatically a worse decision |

- **Common error:** infer complementarity from trust (never tested)
- **Practical test:** *Would I choose this person for this problem if I had not already trusted them?*
  - Answer **no** is **NOT disqualifying**; **deliberately look for skill gaps**
- **Mission alignment** also required (Jobs and Wozniak: complementary skills, missions not aligned)

---

### Two Team-Formation Strategies

| Feature | Interpersonal attraction | Resource-seeking |
|---------|--------------------------|------------------|
| **Basis** | Like, trust, get along | Gap: capital, tech, distribution, domain credibility |
| **Order** | **Relationship first** | **Capability first** |
| **Example** | Zepto pattern; most early founding | "What will you bring?" conversations |
| **Pros** | Fast; fewer early fights | Complementary on paper |
| **Risk** | **Homophily trap** | **Untested under pressure** |
| **Failure** | Same blind spots | Perfect pitch deck, **collapses in year 2** |

- **Diagnostic question:** *How did the founders meet and why did they pick each other?*
  - Warm story, **no skills mentioned** -> probe skills
  - **All skills, no warmth** -> note the risk

---

### Razorpay Counterexample

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Founders** | Harshil and Shashank; IIT Roorkee, same student tech group |
| **Roles** | **Harshil:** strategy, external relations, outward vision; **Shashank:** product and tech build |
| **Why it worked** | Different working styles + honesty to divide roles |
| **Adversity** | **100+ investors rejected**; started as **crowdfunding**, changed after listening to users |
| **Insight** | Shared adversity plausibly **forced role clarity** |
| **Research point** | Personality/working-style diversity beats sameness, **even in a homogeneous pair** |
| **Dangerous homophily** | **NOT** "same college" but "same college **and** think/work identically" (echo chamber) |

---

### Role Diversity

| Orientation | Focus | Metaphor |
|-------------|-------|----------|
| **Outward** | Partnerships, fundraising, market positioning | Company's **face** |
| **Inward** | Product, architecture, operations | Company's **engine** |

- Useful diversity = **decision orientation**, **NOT** "opposites attract"
- Joining question: **which founder owns my reporting area, and is ownership real or assumed?**
- Same-background pairs work only when **complementary roles are consciously built**; **NOT** automatic
- **Amagi:** Bhaskar (CEO, media, vision); Srividya (internal technical); Srini (marketing, moved to US)
- **NSRCEL startup:** investor offered funding if one founder stayed in the US; family reasons blocked it; **company split** (pressure shakes balance)

---

### External Signals of Founding-Team Health

| Signal | Healthy | Warning |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **How they talk about each other** | Same origin story; specific, generous about other's role | One centres self; vague or minimizing |
| **Role clarity** | **Named role split** | "We both do everything"; random division |
| **Equity** | **Documented, vetted agreement** | Verbal understanding; **~70% split equity in month 1, often equally, no vesting** |
| **Disagreement** | Open, gentle sparring ("we fight all the time") | "**We never fight**" = more concerning |
| **Third person / early hire** | Talked about and respected (Mohandas Pai brought in at founder level at Infosys) | Ignored |
| **Roles vs. background** | Roles match actual backgrounds | Both describe the **same job** differently |

- Signals **guarantee nothing**; read **indirectly** (podcasts, YouTube, interview cues)
- Joining can be a **conscious bet** if the role builds **lasting capability** (sales, marketing, agents, investors); stay cautious about betting on a team **long-term**

## Quick Reference: Choosing and Assessing Co-founders

| Situation | Action/Answer |
|-----------|---------------|
| Deciding solo vs. team | Team better **on average**; but **right co-founder or start alone** |
| Co-founders are close friends/old classmates | Expect **homophily**; check skills, blind spots, network overlap; confirm roles and final-decision rights discussed |
| Strong trust felt | **Do NOT** infer complementarity; apply the **"would I choose them without prior trust?"** test |
| Co-founders met by liking each other | **Interpersonal attraction**; risk = **homophily trap**; probe skills |
| Co-founders matched by gaps | **Resource-seeking**; risk = **untested under pressure** |
| Same-background pair | Succeeds only with **clear role split** (Razorpay) |
| Evaluating a team to join | Ask how they met; check origin story, role split, equity paperwork, disagreement style, third-person treatment, roles vs. backgrounds |
| Equity agreement is only verbal / equal with no vesting | **High risk** (KiranaKart/Ansh Nanda pattern) |
| Founders say "we never fight" | **More concerning** than open sparring |
| Role builds transferable capability | Can accept team risk **consciously** |
| Startup stuck between success and failure | Pitfall; gut feel in **12 to 18 months**, certainty by **18 to 24 months** |

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Structural, not moral** | Founder conflicts come from structure, **NOT** bad intentions; cannot be screened by "trustworthiness" |
| **Bad team < good solo** | Team premium appears only when the team functions |
| **Friends ≠ safe** | Friend teams have **higher** turnover than stranger teams |
| **Trust ≠ complementarity** | Test each separately |
| **Practical test** | Would I pick this person for this problem without prior trust? |
| **Two strategies, two risks** | Interpersonal = homophily trap; resource-seeking = untested working relationship |
| **Diagnostic question** | How did the founders meet and why did they pick each other? |
| **Role split** | Outward (face) + inward (engine); ownership must be **real, NOT assumed** |
| **Equity** | Must be **documented and vetted**; verbal promise = fragile |
| **Disagreement** | Open sparring is healthy; "we never fight" is a warning |
| **Third person** | How founders treat early contributors reveals team quality |
| **Signals are indirect** | They guarantee nothing; read them without interrogating |
| **Align early** | Ask what each is in this for, exit horizon, and wildest-success vision |
| **Layer bets** | For failure, every level of the bet must fail |
| **Affordable loss** | Fix the runway you can lose before leaving a job |
| **Beware survivorship bias** | Visible successes hide the many failures with the same traits |

## MODULE 3: Choosing the Right Startup to Join

---

### Scope of Evaluation (Joinee View)

| Area | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **Founder behaviour** | Use of power, handling of disagreement |
| **Culture** | Working norms, safety |
| **Attrition** | Who joins, leaves, stays |
| **Psychological safety** | Can problems be raised? |

### Startup Health from Inside (Zepto Case)

- **Health** = worth joining as a place, **NOT** venture performance
- **Diagnostic:** **relationships, roles, rewards**

| Story | Facts |
|-------|-------|
| **Healthy on paper** | Equity dispute settled by **documented settlement**; strategy/outward face vs **product** split held for years |
| **Culture concerns** | Meetings at **2:00 AM** (CEO's schedule); long hours; **experienced professionals avoid**; **dark patterns** admitted (since removed) |

- **Trap:** one-dimension diagnostic gives half the picture; outside reader sees only the first story they meet
- Check: how dimensions form once founder has power; signals absent from pitch deck; learning; what you gain vs give up

### Joinee versus Co-founder

| Aspect | Co-founder | Joinee |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Decision | Who to build with | Whether to hand **2-3 years** to people who already decided |
| Roles/equity | Negotiated **before day one** | **Inherited** |
| Access | Inside | **Few interviews + public data** |

- **Three actionable reads:** **attrition**, **equity documentation**, **POSH committee**
- Investors: worth knowing, but first-time joinee has **no levers**

### Founder Mode versus Manager Mode

| Mode | Idea |
|------|------|
| **Manager mode (conventional)** | Hire good managers, **get out of the way** (causal stage) |
| **Founder mode** | Delegating too early = **drift from mission/vision**; stay involved |
| **Chesky example** | Took decision rights back; company **healthier** |
| **Critics** | Founder mode = **micromanagement** |

- **Both sides partly right**; labels unreliable: **nobody calls themselves a micromanager**

### Does Disagreement Survive?

| Feature | Healthy | Dysfunctional |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Target | **Decision** | **Person** |
| After losing | Heard something | Stop bringing **bad news** |
| Dissent | Still employed next morning | Unwelcome; **attrition rises** |

| Case | Signals |
|------|---------|
| **Ola** | Founder in many decisions; **~13 senior exits in 5 years**; **45% in one year**; hired CEO left in **4 months**; **revolving door** |
| **Airbnb** | Founder took ownership on drift; **turned around** |

- **Diagnostic question:** What happened **last time** someone told the founder he was wrong?

| Answer | Reading |
|--------|---------|
| **Specific story** (incl. founder wrong, changed course) | Good |
| **General philosophy** of open door | **No specifics** = warning |

### Using Attrition

- Dysfunction **permeates**: shouted-at-by-founder shouts at juniors
- Ola is **NOT** a bad business; attrition shows **what kind of company**

| Check | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Source | **LinkedIn** |
| Window | **Couple of years** |
| Measure | Joined, left, **tenure**, **destination** |
| Left for education | **Natural progression** |
| Left for another firm | Talk to them |
| **Under 1 year** | **Red flag** |
| Founder comments on ex-employee post | Separation likely **not bad** |
| Small teams (e.g., 6 vs ~30) | Hard to read |

- Also: founder's **social media** (patterns), **industry comparison**

### Psychological Safety and Learning

- **Google research:** psychological safety = **strongest predictor** of high-performing teams (ahead of who was in team)

| | **Low expectation** | **High expectation** |
|---|---|---|
| **Low safety** | **Empathy zone** (lecture term): nobody pushed | **Anxiety zone**: mistakes hidden, nothing learned |
| **High safety** | **Comfort zone**: pleasant, stagnant | **Learning zone**: intensity becomes skill |

- Same intensity, opposite value: anxiety (**blame**) vs learning (**accountability, mistakes worked through**)
- Healthy founder mode (**decision not person**) more likely gives learning zone
- **Cues:** founder's talk on **past failure**; leavers describe **specific learnings**
- Ola: many teams in **anxiety zone**

| Weigh | Not |
|-------|-----|
| **Rate of learning**, scope of work, people, **manager** (check reputation, past employers) | **Pay (LPA)**, **title** |

| Same title: Product Analyst | Large company | 5-person startup |
|-----------------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Ownership | **One metric, one team** | **Whole onboarding** |
| Decisions | **3 layers** of approval | Makes the call |
| Feedback | Slow | Within **a week** |
| Process | Standardised; **no one indispensable** | High impact |

- Ask **delta add**; **knowledge is on tap**; personality, communication, execution matter most
- Great founder + poor manager = little learning; poor founder + great manager = still learn

### Candidate's Responsibility

| Dishonest act | Issue |
|---------------|-------|
| Claim 3-year stay while applying for **master's abroad** | Misleading |
| Building own venture on side without telling | Not true |
| Leaving after **4 months** when known from start | **Disrepute** to self and programme; firms may stop hiring |

- **Do NOT withhold** information; disclose side venture, other offers, plans abroad
- Reciprocity: expecting honest answers from founders means answering honestly

## MODULE 4: Making the Call on a Startup Offer

---

### Module Purpose

- **Core question:** not "Is this a good startup?" but **"Is this the right opportunity for me?"**
- Evaluate **founder, team, product, stage** + own **goals, needs, risk capacity**

---

### The Five-Question Framework

| # | Question | Area |
|---|----------|------|
| 1 | **Who is the founder; what are they optimizing for?** | People |
| 2 | **Is the founding team in equilibrium?** | People |
| 3 | **Is the startup building something real?** | Product |
| 4 | **What stage is the company at; does it match what I need?** | Company |
| 5 | **Can I survive the worst case?** (**most important**) | You |

- **Order:** people, product, company, you
- **Heuristic / thumb rule, NOT a score**; a startup can fail one question and still suit you; **the candidate decides**
- Know **which question fails** and whether it is an **acceptable risk**

---

### Questions 1 and 2: Founder and Team

| Check | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| **Wealth-focused (rich) founder** | Hires experienced leaders; gives up control to grow |
| **Control-focused (king) founder** | Keeps decisions close; may avoid hiring stronger people |
| **Healthy team (3 Rs: relationships, roles, rewards)** | Answers equity, roles, who-decides questions easily |
| **Problem team** | Avoids questions; **two founders give different answers** |

- Both founder types can build good companies; workplaces differ

---

### Question 3: Is the Startup Building Something Real?

#### Delta 4 (Kunal Shah)

$$\text{Irreversible behaviour change} \Leftarrow (\text{New score} - \text{Old score}) \geq 4 \quad (\text{scale } 0\text{ to }10)$$

| Example | Old way | New way |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **UPI** | Cash, change, "pay tomorrow" | Scan and pay |
| **IRCTC** | Station queue | Online (even with a bad UI) |
| **RedBus** | Agent | App |
| **Flipkart** | Travel to book store | Order from home |

- **Brag-worthy proposition:** users tell others; using it feels **smart**
- **Risk of small improvement:** growth can be **bought** with discounts and marketing; customers return to old habits when money slows; **newest employees go first**

#### Three Outside Tests

| Ask | Weak answer means |
|-----|-------------------|
| What happens to users if you **shut down next month**? | They switch to a competitor: **not yet reversible** |
| Will customers **stay without being paid to stay** (no discounts)? | Demand is bought |
| What **numbers** does the founder share? | Only **vanity metrics** (downloads, sales volume) = growth, **not** retention |

- Read **reviews, app stores, comments**; check **how founders respond** to bad comments (honest, responsible = good)
- **Very young startup with no proof:** still ask; judge **knowledge of the field, own competence, openness, honesty** (**trustworthiness**)

---

### Question 4: What Stage Is the Company At?

- **Two parts:** (1) company stage, (2) **what I need from the next 2 years**
- **PMF:** clear customer group wants the product badly; test = **zero marketing budget, still wanted**; demand **pulls** (before PMF the company **pushes**)

| Stage | Features | Joinee experience | Risk / reward |
|-------|----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Pre-PMF** | Searching; product changes in weeks; few people; founders involved | Many jobs; no structure; **no formal mentoring** | **Highest failure**; **greatest learning**; equity pays only on success, high upside |
| **Post-PMF** | Repeatable delivery; sales processes; operations playbook; **effectual to causal**; **founder mode to manager mode**; KPIs and turfs | Distinct roles; learns coordination and building organizations | **Series A** best (investor betting); **Series B** higher salary, less equity |
| **Growth** | Hockey stick; no experiments; compliance; fundraising; informal becomes formal | Quick growth; **manage people**; "get things done" | **Politics and turf wars**; example: Zepto 18 people to about 5 billion valuation in under 3 years |

- Earlier stage = **more equity, lower salary**; later = **higher salary, less equity**

#### Match Need to Stage

| Need | Choose |
|------|--------|
| **Breadth**, unsure what you are good at | **Pre-PMF** or just-about-PMF |
| **Depth** in known interest + senior teacher | **Slightly larger** startup with a mentor |
| **Stable income** (family, education loans) | **Well-funded scaling** startup (Series B or C) |

- **Mismatch** (confused but joining tiny startup, then complaining of no learning) = avoid
- Option: **short trial** (10 days to 2 months), possibly unpaid
- Write down **stage + own needs** for every offer

---

### Question 5: Can I Survive the Worst Case?

#### Plan A, B, Z (Reid Hoffman, The Start-Up of You)

| Plan | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Plan A** | What you do now |
| **Plan B** | Move if A changes; builds on learning |
| **Plan Z** | **Lifeboat** (back to parents, stable job, study) |

- "Safe" option has its own risk; the real danger is **risk you cannot recover from**
- A livable Plan Z = failure is **survivable**; **write it down** ("I'll figure it out" is **NOT** a plan)
- Worst case for a young joinee: **2 to 3 years of compressed experience** + colleagues who later build things

#### Four Worst-Case Questions

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | Shutdown in **9 months**: can I pay bills for **3 to 6 months**? |
| 2 | Will the next employer **understand what I did**? (Employee no. 12 who **built something** = strong; 4 months with no output = weak) |
| 3 | What am I **giving up that cannot be recovered**? (scholarship, rare visa) |
| 4 | What is my **Plan Z**? |

- **Kanwal Rekhi:** never only one train; **finish degree, gain about 3 years, then join a startup**
- **Affordable loss** (effectuation): 1, 2 or 3 years; young + no encumbrances = can risk time; career is a **marathon**
- **Do NOT** gamble when family needs income or loans are pending: **irresponsible**; stay within **limits of responsibility**
- Responsible example: loan paid + simple house + **2-year runway** + friends who offer jobs, then start

#### Family (Indian Context)

| Action | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Take parents' opinion | Tell them **affordable loss** and **fallback plan** |
| Find the **underlying worry** | Reason behind yes or no |
| First-generation / education loans | Pay off first |
| Note | Some parents **want** children to try; "unfavorable personal circumstances" (Zomato founder's family) |

#### Research Before Joining

| Source | Look for |
|--------|----------|
| Tracxn / Crunchbase, Ministry of Corporate Affairs | Funding history, cash position |
| Founder podcasts / interviews | **Consistency** (culture talk vs losing early staff) |
| LinkedIn | How founder responds to people |
| Interview | Are questions welcomed? Ask **"What does winning look like for the founders?"** |
| Former employees | Look at **logic**, **triangulate** |

- **Identity test:** JustBooks vs HP case: large firm = **cog in the wheel**; startup = can change the company's destination
- Join **only if truly believing** the space is right to learn from, **NOT** because others say so

---

### Interview Insights (Design Generalist at Great Learning)

| Theme | Takeaway |
|-------|----------|
| **Company and problem** | Pick the **problem** and **how much of it you own** before equity; **first company sets the tone** |
| **Ownership / breadth** | Owning a whole function gives breadth; be known for one thing (**purple cow / USP**); **problem safari** until a cluster of problems fits |
| **Founder trait** | **Go-getter, relentless**; setbacks needed to go from A to B |
| **Salary** | Not an NGO; optimize for own benefit **too** |
| **Equity** | Discuss **clean and straight**; fresh-joinee ESOPs seem meaningless early; judge **long-term sense**; reasonable cap table; networks outlast failed equity |
| **Bootstrapped** | Control + exit on own terms; **slow but sustainable**; peace of mind |
| **Funded** | "All money comes with strings"; investor gets **preferential exit**; do not raise just because you can |
| **Hyper-growth (10X to 100X)** | Needs money to burn |
| **Culture** | **Default way of doing things**; **no good or bad, only fit**; finance needs hierarchy; **process control** (sales) vs **culture control** (product, tech, design); subcultures exist |
| **Assess culture** | Talk to former + current employees, filter bias; **Hawthorne effect**; set expectations early on both sides |
| **Career pacing** | **Marathon**; build influence and trust; stay long = credibility and network; "run together to run long" |
| **Learning** | Shadow people; execution is getting cheaper, **thinking and knowing what to solve** is rarer |
