#### Jeremy Caradonna's 4 Core Ideas of Sustainability
- **Interconnectedness:** Human society, economy, and the biosphere are indivisible nested systems.
- **Ecological Limits:** Human industrial activity must stay strictly within natural planetary boundaries.
- **Future-Oriented Planning:** Moving beyond short-term quarterly gains toward multi-generational stewardship.
- **Local Resilience:** Decentralized, context-specific solutions provide higher systemic resilience than hyper-centralized models.

### Anthropocene, Planetary Boundaries & Triple Bottom Line (TBL)

- **The Anthropocene:** Current geological epoch (past ~200 years) popularized by **Paul Crutzen**, defined by human activity as the dominant driver of global environmental change.
- **Planetary Boundaries Framework (Johan Rockström & Will Steffen, 2009):** Identifies 9 Earth-system thresholds. As of 2023, **6 of 9 boundaries have been breached** (Climate Change, Biosphere Integrity, Land-system Change, Freshwater Change, Biogeochemical Flows, Novel Entities).

- $\text{Economic (Profit)} + \text{Environmental (Planet)} = \text{Viable}$
- $\text{Social} + \text{Environmental} + \text{Economic} = \text{Sustainable}$

### Systems Thinking, Problem Typology & The Iceberg Model

- **Ecological Footprint:** Measures biologically productive land and water area needed to generate resources consumed and assimilate waste produced (measured in **global hectares / gha**).
  - *Global Footprint:* India has a high total national footprint (3rd globally) but a low per-capita footprint (**1.19 gha**).
- **Reductionist / Mechanistic Thinking:** Breaks systems into isolated parts; root cause of systemic ecological overshoot.
- **Systems Thinking:** Focuses on relationships, feedback loops, emergent behaviors, and leverage points.

| Problem Class | System Dynamics | Methodological Approach | Typical Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Simple** | Linear cause-effect; high repeatability | Standardized recipe / checklist | Routine manufacturing |
| **Complicated** | Multi-step; predictable through expert analysis | Specialized engineering / formulas | Rocket trajectory design |
| **Complex** | Dynamic interactions; emergent behavior | Adaptive management / experimentation | Child development, weather |
| **Wicked** | Ill-defined, nested, contested values, no final stop | Multi-stakeholder systems transformation | Climate change, Urban waste |

#### The Iceberg Model (Levels of Systems Thinking)
1. **Events (Tip):** What happened? (Observable, reactive daily occurrences — lowest leverage)
2. **Patterns / Trends:** What is recurring? (Historical behaviors over time)
3. **Systemic Structures:** What rules, policies, and incentives cause this? (Organizational and physical structures)
4. **Mental Models (Base):** What deep assumptions, worldviews, and beliefs hold the system in place? (Transformative — highest leverage)

> **Q:** Most commonly identified approach to solve interconnected sustainability challenges = **Systems thinking** ✓
> **Q:** Tool from systems thinking used to uncover underlying public mental models and beliefs = **The Iceberg Model** ✓
> **Q:** Why urban waste segregation persists as an unresolved problem despite municipal policies = **People demonstrate an interconnectedness with social behaviors and lack of clear solutions** ✓

### Linear vs. Circular Economies & Cradle to Cradle (C2C)

- **Linear Economy Flow (Cradle-to-Grave):** $\text{Extract (Take)} \rightarrow \text{Make} \rightarrow \text{Distribute} \rightarrow \text{Consume (Use)} \rightarrow \text{Dispose (Waste)}$

- **Linear Economy Critique (*The Story of Stuff*, Annie Leonard):** Relies on endless extraction, planned obsolescence, toxic production, and landfill disposal—structurally impossible on a finite planet.

| Dimension | Eco-Efficiency | Eco-Effectiveness (Cradle to Cradle / C2C) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pioneers** | Traditional Environmental Management | William McDonough & Michael Braungart |
| **Core Goal** | Minimize harm / "Be less bad" | Create regenerative positive impact / "Be good" |
| **Strategy** | 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) / Down-cycling | Systemic redesign eliminating waste |
| **Material Flow** | Extends linear lifespan to grave | Closed loops: **Biological** & **Technical Nutrients** |

#### Kate Raworth’s Doughnut Economics
- **Inner Ring (Social Foundation):** 12 minimum social baselines (food, clean water, healthcare, education, social equity).
- **Outer Ring (Ecological Ceiling):** 9 planetary boundaries (climate change, ocean acidification, chemical pollution).
- **The Doughnut Space:** The environmentally safe and socially just space where humanity can thrive without overshoot or shortfall.

## MODULE 3: Tragedy of Commons and Governing the Commons

---

### Externalities, Goods Classification & Hardin's Dilemma

$$\text{Social Cost} = \text{Private Cost} + \text{External Cost}$$

| Externality Type | Core Mechanism | Real-World Example | Policy Correction |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Negative Production** | Factory offloads pollution onto third parties | Industrial effluent dumped into rivers | **Pigouvian Tax**, effluent standards |
| **Negative Consumption** | Consumer use directly damages shared spaces | Single-use plastic clogging municipal storm drains | Extended Producer Responsibility (**EPR**) |
| **Positive Production** | Company activity yields uncompensated societal benefits | Corporate R&D spillover, solar tech patents | Direct government subsidies / tax credits |
| **Positive Consumption** | Individual choice improves broader community well-being | Mass vaccination programs, rooftop rainwater capture | Public provision, civic incentives |

| Good Classification | Rivalry & Excludability | Defining Characteristics & Examples |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Private Goods** | **Rivalrous** + **Excludable** | Consumption diminishes availability; access easily restricted (e.g., Food, Cars, Clothing). |
| **Common-Pool Resources (CPRs)** | **Rivalrous** + **Non-Excludable** | Consumption subtracts from stock; difficult to exclude users $\rightarrow$ prone to overuse (e.g., Groundwater, Fisheries, Grazing pastures). |
| **Club / Toll Goods** | **Non-Rivalrous** + **Excludable** | One user's consumption doesn't diminish others; access can be restricted (e.g., Subscription streaming, Toll expressways). |
| **Public Goods** | **Non-Rivalrous** + **Non-Excludable** | Non-depletable by individual use; impossible to exclude anyone (e.g., Clean air, National defense, Street lighting). |

- **Garrett Hardin (1968) - Tragedy of the Commons:** Rational individuals acting independently in self-interest deplete open-access, finite common-pool resources ($CPRs$). Modeled via the **Prisoner's Dilemma** (individual dominant strategy to over-extract causes collective ruin).
- **Coase Theorem (Ronald Coase):** If property rights are well-defined and transaction costs are zero, private bargaining achieves optimal efficiency without state intervention.

> **Q:** Definition of a "negative externality" = **A cost that an individual or firm's action imposes on others, not involved in the original transaction** ✓
> **Q:** The "Tragedy of the Commons" models how individual rational self-interest leads to = **Depletion of shared resources** ✓
> **Q:** A village fishery is destroyed because individual fishermen over-extract out of fear that others will do so first. This exemplifies = **Tragedy of the Commons** ✓
> **Q:** Unregulated open-access exploitation of shared common-pool resources predictably results in = **Overexploitation and depletion of the resource** ✓

### Urban Commons Degradation & Elinor Ostrom’s Framework

- **Urban Commons:** Municipal water bodies, floodplains, and urban forests functioning as natural buffers.
- **Chennai Wetlands & *Poromboke*:** T.M. Krishna's *Poromboke* song protested the destruction of Ennore Creek and Pallikaranai marshlands by fly ash and real estate encroachment.
- **2014 Srinagar Floods:** Encroachment shrunk Srinagar's water-sponge lakes to 10% of their 1911 size, causing systemic urban flooding.

#### Elinor Ostrom’s 8 Design Principles for Commons Governance
*Refuted Hardin's false binary that only state coercion or private ownership can save common resources.*

| # | Design Principle | Operational Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Clearly Defined Boundaries** | Strict spatial perimeter and clear user eligibility rules. |
| **2** | **Proportional Equivalence** | Rules match local costs of maintenance to benefits extracted. |
| **3** | **Collective-Choice / Participatory Rulemaking** | Resource users participate directly in creating and modifying rules. |
| **4** | **Active & Accountable Monitoring** | Monitors are the resource users themselves or accountable to them. |
| **5** | **Graduated Sanctions** | Rule violations trigger progressive penalties rather than instant exclusion. |
| **6** | **Fast, Low-Cost Conflict Resolution** | Local, informal mechanisms resolve disputes rapidly. |
| **7** | **Minimal Recognition of Rights to Organize** | Government authorities do not override self-governing local rules. |
| **8** | **Nested Enterprises** | Multi-tiered governance for CPRs spanning larger geographic scales. |

- **Puttenahalli Lake (Bengaluru):** Urban community collective action revived a dying lake by establishing user monitoring, trust structures, and municipal collaboration (Ostrom Principle in practice).

> **Q:** Primary factor enabling the successful citizen revival of Puttenahalli Lake in Bengaluru = **They engaged in consistent collective action** ✓
> **Q:** Elinor Ostrom refuted Garrett Hardin by showing that = **Communities can effectively manage shared resources through self-organizing institutions and collective action** ✓

### Energy Dynamics, India's Power Mix & Just Transitions

- **First Law of Thermodynamics:** Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it is continuously transformed across states.
- **Energy Sources vs. Carriers:**
  - *Primary Sources:* Coal, crude oil, solar irradiance, wind kinetic flow.
  - *Energy Carriers:* Electricity, liquid transport fuels, refined hydrogen.

#### India's Electricity Mix & Structural Consumption Inequity
- **Generation & Emission Impact:** Coal generates **$\sim 70\%$** of total electricity and produces **$95\%$** of power-sector carbon emissions.
- **Consumption Skew by Customer Base:**
  - **Industrial Sector:** Represents only **$1.5\%$** of total consumer connections but consumes **$42\%$** of total grid electricity.
  - **Domestic Sector:** Represents **$79\%$** of total connections but consumes only **$24\%$** of total grid electricity.

- **DISCOM Crisis:** Distribution companies face massive structural debt ($\approx ₹6.77\text{ lakh crores}$) due to agricultural cross-subsidies; high-paying industrial consumers switching to rooftop solar accelerates revenue loss.
- **Jevons Paradox:** Gains in energy efficiency trigger cheaper operating costs, driving higher overall resource consumption.
- **Decentralized Renewable Energy (DRE):** Solar micro-grids and solar water pumps deliver localized power, cutting transmission losses and bypassing grid unreliability.

> **Q:** Fundamental physical principle of energy identified by George Zayden and Charles Morton = **Energy is continuously transformed (e.g., from potential to kinetic, chemical to thermal), and all sources, whether fossil or renewable, are part of this ongoing flow** ✓

### Water Scarcity Realities & Contamination Typology

- **Global Water Composition:** 97.5% Saline $\rightarrow$ 2.5% Freshwater $\rightarrow$ **$<1\%$ accessible liquid freshwater** (remainder locked in glaciers/deep aquifers).
- **The Rainfall-Security Paradox:** High precipitation does not guarantee water security (Cherrapunji receives $>11,000\text{ mm}$ rain yet suffers winter dry taps; Jaisalmer manages on $250\text{ mm}$ via traditional rainwater harvesting).

| Classification | Technical Threshold / Definition | Core Driver |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Water Shortage** | Physical, immediate shortfall in supply vs. demand | Inadequate delivery infrastructure, distribution failure |
| **Water Scarcity** | Volumetric availability is physically inadequate | Arid geography, absolute demand exceeding local capacity |
| **Water Stress** | Annual renewable supply drops below **$1,700\text{ m}^3/\text{capita}$** | India average $\approx 1,400\text{ m}^3/\text{capita}$ (Systemic overdraft) |

| Contamination Category | Causative Agents | Disease Progression & Examples | Treatment Cost & Complexity |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pathogenic** | Biological microbes (Bacteria, Viruses, Parasites) | Rapid onset, acute infectious diseases (Cholera, Diarrhea, Typhoid) | Low cost / Simple (Chlorination, boiling, UV disinfection) |
| **Geogenic** | Naturally occurring mineral leaching (Fluoride, Arsenic, Iron) | Slow-developing, chronic debilitating diseases (Fluorosis, Arsenicosis) | High cost / Complex (Reverse Osmosis, specialized adsorption media) |

### Social Innovation & Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)

- **Digital Public Goods (DPG) vs. Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI):**

- **First Floor — Core Standards & APIs:** Open protocols, data interoperability schemas, and communication specifications.
- **Ground Floor — Source of Truth Registries:** Foundational master datasets (e.g., aquifer maps, verified user registries, pumping assets).

  1. **Identity Rail:** Unique, verifiable identity (e.g., Aadhaar).
  2. **Payment Rail:** Instant, zero-cost, interoperable payment transfer (e.g., UPI).
  3. **Data Exchange / Consent Rail:** Secure, user-permissioned data flows (e.g., Account Aggregators, DigiLocker).
- **Community-Driven Platforms (Digital Commons):** OpenStreetMap (OSM) and Wikipedia leverage decentralized peer governance and open licensing.

### Air Pollution Science, Airsheds & Agricultural Dilemmas

| Pollutant Class | Chemical Species / Size | Primary Origin in India | Direct Atmospheric / Health Impact |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **PM 2.5 / PM 10** | Micro-particulates ($<2.5\mu\text{m}$) | Vehicular exhaust, crop burning, dust | Penetrates alveolar lung barriers, enters bloodstream |

### The Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC)

- **EKC Trajectory (Inverted U-Curve):**
  $$\text{Pre-Industrial (Low Impact)} \xrightarrow{\text{Industrialization}} \text{Peak Degradation (Tipping Point)} \xrightarrow{\text{Service/Tech Shift}} \text{Post-Industrial (Declining Local Pollution)}$$

- **The Hypothesis:** Environmental degradation first worsens with economic development, peaks at an inflection income level, and declines as citizens demand cleaner environments and adopt green technologies.
- **The Reality & Failure:**
  - *Holds partially for:* Localized particulate pollutants ($SO_2$, PM 2.5).
  - ***Completely FAILS for:*** **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)** and aggregate material consumption, which continue to rise with higher per-capita GDP.

> **Q:** An energy strategy prioritizing GDP growth on the assumption that pollution naturally peaks and drops follows the = **Environmental Kuznets Curve** ✓

### Payment for Ecosystem Services (PES) & Carbon Markets

$$\text{1 Carbon Credit} = \text{1 Metric Ton of } CO_2\text{e Avoided or Removed}$$

| Core Criterion | Carbon Market Definition & Verification Requirement |
|:---|:---|
| **Additionality** | Emissions reductions must be **additional** to what would have occurred under "business as usual". |
| **Permanence** | Carbon must remain sequestered indefinitely without risk of early reversal or re-release. |
| **No Leakage** | Protecting carbon in one area must not displace deforestation/emissions to another site. |
| **MRV** | **Measurement, Reporting, and Verification** by independent, accredited third-party registries. |

- **Bastar (Chhattisgarh) Mahogany Project Case Study:** Rural farmers allocated land for mahogany trees for carbon credits; the corporate intermediary captured 88% of the revenue, delivering only 12% to the tribal landowners (highlighting the **equity and additionality crisis** in voluntary carbon markets).

> **Q:** Mandatory technical requirements for generating valid, certified carbon credits = **Permanence, additionality, and MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, Verification)** ✓
> **Q:** A project claiming carbon credits for pre-planned tree plantations while underpaying local tribal communities demonstrates = **Additionality and equity concerns** ✓

### The Multi-Level Perspective (MLP) on Socio-Technical Transitions

- **Transition Dynamics:** Macro-level **Landscape** pressures destabilize locked-in socio-technical **Regimes**, opening windows of opportunity for micro-level **Niche** innovations to mature and break through into the mainstream.

| MLP Analytical Level | Structural Role in Transitions | Real-World Empirical Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Socio-Technical Landscape** | Slow-moving macro external pressures destabilizing existing regimes | Global warming targets, international decarbonization treaties |
| **Socio-Technical Regime** | Established, locked-in rules, technologies, and dominant institutions | Centralized coal-fired power grid, DISCOM financial contracts |
| **Technological Niches** | Protected incubation spaces for radical, novel eco-innovations | **System of Rice Intensification (SRI)**, decentralized solar cooperatives |

- **System of Rice Intensification (SRI):** Scaled not via top-down state decrees, but through decentralized, multi-actor **Communities of Practice** (NGOs, innovative farmers, agricultural extension workers).
- **Just Transition:** Transition policies must not burden vulnerable workers (e.g., thermal coal mine closures requiring proactive re-skilling and regional social safety nets).

> **Q:** The three analytical tiers of Frank Geels' Multi-Level Perspective (MLP) framework = **Niches, regimes, and landscapes** ✓
> **Q:** The "Echo Model" of organizational capacity building expands training through = **Community of practice and sharing problems** ✓
> **Q:** A major systemic hurdle facing India's clean energy capacity transition = **Data gaps and un-commissioned projects** ✓

### Social Entrepreneurship & Mindful Consumption

- **Paryavaran Mitra (Ahmedabad, founded by Ashish Agrawal):**
  - *Context:* Waste collection was a ₹7,000 crore unorganized sector where $\sim 30,000$ rag-picking women were systematically cheated by middlemen using manipulated scales.
  - *Intervention:* Established transparent, digitized weighing and fair-pricing collection points, immediately boosting waste collector daily incomes by 33%.
  - *Institutional Partnership:* Scaled by integrating with the Ahmedabad Cantonment Board and UNDP/Coca-Cola EPR networks, formalizing informal rag-pickers with ID cards, fixed hours, and protective gear.
- **Bridge 4 Change & Mindful Consumption:**
  - *Core Insight:* Social change requires addressing burnout and personal overconsumption.
  - *Mindful Consumption:* Systematically decluttering consumption habits to reduce demand-side pressure on ecosystems.

- **Quantified Impact of Mindful Consumption:**
  - **1 Less Pair of Jeans Purchased:** Saves $\mathbf{3{,}800\text{ Liters}}$ of freshwater (equivalent to 1 year of individual drinking water).
  - **$200\text{ kg } CO_2$ Saved / Person / Year:** Equivalent to the annual carbon sequestration capacity of **10 mature trees**.

- **The Doodh Talai Fable:** A king instructed every citizen to pour 1 liter of milk into a dry pond overnight; morning revealed a pond of pure water because each citizen assumed their single cup of diluted water would go unnoticed. Illustrates the **failure of collective action** when individuals abdicate responsibility.
- **The Pluriverse:** Rejects one-size-fits-all Western developmental blueprints, recognizing diverse, indigenous, community-led models of ecological prosperity.

> **Q:** A consumer choosing to buy fewer clothes and maintain existing garments longer advances = **Enhancing personal wellbeing and contributing to climate change mitigation** ✓

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Analytical Domain | Decision / Evaluation Rule |
|:---|:---|
| **Problem Formulation** | If a problem has no single definitive formulation, contested values, and symptoms nested in other problems $\rightarrow$ Classify as **Wicked Problem**. |
| **Systems Intervention** | If addressing superficial day-to-day occurrences $\rightarrow$ Intervening at **Events Level** (Lowest leverage). If transforming underlying paradigms/beliefs $\rightarrow$ Intervening at **Mental Models Level** (Highest leverage). |
| **Goods Economics** | If a good is **Rivalrous** and **Non-Excludable** $\rightarrow$ It is a **Common-Pool Resource** (Prone to Tragedy of the Commons without governance). |
| **Commons Management** | If community management lacks local monitoring or local rulemaking $\rightarrow$ Ostrom principles violated; long-term resource failure expected. |
| **Carbon Market Integrity** | If a carbon credit project would have occurred anyway under normal business operations $\rightarrow$ **Fails Additionality Rule** (Invalid credit). |
| **Pollution Policy** | If an emissions issue is regional and transboundary $\rightarrow$ Manage across the entire **Airshed**, not via isolated city borders. |
| **Climate Strategy** | If reducing emissions at source $\rightarrow$ **Mitigation**. If adjusting infrastructure to withstand climate shocks $\rightarrow$ **Adaptation**. |
