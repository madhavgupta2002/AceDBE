## MODULE 1: Heuristics, Biases and Risk Preference

### Foundations: Econs vs Humans & Dual-System Thinking

| Dimension | Homo Economicus ("Econ") | Real Human ("Human") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rationality** | Unbounded rationality, perfect foresight | **Bounded rationality**, cognitive limits |
| **Optimization** | Maximizes narrow material payoffs | Uses **heuristics**, influenced by emotions |
| **Self-Control** | Complete willpower, dynamically consistent | Suffers from **present bias** & self-control issues |
| **Social Prefs** | Purely self-interested | Governed by **fairness**, social norms, reciprocity |

- **System 1 (Reflexive):** Fast, automatic, effortless, emotional, heuristic-driven.
- **System 2 (Reflective):** Slow, deliberate, effortful, logical, rule-governed.
- **Heuristic:** Mental shortcut or rule of thumb used to reduce cognitive load under uncertainty.
- **Bias:** Systematic, predictable error or deviation from normative rational choice.

### Representativeness Heuristic & Associated Biases

**Representativeness Heuristic:** Evaluating the probability of an event based on its degree of similarity to a mental prototype or stereotype rather than objective statistical laws.

| Bias | Definition | Canonical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conjunction Fallacy** | Believing a joint condition ($A \cap B$) is more likely than a single general condition ($A$). Violates probability law $P(A \cap B) \le P(A)$. | **Linda Problem:** Linda is judged more likely to be a "feminist bank teller" than just a "bank teller." |
| **Insensitivity to Base Rates** | Ignoring prior statistical base rates in favor of descriptive, representative details. | Estimating an individual is an engineer at 90% certainty despite a 70/30 lawyer-to-engineer population ratio. |
| **Insensitivity to Sample Size** | Disregarding the fact that small samples display higher variance and extreme deviations. | Believing a small hospital and large hospital have equal chances of recording $>60\%$ male births on a given day. |
| **Misconceptions of Chance** | Expecting local sequences of random events to mirror global probabilities. | **Gambler's Fallacy:** Believing "Heads" is due after 5 consecutive "Tails." |
| **Law of Small Numbers** | Believing small samples closely replicate the true underlying population distribution. | **Hot Hand Fallacy:** Believing a basketball player who made 3 shots in a row has "hot hands," despite statistical independence. |
| **Regression to the Mean** | Failing to recognize that extreme deviations are naturally followed by outcomes closer to the average. | Flight instructors falsely concluding punishment works better than praise because a great landing is followed by a worse one. |

### Availability, Anchoring, Affect & Cognitive Biases

| Heuristic / Bias | Definition & Mechanism | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Availability Heuristic** | Assessing event frequency/probability by the ease with which instances come to mind. Driven by **salience** and recency. | Overestimating deaths from plane crashes, war, or terrorism relative to common diseases. |
| **Retrievability Bias** | Sub-type of availability where vivid or personally experienced events are retrieved faster from memory. | Reluctance to fly after viewing crash coverage; refusing to hire from a college after one bad experience. |
| **Anchoring & Adjustment** | Starting from an initial reference value (**anchor**) and adjusting insufficiently to arrive at an estimate. | Arbitrary wheel spin (10 vs 65) distorting estimates of African UN nations (25% vs 45%); initial salary offers. |
| **Affect Heuristic** | Relying on rapid, positive or negative emotional evaluations to make judgments. | Selecting a movie based purely on poster visuals; gruesome photos on cigarette packs reducing smoking. |
| **Identifiable Victim Effect** | Greater willingness to donate aid to a single, named individual than to an abstract statistical group. | Driven by **Affect Heuristic** (empathy triggered by vivid individual stimuli). |
| **Overconfidence** | Subjective confidence systematically exceeds objective accuracy (Overprecision, Overestimation, Overplacement). | **Planning Fallacy:** Underestimating project completion times; 93% of drivers believing they are above average. |
| **Exponential Growth Bias** | Linearizing compounding growth functions, severely underestimating future exponential paths. | Underestimating compound debt accumulation; failing to respect epidemic growth during COVID-19. |
| **Confirmation Bias** | Seeking and weighting evidence that supports pre-existing beliefs while ignoring contrarian evidence. | Sports fans blaming referee errors for losses while attributing wins purely to team skill. |
| **Hindsight Bias** | "I knew it all along" effect; perceiving past events as predictable after the outcome is known. | Eliminating learning opportunities by misremembering prior uncertainty. |

### Risk Preferences: Expected Utility vs. Prospect Theory

$$\text{Expected Value: } E(V) = \sum p_i x_i \quad \Big| \quad \text{Expected Utility: } E(U) = \sum p_i u(x_i)$$

- **St. Petersburg Paradox:** Infinite expected monetary value, but humans pay only small sums to play $\implies$ proves utility is non-linear (concave for wealth).
- **Risk Premium:** The dollar amount an agent sacrifices to guarantee a certain payout rather than face a risky lottery.

```
                  Value v(x)
                     |       Gain Domain (Concave: Risk-Averse)
                     |         * * *
                     |     *
                     |  *
  -------------------+------------------- Outcome x
              *      | Reference Point (0,0)
           *         |
         *           |
       *             | Loss Domain (Convex: Risk-Seeking, Steeper Slope)
```

#### The Three Core Pillars of Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky)
1. **Reference Dependence:** Utility is derived from *changes* relative to a reference point, **NOT** absolute wealth levels.
2. **Loss Aversion:** Losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains ($\lambda \approx 2$).
3. **Diminishing Sensitivity:** 
   - Concave in the gain domain $\implies$ **Risk-Averse** for gains.
   - Convex in the loss domain $\implies$ **Risk-Seeking** for losses.
   - **Disposition Effect:** Tendency of investors to sell winning stocks too early (locking in gains) while holding losing stocks too long (gambling to avoid realizing losses).

#### Probability Weighting Function
- **Possibility Effect:** Small probabilities are heavily **overweighted** ($0\% \to 5\%$ feels massive; drives lottery purchases and insurance against rare disasters).
- **Certainty Effect:** High probabilities are **underweighted** relative to absolute certainty ($95\% \to 100\%$ carries disproportionate premium).
- S-shaped weighting curve crosses the $45^\circ$ objective benchmark once.

### Mental Accounting & Hedonic Editing

**Mental Accounting (Richard Thaler):** Cognitive system where individuals categorize, budget, and track money in discrete, non-fungible accounts, violating economic **fungibility** (the principle that money has no intrinsic labels).

#### Hedonic Editing Rules (Maximizing Psychological Value)

| Rule | Mathematical Condition | Behavioral Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Segregate Gains** | $v(x) + v(y) > v(x+y)$ | Concave gain curve: multiple small wins yield higher total utility than one lumped win. |
| **Integrate Losses** | $v(-x) + v(-y) < v(-x-y)$ | Convex loss curve: one combined loss hurts less than repeatedly absorbing separate losses. |
| **Silver Lining** | $v(x) + v(-y) > v(x-y) \quad (\text{for } x \ll y)$ | A small gain segregates a bright spot from a large, unavoidable loss. |

#### Utility Decomposition & Sunk Costs

$$\text{Total Utility} = \text{Acquisition Utility (AU)} + \text{Transaction Utility (TU)}$$

- **Acquisition Utility (AU):** $\text{Value of Good} - \text{Price Paid}$ (Standard consumer surplus).
- **Transaction Utility (TU):** $\text{Reference Price (MRP)} - \text{Price Paid}$ (Perceived deal quality).
  - Buying useless clearance goods happens when: **$\text{TU} \gg \text{AU} = 0$**.
  - **Decoupling consumption from payment:** All-inclusive resorts eliminate the negative transaction utility ("pain of paying") of individual transactions.
- **Sunk Costs:** Econs treat sunk costs as unrecoverable and ignore them. Humans honor sunk costs because abandoning an investment forces them to officially close a mental account at an agonizing **loss**.

### Intertemporal Choice & Discounting Models

**Intertemporal Choice:** Evaluating trade-offs between current utility and future utility (saving, health, education).

#### 1. Exponential Discounting (Classical "Econ" Model)

$$U_0 = u_0 + \delta u_1 + \delta^2 u_2 + \dots = \sum_{t=0}^\infty \delta^t u_t \quad (\delta \in [0, 1])$$

- **Discount Factor ($\delta$):** $\delta \approx 1$ signifies patience; $\delta \approx 0$ signifies extreme impatience.
- **Dynamic Time Consistency:** Plans mapped out today remain optimal when evaluated tomorrow. No preference reversal occurs.

#### 2. Present-Biased / Hyperbolic Discounting ($\beta$-$\delta$ Model)

$$U_0 = u_0 + \beta \sum_{t=1}^\infty \delta^t u_t \quad (\beta < 1, \; \delta \le 1)$$

- **$\beta$ (Present Bias):** Short-run discount factor penalizing all delayed periods uniformly.
- **Dynamic Time Inconsistency:** Plans change as time passes. Preferences reverse when distant events become immediate.
  - **Vices:** Immediate gratification, delayed cost $\implies$ **Preponed consumption**.
  - **Virtues:** Immediate cost, delayed gratification $\implies$ **Postponed consumption**.

| Agent Type | Self-Control Awareness | Parameter Rule | Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Naif** | Unaware of present bias; assumes future self will be an Econ. | $\hat{\beta} = 1$ | Procrastinates virtues; succumbs to vices repeatedly. |
| **Sophisticate** | Fully aware of future self-control failure; uses backward induction. | $\hat{\beta} = \beta$ | Imposes **commitment contracts** to restrict future actions. |
| **Partial Naif** | Recognizes self-control problem, but underestimates its severity. | $\beta < \hat{\beta} < 1$ | Adopts imperfect, insufficient commitment strategies. |

- **Commitment Devices:** Ulysses contracts, stickK.com, CARES savings accounts for smokers, alarm clocks that shred money or donate to charity.
- **Dominated Contracts:** Voluntarily agreeing to penalties/lower wages if a target is missed (e.g., Kremer's factory study where workers voluntarily chose half-pay penalties to maintain discipline).

### Quantitative Problem Solving: Intertemporal Choice

**PYQ:** Calculate the one-year discount factor ($\delta$) if individual A is an exponential discounter indifferent between ₹40 today and ₹80 in two years.

> *Why: Exponential discounting equates current utility to future discounted utility via $u_0 = \delta^t u_t$.*
> $$40 = \delta^2 \times 80$$
> $$\delta^2 = \frac{40}{80} = 0.5$$
> $$\delta = \sqrt{0.5} \approx 0.7071$$
> **Answer = 0.71** ✓
> *(Ensure not to divide by 2; exponential discounting squares $\delta$ over two periods)*

### Strategic Thinking: Econs (Game Theory) vs. Humans (Level-$k$ & CH)

| Strategic Model | Theoretical Assumption | Equilibrium Concept | Behavioral Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Econ Strategy** | Common knowledge of rationality; infinite iteration | **Nash Equilibrium (NE)**, SPNE via backward induction | Plays 0 in Beauty Contest; exits immediately in Centipede game. |
| **Level-$k$ Thinking** | Boundedly rational hierarchy; agents assume all others are at Level-$(k-1)$ | Best response to perceived distribution | $L_0$ (random), $L_1$ (responds to $L_0$), $L_2$ (responds to $L_1$). |
| **Cognitive Hierarchy (CH)** | Players assume rivals are distributed from Type 0 to Type $(k-1)$ via **Poisson($\tau$)** | Best response to Poisson mixture | Higher $\tau \implies$ deeper reasoning, higher firm profits, better survival. |

```
Prisoner's Dilemma Matrix:
                     Prisoner 2: Not Confess    Prisoner 2: Confess
Prisoner 1: Not Confess      (-1, -1)                  (-10, 0)
Prisoner 1: Confess          (0, -10)                  (-5, -5)
```

- **Dominant Strategy:** Yields strictly higher payoffs regardless of rival actions. (For both prisoners: **Confess**).
- **Nash Equilibrium:** State where neither player can unilaterally deviate to improve payoff. Result: **(Confess, Confess)**.
- **Social Optimum:** **(Not Confess, Not Confess)** gives minimum aggregate jail time (2 years vs 10 years).

### Social Preferences & Perceptions of Fairness

- **Capuchin Monkey Experiment (Frans de Waal):** Demonstrates **inequity aversion**; subjects reject equal food rewards if peers receive superior items for the same effort.
- **Framing & Status Quo:** 
  - Canceling a discount is perceived as **acceptable**.
  - Imposing a surcharge of the exact same magnitude is perceived as **unfair**.
- **Nominal Wage Rigidity:** Workers assess pay cuts in nominal rather than inflation-adjusted real terms. Cutting nominal wages causes outrage and destroys productivity; firms prefer layoffs while preserving status quo nominal pay.
- **Price Gouging:** Raising prices on critical goods during crises (blizzards, hurricanes) triggers massive reputational penalties.

### Choice Architecture & Nudges

**Nudge (Thaler & Sunstein):** Any aspect of choice architecture that alters human behavior in a predictable manner **without** forbidding options and **without** significantly changing economic incentives.

**Libertarian Paternalism:** Preserves total freedom of choice (**Libertarian**) while organizing context to maximize welfare (**Paternalistic**).

```
                      SUITABILITY MATRIX FOR NUDGES
                  High Stakes / Rare Decisions (Houses, Pensions)
                                     ▲
                                     │   ★ HIGH NUDGE SUITABILITY
                                     │
           No Immediate Feedback ────┼──── Immediate Costs / Delayed Benefits
                                     │    (Virtues: Savings, Exercise)
                                     ▼
```

#### Core Elements of Choice Architecture

| Architecture Element | Principle | Real-World Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Defaults** | Humans follow the path of least resistance. | Automatic 401(k) enrollment; opt-out organ donation. |
| **Active Choice** | Requiring users to state a preference explicitly. | Mandatory Yes/No organ donation prompts during driving license renewal. |
| **Post-Completion Error** | Forgetting cleanup steps after achieving the primary goal. | ATMs returning bank cards **before** cash is dispensed; vehicle gas caps tied by cord. |
| **Descriptive Social Norms** | Highlighting majority cooperative behaviors. | HMRC letters: "9 out of 10 people in your area pay tax on time." |
| **Salience & Visual Cues** | Aligning design affordances with behavioral intention. | Etched housefly in Schiphol urinals; eye-level fruit in cafeterias. |
| **Pre-Commitment** | Overcoming hyperbolic discounting via future allocation. | **Save More Tomorrow (SMarT):** Committing future pay raises to retirement savings. |

### Foundations, Typology & Experimental Design

**Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT):** Random assignment to Treatment ($T$) and Control ($C$) groups to guarantee that any difference in outcomes is causally driven by the intervention, eliminating **selection bias**.

#### Typology of Experiments

| Experiment Type | Environment | Subject Pool | Trade-Offs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Laboratory** | Highly controlled simulated lab | Standard convenience (students; often **WEIRD**) | High internal validity; lower external validity. Payoffs incentivized. |
| **Lab-in-the-Field** | Controlled lab setup in natural environment | Target populations (farmers, village residents) | Combines field relevance with experimental control. |
| **Online / Survey** | MTurk, Prolific, Qualtrics | Broad geographic population | Low cost, high scale; vulnerable to bots and survey fatigue. |
| **Field Experiment / RCT** | Real-world habitat without subjects' knowledge | Target real-world economic agents | Eliminates **Hawthorne Effect**; high external validity. |
| **Quasi- / Natural Exp.** | External shocks or geographic boundaries | Affected real-world populations | Exploits exogenous variation when direct RCTs are infeasible. |

- **WEIRD Subjects:** Samples drawn disproportionately from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic backgrounds.
- **Balance Table:** Compares baseline demographic covariates ($t$-tests, $p > 0.05$) to verify that random assignment created identical groups before treatment.
- **Hawthorne Effect:** Subjects altering their natural behavior simply because they know they are being observed.

### Research Ethics & Institutional Oversight

| Framework / Landmark | Core Mandate / Historical Event | Key Policy / Finding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tuskegee Syphilis Study** | Withheld penicillin from poor African American men (1932–72). | Catastrophic ethical failure; led directly to modern human subjects protection. |
| **Milgram & Stanford Studies** | Electric shock obedience and mock prison abuse. | Demonstrated severe psychological harm; eliminated abusive protocols. |
| **Belmont Report (1979)** | Established the Three Ethical Pillars. | **1. Respect for Persons** (Informed Consent).<br>**2. Beneficence** (Do No Harm; minimize risk).<br>**3. Justice** (Fair distribution of burdens). |
| **Institutional Review Board (IRB)** | Independent multi-disciplinary panel (min. 5 members). | Reviews studies: **Exempt** (minimal risk), **Expedited**, or **Full Board**. |
| **Economics Anti-Deception Norm** | Methodological taboo against using false info. | Deception is strictly banned in economics to prevent subject pool contamination. |

## Quick Reference: Decision & Policy Rules

| Empirical Situation / Context | Behavioral Driver | Prescribed Policy / Intervention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Low retirement savings rate | Present bias & loss aversion | **Save More Tomorrow (SMarT)** default escalation |
| High employee error on secondary tasks | Post-completion error | **Forced function** (e.g., return card before cash) |
| Organ donation shortage | Inertia & default bias | **Opt-out default** or **Mandatory Active Choice** |
| Low minority hiring (Information gap) | Statistical discrimination | **Standardized skill tests; eliminate info asymmetry** |
| Minorities underperforming under identity cues | Stereotype threat | **Blind auditions / anonymized grading** |
| Men dominating tournament entry | Male overconfidence | **Single-sex tournament tracks** |
| Women overloaded with low-promotability work | Uncoordinated expectations | **Random rotation & public praise recognition** |
| Price hike during natural disaster | Social fairness norms violated | **Non-price queue rationing / transparent cost framing** |

## Key Decision Rules Summary

| Decision Rule | Operational Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hedonic Editing Rule** | Segregate gains into small parcels; bundle losses into single aggregates. |
| **First Elimination in Beauty Contest** | Eliminate all numbers strictly $> 66.67$ (since $\frac{2}{3} \times 100 = 66.67$). |
| **Present Bias Reversal Rule** | For vices: preponed. For virtues: postponed. |
| **Sophisticate Identification Rule** | Operates with $\hat{\beta} = \beta < 1$; actively demands dominated commitment contracts. |
| **Deception Policy in Economics** | Deception is universally banned by experimental economics norms. |
| **Libertarian Paternalism Criterion** | An intervention must never ban options or alter financial payoffs significantly. |
