
# **Financial Statements and Business Performance**

# **Module 1: Introduction to Financial Accounting and Mechanics of Accounting**

## **1. Scope and Purpose of Financial Accounting**
*   **Definition:** Financial accounting involves the systematic recording, summarizing, and reporting of business transactions.
*   **Key Outputs (Financial Statements):**
    *   **Income Statement (Profit & Loss A/c):** Shows Revenue earned vs. Expenses incurred. Result = Profit or Loss.
    *   **Balance Sheet:** Shows Sources of Capital (Liabilities/Equity) and Utilization of Capital (Assets) at a specific point in time.
    *   **Cash Flow Statement:** Summarizes cash inflows and outflows.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   To record transactions systematically (replacing reliance on memory).
    *   To answer: How much capital was raised? How was it used? What is owed to suppliers? What is due from customers? Is the business profitable?

## **2. Forms of Business Organisations**
Distinct features affect how equity/capital is recorded, though general accounting mechanics remain similar.

| Form | Ownership & Liability | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Owned by 1 individual. **Unlimited Liability**. | Owner and business are the **same entity** in the eyes of the law. Owner bears all losses/profits. |
| **Partnership** | Owned by partners. **Unlimited Liability** (Jointly & Severally liable). | governed by Partnership Deed. Profit/Loss distributed per deed (or equally if undefined). |
| **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** | Partners have **Limited Liability**. | Personal wealth is protected. Liability limited to the investment in the business. |
| **Company (Joint Stock)** | Registered under Companies Act. **Limited Liability**. | Shareholders are owners. Voting rights depend on **number of shares**. |
| *-- Public Ltd* | Shares traded on stock exchanges. | High liquidity; public can subscribe. |
| *-- Private Ltd* | Shares **not** traded on exchanges. | Limited liquidity. |
| **Co-operative Society** | Members with common interest (e.g., Amul). | Voting is **One Member = One Vote** (irrespective of investment). Mutual benefit focus. |

## **3. Users of Accounting Information**
Accounting is the "Language of Business" used to communicate performance to stakeholders.

*   **Investors:** Assess business performance and return on investment.
*   **Lenders (Banks):** Assess repayment capacity and solvency before granting loans.
*   **Credit Rating Agencies:** Assign ratings (e.g., AAA) to judge creditworthiness/stability.
*   **Suppliers:** Assess financial viability to determine if they can supply on credit.
*   **Government/Tax Authorities:** Determine tax liabilities and use data for economic planning (e.g., NITI Aayog).
*   **Employees/Unions:** Assess profitability for wage negotiations, bonuses, and job security.
*   **Customers:** Assess long-term stability (crucial for products requiring long-term service/support).

## **4. Double-Entry System of Bookkeeping**
*   **Concept:** Every transaction has a two-fold effect. Every Debit (Dr.) must have a corresponding Credit (Cr.).
*   **Origin:** Latin terms — *Debitum* (what is due) and *Creditum* (something entrusted/loan).
*   **Process Flow:**
    1.  **Source Documents:** Evidence of transaction (Invoice, Bill, Receipt, Voucher).
    2.  **Journal:** Recording transactions chronologically (Date-wise) as they occur.
    3.  **Ledger:** Classifying entries into specific accounts (T-format).
    4.  **Trial Balance:** Summarizing debit and credit balances to check arithmetical accuracy.
    5.  **Financial Statements:** Preparation of Final Accounts.

## **5. Classification of Accounts & Golden Rules**
To record transactions, accounts are classified into three categories.

### **A. Personal Accounts**
Related to individuals, companies, or organizations.
*   **Natural Personal:** Human beings (e.g., Mr. Ram, Narasimhan).
*   **Artificial Personal:** Corporate bodies/institutions (e.g., SBI, Asian Paints Ltd, Infosys).
*   **Representative Personal:** Represents a group (e.g., Outstanding Rent, Prepaid Insurance, Sundry Debtors/Creditors).
*   **Golden Rule:**
    *   **Debit the Receiver**
    *   **Credit the Giver**

### **B. Real Accounts**
Related to assets/properties of the business.
*   **Tangible:** Can be touched/felt (e.g., Cash, Machinery, Building, Furniture, Inventory).
*   **Intangible:** Cannot be touched (e.g., Software, Patents, Spectrum License fees).
*   **Golden Rule:**
    *   **Debit what Comes In**
    *   **Credit what Goes Out**

### **C. Nominal Accounts**
Related to expenses, losses, incomes, and gains. These accounts do not carry a balance to the next year; they are closed to the P&L Account.
*   *Examples:* Salary, Rent, Electricity charges, Sales, Interest received.
*   **Golden Rule:**
    *   **Debit all Expenses and Losses**
    *   **Credit all Incomes and Gains**

---

## **6. Books of Accounts**

### **Subsidiary Books (Day Books)**
Used when transaction volume is high to avoid cluttering the main Journal.
*   **Purchase Book:** Records all credit purchases of goods.
*   **Sales Book:** Records all credit sales of goods.
*   **Cash Book:** Records all cash receipts and payments.
*   *Note:* Non-frequent transactions (like buying a building on credit) go to the *General Journal*, not specific subsidiary books.

### **Ledger**
*   **Definition:** The book of final entry where transactions are classified by account (e.g., Cash A/c, Capital A/c).
*   **Structure:** T-shape. Left side = Debit (Dr.); Right side = Credit (Cr.).
*   **Subsidiary Ledgers:** Detailed ledgers for specific groups:
    *   *Sundry Debtors Ledger:* Individual accounts of all customers.
    *   *Sundry Creditors Ledger:* Individual accounts of all suppliers.
    *   *General Ledger (GL):* Contains the summary accounts (Control Accounts) for Debtors/Creditors and all other Real/Nominal accounts.

### **Trial Balance**
*   A statement summarizing the net balance (Debit or Credit) of every ledger account.
*   **Purpose:** To verify that Total Debits = Total Credits (Arithmetical accuracy).

---

## **7. The Accounting Equation**
The foundation of the Balance Sheet. It must always remain balanced.

**Basic Formula:**
$$Assets = Liabilities + Owner’s Equity$$

**Expanded Formula:**
$$Assets = Liabilities + (Capital + Revenue - Expenses - Dividends)$$

**Key Transaction Effects (Examples):**
1.  **Start business with Cash:** Assets (Cash) $\uparrow$, Equity (Capital) $\uparrow$.
2.  **Purchase Goods for Cash:** Assets (Inventory) $\uparrow$, Assets (Cash) $\downarrow$ (Net Asset change is 0).
3.  **Purchase Goods on Credit:** Assets (Inventory) $\uparrow$, Liabilities (Creditors) $\uparrow$.
4.  **Pay Expenses (Rent):** Assets (Cash) $\downarrow$, Equity (via Expenses) $\downarrow$.
5.  **Revenue Earned:** Assets (Cash/Debtor) $\uparrow$, Equity (via Revenue) $\uparrow$.

---

## **8. Comprehensive Accounting Mechanics (Summary of Example)**
*   **Entity Concept:** The owner (Mr. Ram) and the Business are separate. Capital invested is a liability of the business to the owner.
*   **Cost of Sales:** When goods are sold, two entries happen:
    1.  Recognize Revenue (Credit Sales, Debit Cash/Debtor).
    2.  Recognize Expense (Debit Cost of Goods Sold, Credit Inventory).
*   **Depreciation:** Reduction in value of fixed assets.
    *   Entry: Debit Depreciation Expense, Credit Accumulated Depreciation (Contra-Asset).
*   **Accruals:**
    *   **Prepaid Expense:** Paid in advance (Asset).
    *   **Outstanding Expense:** Incurred but not paid (Liability).
*   **Closing Stock:** Unsold goods at the end of the period reduce the "Cost of Material Consumed" expense and appear as an Asset.

# **Module 2: Preparation of Profit and Loss Account, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement**

## **1. Adjustment Entries**
Financial statements must reflect a "True and Fair View." Adjustments are necessary to account for Accruals, Deferrals, and Non-Cash items, adhering to the **Matching Concept** (Revenue matched with expenses of the same period).

### **A. Adjustments Related to Income**
| Scenario | Impact on P&L (Income) | Impact on Balance Sheet | Entry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accrued Income** (e.g., Interest earned on FD but not received) | Increases Income | **Asset** (Accrued Interest) | Dr. Accrued Interest <br> Cr. Interest Income |
| **Percentage of Completion** (Long-term contracts like construction) | Recognize revenue proportional to work done | **Asset** (Unbilled Revenue) | Recognize revenue based on $\%$ of work completed in the current year. |

### **B. Adjustments Related to Expenses**
| Scenario | Impact on P&L (Expense) | Impact on Balance Sheet | Entry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prepaid Expenses** (e.g., Insurance paid in Oct for full year) | Reduces Expense (Remove future period portion) | **Asset** (Prepaid Insurance) | Dr. Prepaid Insurance <br> Cr. Insurance Expense |
| **Outstanding Expenses** (e.g., Dec electricity bill paid in Jan) | Increases Expense (Record current period cost) | **Liability** (Outstanding Exp) | Dr. Electricity Expense <br> Cr. Outstanding Electricity |

### **C. Provisions (Estimations)**
Used when the exact amount is unknown but the liability is probable.
*   **Provision for Bad Debts:**
    *   Estimating % of receivables that may default.
    *   Entry: **Dr. Bad Debt Expense** | **Cr. Provision for Doubtful Debts**.
    *   *Presentation:* Deducted from Sundry Debtors (Asset side) or shown as Contra-Asset.
*   **Provision for Warranty:**
    *   Matching sale revenue with future repair costs.
    *   Entry (Creation): **Dr. Warranty Expense** | **Cr. Provision for Warranty**.
    *   Entry (Actual Repair): **Dr. Provision for Warranty** | **Cr. Inventory/Cash** (No P&L impact at time of repair).
*   **Gratuity:**
    *   Accrued liability for employee service (e.g., 15 days salary per year).
    *   Entry: **Dr. Gratuity Expense** | **Cr. Provision for Gratuity** (Long-term Liability).

### **D. Inventory Consumption Adjustment**
Since purchase entries record inflow to stores, consumption must be calculated to determine the expense.
*   **Formula:** $$Material Consumed = Opening Stock + Purchases - Closing Stock$$
*   **Entry:**
    *   Dr. Cost of Material Consumed (Expense)
    *   Cr. Inventory (Asset)

### **E. Depreciation (Fixed Assets)**
Allocation of asset cost over its useful life.
*   **Nature:** Non-cash expense.
*   **Entry:** **Dr. Depreciation Expense** | **Cr. Accumulated Depreciation**.
*   **Presentation:** Shown as a deduction from the Gross Block (Asset) in the Balance Sheet to arrive at Net Block.

### **F. Revaluation of Financial Assets**
*   **Fair Value Through Profit & Loss (FVTPL):** Short-term/Trading assets. Gain/Loss goes to **P&L**.
*   **Fair Value Through Other Comprehensive Income (FVTOCI):** Long-term investments. Gain/Loss goes to **Other Equity** (Reserves), not P&L.

---

## **2. Preparation of Income Statement (Profit & Loss Account)**
*   **Nature:** Summary of all **Nominal Accounts** for a specific period.
*   **Purpose:** To determine Profit or Loss (Performance).
*   **Standard Sequence:**
    1.  **Revenue:** Sales (Net of returns) + Other Operating Revenue (e.g., sale of by-products like molasses).
    2.  **Other Income:** Interest, Profit on sale of assets.
    3.  **Expenses:**
        *   Raw Material Consumed.
        *   Employee Benefits (Salaries, PF, Gratuity).
        *   Operating Expenses (Power, Rent, Freight).
        *   Depreciation & Amortization.
        *   Finance Costs (Interest).
    4.  **Profit Before Tax (PBT)** $\rightarrow$ Less: Tax $\rightarrow$ **Profit After Tax (PAT)**.

### **P&L Appropriation Account**
Links P&L to the Balance Sheet (Retained Earnings).
*   **Opening Balance** (Retained Earnings)
*   (+) **Net Profit** for the year
*   (-) **Dividends** declared
*   (-) Transfer to **General Reserves**
*   (=) **Closing Balance** (Carried to Balance Sheet under "Reserves & Surplus").

---

## **3. Preparation of Balance Sheet**
*   **Nature:** Summary of **Real and Personal Accounts** at a specific point in time.
*   **Purpose:** Statement of Wealth / Financial Position.

### **Structure**
*   **Sources of Funds (Equity & Liabilities):**
    *   **Shareholders' Equity:** Share Capital + Reserves & Surplus (Retained Earnings).
    *   **Non-Current Liabilities:** Long-term borrowings (Loans), Long-term provisions (Gratuity).
    *   **Current Liabilities:** Trade Payables (Creditors), Short-term loans, Outstanding expenses.
*   **Application of Funds (Assets):**
    *   **Non-Current Assets:**
        *   *Tangible:* Property, Plant, Equipment (Net of depreciation).
        *   *Intangible:* Software, Patents, Goodwill.
    *   **Current Assets:** Inventories, Trade Receivables (Debtors), Cash & Bank, Loans & Advances given.

---

## **4. Cash Flow Statement**
Summarizes cash inflows and outflows. Mandatory for companies.

| Activity Head | Includes | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operating** | Principal revenue-generating activities. | Cash from Customers, Payments to Suppliers, Salaries, Taxes paid. |
| **Investing** | Acquisition/Disposal of long-term assets. | Purchase/Sale of Machinery, Sale of Investments, **Interest Received**, **Rent Received** (if non-operating). |
| **Financing** | Changes in size/composition of capital/borrowings. | Issue of Shares, **Taking/Repaying Loans**, **Dividends Paid**, **Interest Paid**. |

*   **Direct Method:** Uses actual cash receipts and payments (from Cash Book).
*   **Indirect Method:** Starts with Net Profit and adjusts for non-cash items (Depreciation) and working capital changes.

---

## **5. Partnership Accounting**
Distinct from companies; governed by Partnership Deed.

### **Key Features**
*   **Entity Concept:** Partners and Firm are separate entities for accounting. Interest on capital/drawings is recorded.
*   **Capital vs. Current Accounts:**
    *   *Capital A/c:* Records fixed capital contribution.
    *   *Current A/c:* Records fluctuating items like Share of Profit, Drawings, Interest on Capital/Drawings.

### **Goodwill Valuation**
*   Value of future profitability based on historical performance.
*   **Calculation:** Average Historical Profit $\times$ Multiplier (e.g., 3 years or 5 years purchase).

### **Events in Partnership**
1.  **Admission of a Partner:**
    *   Revalue Goodwill (Create Asset: **Dr. Goodwill | Cr. Old Partners' Capital** in *Old Ratio*).
    *   New partner brings Capital (Dr. Cash | Cr. New Partner Capital).
    *   Profit Sharing Ratio changes. Existing partners sacrifice share.
2.  **Retirement of a Partner:**
    *   Revalue Goodwill again (Incremental value).
    *   Distribute increase to *All Partners* (including retiree).
    *   Settlement: Pay retiring partner cash or convert dues to a Loan account.

---

## **6. Introduction to Company Accounts**
*   **Share Capital:** Money raised by issuing shares to the public.
*   **Terminology:**
    *   *Face Value (Par Value):* Base cost (e.g., Rs. 10).
    *   *Share Premium:* Excess amount paid by investors (e.g., Issue Price Rs. 100 - Face Value Rs. 10 = Premium Rs. 90). Premium goes to **Securities Premium Account** (part of Reserves).

### **Accounting Stages for Share Issue**
1.  **Application:** Money received with application.
    *   *Oversubscription:* Excess money refunded or adjusted.
2.  **Allotment:** Shares formally assigned.
3.  **Calls:** Balance money collected in stages (First Call, Final Call).

### **Forfeiture of Shares**
*   If a shareholder fails to pay allotment/call money, shares are cancelled (forfeited).
*   **Key Rule:** Money already received is **NOT refunded**.
*   **Accounting Treatment:**
    *   The amount already collected on forfeited shares is transferred to **Capital Reserve** (Capital Profit).
    *   It is *not* treated as revenue profit.

### **Foreign Currency Transactions**
*   If a loan/payable is in foreign currency (e.g., USD), it must be revalued at the Balance Sheet date rate.
*   **Adjustment:**
    *   If Liability increases (Rupee depreciates): **Dr. Exchange Loss (Expense) | Cr. Foreign Loan (Liability)**.

# **Module 3: Accounting Concepts**

## **1. Introduction**
*   **Definition:** Accounting concepts are the basic assumptions, rules, and principles that act as the "grammar" of the language of business.
*   **Purpose:** To ensure financial statements provide a "True and Fair View" and maintain consistency for comparison.

---

## **2. Core Accounting Concepts**

### **A. Money Measurement Concept**
*   **Principle:** Only transactions that can be expressed in monetary terms (currency) are recorded.
*   **Recorded:** Sales, Expenses, Salary, Purchase of Assets.
*   **Not Recorded:**
    *   Skill level of employees.
    *   Brand value generated internally (unless acquired).
    *   R&D breakthroughs (until they result in a commercial product/patent with value).
    *   HR Survey rankings (e.g., "Best Place to Work").

### **B. Entity Concept**
*   **Principle:** The Business and the Owner are **two separate legal/accounting entities**.
*   **Implications:**
    *   **Capital:** Treated as a liability of the business towards the owner.
    *   **Drawings:** Personal expenses of the owner paid by the business are not business expenses; they reduce capital.
    *   **Inter-branch:** Different branches of the same bank/company treat each other as separate entities for transaction recording (Receivables/Payables).

### **C. Going Concern Concept**
*   **Principle:** Assumption that the business will continue its operations indefinitely (infinite life).
*   **Key Application:**
    *   **Fixed Assets:** Recorded at cost and depreciated over useful life (not expensed immediately), assuming the business will exist to use them.
    *   **WIP Inventory:** Semi-finished goods valued as assets, assuming they will be finished and sold.
*   **Exceptions:**
    *   **Mining Companies:** Finite life based on mineral reserves.
    *   **BOOT Projects:** (Build-Own-Operate-Transfer) e.g., Airports/Roads where assets are transferred to the government after a fixed period (e.g., 30 years).

### **D. Cost Concept (Historical Cost)**
*   **Principle:** Assets are recorded at the **original price paid** to acquire them, not the current market value.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Fixed Assets:** Shown at `Cost - Accumulated Depreciation`.
    *   **Intangible Assets:** Recorded at cost only if acquired (e.g., Purchased Brand/Patent). Self-created brands are ignored.
*   **Exceptions (Fair Value Accounting):**
    *   **Financial Assets:** Investments (Shares/Bonds) are revalued to market price at year-end.
    *   **Impairment:** If an asset's value drops permanently (e.g., fire damage), it must be written down.

### **E. Dual Aspect Concept**
*   **Principle:** Every transaction has a two-fold effect. This is the basis of the **Double-Entry System**.
*   **Accounting Equation:**
    $$Assets = Liabilities + Equity$$
*   **Example:** Taking a bank loan $\rightarrow$ Increases Asset (Cash) + Increases Liability (Loan).

### **F. Accounting Period Concept**
*   **Principle:** The infinite life of a business is divided into shorter time intervals (Year/Quarter) to measure performance.
*   **India:** Financial Year is **April 1st to March 31st**.
*   **Implications:**
    *   Necessitates **Adjustment Entries** (Accruals and Prepayments).
    *   *Example:* Tuition fee paid in Oct (for 1 year) must be split: 6 months revenue for current year, 6 months as "Unearned Revenue" (Liability) for next year.

### **G. Conservatism Concept (Prudence)**
*   **Principle:** **"Anticipate no profit, provide for all possible losses."**
*   **Application:**
    *   **Inventory Valuation:** Valued at **Cost or Market Price (NRV), whichever is LOWER**.
        *   *Scenario:* Purchased Steel at 70, Market is 80 $\rightarrow$ Record at 70 (Cost).
        *   *Scenario:* Purchased Copper at 80, Market is 72 $\rightarrow$ Record at 72 (Market).
    *   **Revenue:** Recognize only when reasonably certain.
    *   **Provisions:** Create provisions for Bad Debts, Warranty claims, Sales returns.

### **H. Matching Concept**
*   **Principle:** Expenses incurred to earn revenue must be recognized in the **same period** as the related revenue.
*   **Application:**
    *   **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** Only the cost of units *sold* is expensed; cost of unsold units remains in Inventory (Asset).
    *   **Depreciation:** Matching asset cost against revenue generated over its life.
    *   **Outstanding Expenses:** Salary for March paid in April is recorded as March expense.

### **I. Consistency Concept**
*   **Principle:** Accounting methods/policies should remain the same from one period to another to allow comparison.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Inventory valuation method (FIFO vs. LIFO).
    *   Depreciation method (Straight Line vs. WDV).
*   **Change:** Permitted if required by law or for better presentation, but the impact on profit must be disclosed.

### **J. Materiality Concept**
*   **Principle:** Disclose items that are significant (material) to decision-making. Insignificant items can be ignored or aggregated.
*   **Application:**
    *   Small assets (Calculators, Pens, Waste bins) are expensed immediately rather than capitalized/depreciated, even if they last > 1 year.
    *   Rounding off discrepancies in utility bills (e.g., electricity reading dates overlapping year-end) can be ignored if immaterial.

---

## **3. Accounting Standards**
*   **Definition:** Written policy documents covering the aspects of recognition, measurement, treatment, presentation, and disclosure of accounting transactions.
*   **Body:** Issued by the Accounting Standards Board (ASB) of ICAI (in India).
*   **Examples:**
    *   Revenue Recognition.
    *   Inventory Valuation.
    *   Cash Flow Statements.
    *   Fixed Assets & Depreciation.

---

## **4. Practical Application Scenarios (from Exercises)**

| Scenario | Concept Applied | Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electricity Bill:** Bill generated on April 2nd includes consumption for March (prev year). | **Materiality & Accounting Period** | If material, prorate (Avg daily consumption $\times$ days in March). If immaterial, record when billed. |
| **Discount Coupons:** Store issues coupons valid for next purchase. | **Matching & Conservatism** | Provision for discount should be created in the current year (based on estimated redemption rate) to match current sales. |
| **Book Publisher:** Books sold with a "Right to Return" unsold copies. | **Conservatism** | Do not recognize full revenue. Recognize revenue only for books likely *not* to be returned (based on past estimates). |
| **Cotton Waste:** Purchased for cleaning machines. | **Materiality** | Expense immediately. Do not treat as inventory/asset. |
| **Identical Machines:** Bought at different prices due to FX fluctuation. | **Historical Cost** | Record Machine A at 20L and Machine B at 19.4L (Actual price paid). |
| **Spectrum Fee:** Paid for 20 years right to use. | **Going Concern + Matching** | Treat as Asset. Amortize (expense) over 20 years. |

## **5. Financial Statement Analysis Exercises (Summary)**

### **A. Balance Sheet Preparation**
*   **Equation Check:** Total Assets must equal Total Equity + Liabilities.
*   **Contra Assets:** Accounts like "Accumulated Depreciation" or "Provision for Doubtful Debts" are deducted from the respective assets (Gross Block / Debtors).

### **B. Analyzing Changes**
*   **Decrease in Non-Current Assets:** Likely due to sale of division or restructuring.
*   **Decrease in Liabilities:** Repayment of loans or waivers during restructuring.
*   **Negative Retained Earnings:** Indicates the firm incurred losses.

### **C. Company Accounts (Share Issue)**
*   **Share Application:** Money received initially.
*   **Share Capital:** Recorded at Face Value.
*   **Share Premium:** (Issue Price - Face Value). Recorded in Reserves.
*   **Forfeiture:** If a shareholder fails to pay allotment money, shares are cancelled. The money *already received* is **not refunded**; it is transferred to **Capital Reserve** (Capital Profit).

### **D. Partnership Exercise**
*   **Profit Distribution:** Based on agreed ratio (or capital contribution ratio if specified).
*   **Retirement:**
    *   Goodwill is valued (e.g., Average Profit $\times$ Multiplier).
    *   Retiring partner gets their share of Capital + Share of Profit + Share of Goodwill.
    *   Accounting Entry: **Dr. Goodwill (Asset)** | **Cr. Partners' Capital Accounts**.
*   **Admission:**
    *   New partner brings Cash.
    *   Entry: **Dr. Cash** | **Cr. New Partner Capital**.

# **Module 4: Revenue Recognition**

## **1. Introduction to Revenue Recognition**
*   **Basic Principle:** Revenue is recognized when performance obligations are fulfilled, consideration is measurable, and collection is reasonably certain.
*   **The Sales Cycle & Recognition:**
    1.  **Purchase Order Received:** No revenue (Contract not executed).
    2.  **Advance Received:** No revenue (Liability created).
    3.  **Production:** Generally no revenue (WIP Asset created).
    4.  **Delivery:** **Revenue Recognized** (Risk and reward transferred).
    5.  **Cash Collection:** Asset conversion (Receivable $\rightarrow$ Cash).

---

## **2. Methods of Revenue Recognition (Long-Term Contracts)**
Used for construction (roads, airports, metros) or software development taking multiple years.

### **A. Percentage of Completion Method (POCM)**
*   **Concept:** Revenue and Profit are recognized proportionately to the work completed in each accounting period.
*   **Usage:** When estimates of progress and costs are reliable.
*   **Formula:**
    $$\text{Revenue Recognized} = \text{Total Contract Value} \times \% \text{ of Work Completed}$$
    $$\% \text{ Completed} = \frac{\text{Costs Incurred to Date}}{\text{Total Estimated Costs}}$$
*   **Nature:** Aggressive method (recognizes profit before project completion).

### **B. Completed Contract Method (CCM)**
*   **Concept:** Revenue and Profit are recognized **only** when the contract is 100% complete and delivered.
*   **Interim Treatment:**
    *   Costs incurred $\rightarrow$ **Work in Progress (Asset)**.
    *   Cash received $\rightarrow$ **Advance from Customers (Liability)**.
*   **Nature:** Conservative method.

### **C. Cost-First Recovery Method**
*   **Concept:** Revenue is recognized only to the extent of costs recovered.
*   **Rule:** No profit is recognized until the **total cash collected exceeds the total cost** of the project.
*   **Application:** Used when collection is uncertain or in early stages of risky ventures.

---

## **3. Handling Losses in Long-Term Contracts**
*   **Golden Rule of Conservatism:** While profit is recognized based on the method chosen (POCM/CCM), **expected losses must be recognized IMMEDIATELY.**
*   **Scenario:** If at any stage (e.g., Year 2), it is estimated that the *total* project cost will exceed *total* revenue:
    1.  Reverse any profit recognized in previous years.
    2.  Book the entire estimated loss for the whole project in the current year (via Provision for Loss).

---

## **4. Methods for Uncertain Collections**

### **A. Instalment Method**
*   **Concept:** Used when collection risk is high. Revenue/Profit is recognized proportionately as cash instalments are received.
*   **Calculation:**
    1.  Calculate **Gross Margin %** $= \frac{\text{Total Profit}}{\text{Total Sales}}$.
    2.  **Profit Recognized** $= \text{Cash Collected in Period} \times \text{Gross Margin \%}$.
*   **Accounting:** Unrecognized profit is kept in a **Deferred Gross Profit** account (Liability).

### **B. Delivery Method (Sales Method)**
*   **Concept:** Standard method. Revenue recognized fully upon delivery/invoice.
*   **Requirement:** Reasonable certainty of collection.

---

## **5. Other Revenue Recognition Scenarios**

### **A. Production Method**
*   **Scenario:** Mining (Gold, Copper) or Agriculture (Grains).
*   **Concept:** Revenue is recognized when the product is mined/harvested (before sales).
*   **Justification:** Products have a ready market and guaranteed/stable prices.
*   **Nature:** Most aggressive method.

### **B. Input Method**
*   **Scenario:** Service contracts (e.g., Software implementation on "Time & Material" basis).
*   **Concept:** Revenue recognized based on effort/inputs (e.g., hours spent).
*   **Calculation:** $\text{Hours Spent} \times \text{Billing Rate per Hour}$.

### **C. Franchise Business**
*   **Initial Fee:** Recognized as revenue only when the **service is delivered** (e.g., when the shop opens/setup is complete), not when cash is received.
*   **Royalty:** Recognized periodically based on sales made by the franchisee.

### **D. Consignment Sales**
*   **Parties:** Consignor (Producer) $\rightarrow$ Consignee (Distributor/Agent).
*   **Risk/Reward:** Remains with Consignor until goods are sold to end-user.
*   **Accounting:**
    *   **Transfer to Consignee:** No Sale. Journal: *Dr. Inventory on Consignment | Cr. Inventory*.
    *   **Sale to Third Party:** Consignor recognizes Revenue and Cost of Sales.
    *   **Unsold Goods:** Remain in Consignor's Balance Sheet as Inventory.

---

## **6. Provisions and Matching Concept**
To adhere to the Matching Concept (matching expenses to the revenue they helped generate), provisions are estimated and recorded in the same year as the sale.

### **A. Provision for Doubtful Debts**
*   **Purpose:** To account for credit sales that may not be collected.
*   **Methods:**
    *   *Flat % Method:* % of total outstanding receivables.
    *   *Aging Analysis:* Higher % provision for older dues (e.g., >60 days).
*   **Accounting Entry (Creation):**
    *   Dr. Bad Debt Expense (P&L)
    *   Cr. Provision for Doubtful Debts (Contra-Asset).
*   **Accounting Entry (Actual Default/Write-off):**
    *   Dr. Provision for Doubtful Debts
    *   Cr. Accounts Receivable.
*   **Recovery of Bad Debt:** If written-off money is recovered later:
    *   Dr. Cash | Cr. Provision for Doubtful Debts (Restores the provision).

### **B. Provision for Warranty**
*   **Purpose:** To account for future repair costs on goods sold today.
*   **Calculation:** Estimated % of Sales (based on history).
*   **Accounting Entry (Creation):**
    *   Dr. Warranty Expense
    *   Cr. Provision for Warranty (Liability).
*   **Accounting Entry (Actual Repair):**
    *   Dr. Provision for Warranty
    *   Cr. Cash/Inventory (Spare parts).

### **C. Allowance for Sales Returns**
*   **Purpose:** When goods are sold with a "Right to Return" (e.g., Amazon/Flipkart model).
*   **Concept:** Revenue should not be recognized for the portion expected to be returned.
*   **Adjustment:** Create a provision for the expected margin loss and inventory write-down (if returned goods are scrapped/sold at loss).

---

## **Summary of Methods & Profit Impact**

| Method | Profit Recognition Timing | Suitability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery Method** | At point of sale | Standard retail/manufacturing |
| **POCM** | Pro-rata over contract life | Long-term construction (Reliable estimates) |
| **Completed Contract** | 100% at end of contract | Long-term (Uncertain estimates/Conservative) |
| **Cost-First Recovery** | After costs are fully recovered | High-risk ventures |
| **Instalment Method** | Pro-rata as cash is collected | High default risk |
| **Production Method** | At time of production | Mining/Commodities |

# **Module 5: Inventory Accounting and Valuation**

## **1. Introduction to Inventory**
*   **Definition:** Inventory consists of material/merchandise a company is engaged in producing, buying, and selling.
*   **Types of Inventory:**
    1.  **Raw Materials:** Basic inputs not yet processed (e.g., Cloth for garments, Steel for cars).
    2.  **Work-in-Progress (WIP):** Goods in the production stage but not yet complete.
    3.  **Finished Goods:** Completed products available for sale.
*   **Significance:**
    *   Inventory valuation directly affects **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** and **Gross Profit**.
    *   **Formula:** $$Gross Profit = Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold$$
    *   **COGS Formula:** $$Opening Stock + Purchases - Closing Stock$$

---

## **2. Inventory Accounting Systems**
Firms choose between two systems to record inventory.

| Feature | **Periodic Inventory System** | **Perpetual Inventory System** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Method** | Physical count is done periodically (usually at year-end) to determine closing stock. | Records are updated in real-time for every purchase and sale/issue. |
| **Value Calculation** | Value of unsold units is calculated; COGS is a derived figure. | Balance quantity and value are available at any point in time. |
| **Suitability** | Small businesses, trading firms with low volume/few items. | Large businesses, manufacturing firms, high volume/high value items (e.g., Car manufacturers). |
| **Technology** | Low reliance on technology. | Requires software/systems (e.g., Barcodes, ERP). |
| **Control** | Less control; shrinkage/theft detected only at end. | High control; discrepancies identified immediately. |

---

## **3. Inventory Valuation Methods (Cost Flow Assumptions)**
Since purchase prices fluctuate, firms must assume a flow of costs to value closing inventory and COGS.

### **A. Specific Identification Method**
*   **Concept:** Identifies each specific item in inventory and assigns its **actual cost**.
*   **Suitability:** High-value, low-volume items (e.g., Jewelry, Cars, Real Estate).
*   **Requirement:** Items must be distinguishable (e.g., via Barcodes/Unique IDs).
*   **Formula:** $$Closing Value = \Sigma (\text{Actual Cost of Units on Hand})$$

### **B. First-In-First-Out (FIFO)**
*   **Assumption:** Items purchased **first** are sold/consumed **first**.
*   **Result:**
    *   **COGS:** Based on older (earlier) prices.
    *   **Closing Inventory:** Based on recent (latest) prices.
*   **Impact during Inflation (Rising Prices):**
    *   Inventory Value: **Higher** (Recent high prices).
    *   Profit: **Higher** (Lower COGS).
    *   Tax: **Higher**.

### **C. Last-In-First-Out (LIFO)**
*   **Assumption:** Items purchased **last** (most recently) are sold/consumed **first**.
*   **Result:**
    *   **COGS:** Based on recent prices.
    *   **Closing Inventory:** Based on older prices.
*   **Impact during Inflation (Rising Prices):**
    *   Inventory Value: **Lower** (Old low prices).
    *   Profit: **Lower** (Higher COGS).
    *   Tax: **Lower** (Tax saving strategy).
*   **Note:** LIFO is **not allowed** under Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) or IFRS, but permitted in the US (US GAAP).

### **D. Weighted Average Method**
*   **Concept:** Calculates an average cost per unit after every purchase.
*   **Formula:**
    $$\text{Weighted Average Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Inventory Available}}{\text{Total Units Available}}$$
*   **Result:** Smooths out price fluctuations. The value lies between FIFO and LIFO.

### **E. Retail Method**
*   **Suitability:** Retailers (e.g., Supermarkets, Big Bazaar) with huge volume where individual tracking is hard.
*   **Method:** Uses the **Gross Margin Percentage** to estimate cost.
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Determine Total Sales.
    2.  Calculate COGS: $$Sales - (Sales \times \text{Gross Margin \%})$$.
    3.  Derive Closing Stock: $$Opening Stock + Purchases - \text{Estimated COGS}$$.

---

## **4. Comparative Impact of Valuation Methods**

| Scenario: Rising Prices | **FIFO** | **LIFO** | **Weighted Average** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inventory Value** | Highest | Lowest | Moderate |
| **COGS** | Lowest | Highest | Moderate |
| **Net Profit** | Highest | Lowest | Moderate |
| **Tax Liability** | Highest | Lowest | Moderate |

*Note: If prices are falling, the effects are reversed.*

---

## **5. Inventory in Manufacturing Companies**
Manufacturing involves three stages of inventory valuation.

1.  **Raw Materials:**
    *   Valued at **Purchase Price + Transport/Freight** to bring it to location.
2.  **Work-in-Progress (WIP):**
    *   Valued at **Raw Material Cost + Direct Labor + Share of Manufacturing Overheads**.
3.  **Finished Goods:**
    *   **Cost of Goods Manufactured:** Sum of Material, Labor, Direct Expenses, and allocated Production Overheads (Factory rent, power, supervisor salary).

---

## **6. Inventory in Service Industries**
*   Service firms (e.g., Consultancies, Software) do not hold physical stock of goods for sale.
*   **Inventory Type:** Primarily **Work-in-Progress (WIP)**.
*   **Job Cost Sheet:** Tracks expenses (Labor, Direct Expenses) incurred on a project not yet billed/completed.
*   **Valuation:** Sum of balances of all open (incomplete) Job Cost Sheets = Closing WIP.

---

## **7. Accounting Concepts Related to Inventory**

### **A. Conservatism Concept (Prudence)**
*   **Rule:** Inventory must be valued at **Cost** or **Net Realizable Value (NRV)**, whichever is **LOWER**.
*   **Logic:** Recognize probable losses immediately; do not anticipate profits.
*   **Definitions:**
    *   *Cost:* Expense incurred to bring asset to current condition/location.
    *   *NRV:* Estimated Selling Price minus Estimated Cost to Complete/Sell.

**Example of Conservatism (WIP Valuation):**
*   Cost incurred (WIP): Rs. 20,000.
*   Estimated cost to complete: Rs. 10,000.
*   Final Selling Price: Rs. 28,000.
*   **Calculation:**
    *   NRV = Selling Price (28,000) - Cost to complete (10,000) = **Rs. 18,000**.
    *   Historical Cost = **Rs. 20,000**.
*   **Valuation:** Value at **Rs. 18,000** (Lower of Cost or NRV). Book a loss of Rs. 2,000 immediately.

### **B. Materiality Concept**
*   Minor items (e.g., nuts, bolts, glue) may be expensed immediately rather than tracked as inventory if their value is insignificant.

### **C. Consistency Concept**
*   A firm must use the same valuation method (e.g., FIFO) year after year to ensure financial statements are comparable.

---

## **8. Key Formulas for Problem Solving**

1.  **Cost of Sales (Trading):**
    $$Opening Stock + Purchases - Closing Stock$$
2.  **Cost of Goods Manufactured:**
    $$Material Consumed + Direct Labor + Manufacturing Overheads + Opening WIP - Closing WIP$$
3.  **Gross Margin %:**
    $$\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100$$
4.  **Weighted Average Rate:**
    $$\frac{(\text{Value of Old Stock} + \text{Value of New Purchase})}{(\text{Qty of Old Stock} + \text{Qty of New Purchase})}$$

# **Module 6: Fixed Assets and Depreciation Accounting**

## **1. Introduction to Fixed Assets**
*   **Definition:** Assets used for producing goods or providing services, creating long-term value for the business. They are not meant for immediate sale (unlike inventory).
*   **Classification:**
    1.  **Tangible Assets:** Have physical substance.
        *   *Infinite Life:* Land.
        *   *Finite Life:* Buildings, Machinery, Furniture, Vehicles.
    2.  **Intangible Assets:** No physical substance; provide rights or technology.
        *   *Examples:* Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, Goodwill, Software.

## **2. Cost Determination of Fixed Assets**
*   **Cost Concept:** Assets are initially recorded at their **Historical Cost** (Purchase Price).
*   **Components of Cost:**
    *   Invoice Price (including non-refundable taxes).
    *   Transportation/Freight to bring asset to location.
    *   Installation and Erection charges.
    *   Trial run costs.
    *   Import duties.
    *   *Exclusion:* Repair/Maintenance after the asset is ready for use is an **Expense** (P&L), not capitalized.
*   **Special Scenarios:**
    *   **Demolition Cost:** If land with an old building is bought to build a new one, the cost of demolishing the old building is added to the cost of the **New Building** (or Land cost depending on intent).
    *   **Self-Constructed Assets:** Includes Material + Labor + Direct Overheads + **Interest on loan** (Capitalized only *until* the asset is ready for use).
    *   **Non-Cash Acquisition:** If acquired by issuing shares/bonds, Cost = **Fair Value** of the shares/bonds issued.

## **3. Accounting Concepts Related to Fixed Assets**
1.  **Cost Concept:** Record at cost; ignore market value fluctuations (unless revaluation model is adopted).
2.  **Matching Concept:** The cost of the asset is spread (depreciated) over the years it generates revenue.
3.  **Materiality Concept:** Small assets (e.g., calculators, staplers) are expensed immediately even if they last long, as tracking them is not efficient.
4.  **Betterment Expenses:** Costs incurred to **improve functionality** or **extend life** (e.g., replacing a petrol engine with CNG) are **Capitalized** (Added to Asset Value). Normal repairs are expensed.

---

## **4. Basket Purchase**
*   **Definition:** Acquiring multiple assets (e.g., Land and Building) for a single lump-sum price.
*   **Problem:** Land is not depreciated, Building is. Hence, costs must be split.
*   **Solution:** Allocate the total cost based on the ratio of their **Independent Appraised Values (Fair Values)**.
    *   *Example:* Lump sum paid = 210. Appraised: Land = 80, Building = 160 (Total 240). Ratio 1:2.
    *   Allocated Cost: Land = 70, Building = 140.

---

## **5. Depreciation Accounting**
*   **Definition:** Systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life.
*   **Key Inputs:**
    1.  **Cost:** Purchase price + incidental expenses.
    2.  **Salvage Value (Residual Value):** Estimated resale value at the end of life.
    3.  **Service Life:** Period the asset is expected to be used by the entity (may differ from physical life).

### **A. Depreciation Methods**

| Method | Characteristics | Formula | Suitability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Straight Line Method (SLM)** | Constant depreciation expense every year. | $\frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Salvage Value}}{\text{Useful Life}}$ | Assets with consistent usage (e.g., Furniture). |
| **Written Down Value (WDV)** | Higher depreciation in early years, declining later. Also called *Declining Balance*. | $\text{Net Book Value} \times \text{Rate \%}$ | Assets losing efficiency fast (e.g., Electronics, Vehicles). |
| **Sum of Years' Digits (SYD)** | Accelerated method based on a formula. | $\text{Depreciable Amt} \times \frac{\text{Remaining Life}}{\text{Sum of Digits}}$ | Rapidly depreciating assets. |

*   **SYD Formula:** Sum of Digits = $\frac{N(N+1)}{2}$. For 5 years: $1+2+3+4+5 = 15$.
    *   Year 1 Rate: 5/15; Year 2 Rate: 4/15, etc.

### **B. Partial Year Depreciation**
*   **Pro-rata:** Exact calculation based on months used (e.g., Purchased Feb 1 = 2/12 months depreciation).
*   **180 Days Rule (India):**
    *   Used $\ge$ 180 days: Full year depreciation.
    *   Used < 180 days: Half year depreciation.
*   **Half-Year Convention (US):** 50% depreciation in the year of purchase and sale, regardless of the actual date.

### **C. Multiple Shifts**
*   If an asset works extra shifts (e.g., double shift), wear and tear increases.
*   **Rule:** Typically **50% extra depreciation** is allowed for each additional shift.

### **D. Accumulated Depreciation**
*   A **Contra-Asset** account.
*   Shows total depreciation charged to date.
*   *Balance Sheet Presentation:* Asset (Gross Block) - Accumulated Depreciation = Net Block (Book Value).

---

## **6. Disposal and Exchange of Assets**

### **A. Sale of Asset**
1.  Calculate depreciation up to the date of sale.
2.  Calculate **Book Value** (Cost - Accumulated Depreciation).
3.  Compare **Sale Price** vs. **Book Value**.
    *   Sale > Book Value = **Profit** (Credit P&L).
    *   Sale < Book Value = **Loss** (Debit P&L).
4.  **Journal Entry:**
    *   Dr. Cash (Received)
    *   Dr. Accumulated Depreciation (Reverse total dep)
    *   Dr. Loss on Sale (if any)
    *   Cr. Asset Account (Original Cost)
    *   Cr. Profit on Sale (if any)

### **B. Exchange of Assets**
*   **Similar Assets (Like-kind):**
    *   No Profit/Loss is recognized.
    *   New Asset Value = Book Value of Old Asset + Cash Paid.
*   **Dissimilar Assets:**
    *   Profit/Loss **is recognized**.
    *   New Asset is recorded at its Fair Value.
    *   Profit/Loss = Fair Value of Old Asset - Book Value of Old Asset.

---

## **7. Group Depreciation**
*   **Concept:** Used for a large number of homogenous assets (e.g., 80,000 laptops) where tracking individual assets is difficult.
*   **Method:** Depreciation is calculated on the **Total Pool**.
*   **Disposal:** No profit/loss is recorded on individual sales.
    *   Entry: **Dr. Cash | Cr. Accumulated Depreciation**.
    *   The Asset account remains at cost until the group is retired.

---

## **8. Natural Resources (Depletion)**
*   Examples: Mines, Oil Wells, Quarries.
*   **Term:** **Depletion** (not depreciation).
*   **Method:** Usually **Unit of Production Method**.
    *   Rate per unit = $\frac{\text{Total Cost}}{\text{Estimated Total Reserve Units}}$.
    *   Expense = Units Extracted $\times$ Rate.
*   **Oil & Gas Accounting:**
    *   **Full Cost Method:** Capitalize *all* drilling costs (successful + dry holes). Total cost spread over successful reserves.
    *   **Successful Effort Method:** Capitalize only successful wells. Expense dry holes immediately.

---

## **9. Intangible Assets**
*   **Term:** **Amortization** (spreading cost over useful life).
*   **Valuation:**
    *   *Purchased:* Recorded at acquisition cost.
    *   *Self-Generated:* Generally expensed (not capitalized), except specific development phases in software/pharma.

### **Specific Intangibles**
1.  **Patents/Copyrights:** Amortized over useful legal life.
2.  **Goodwill:**
    *   Recorded **only when purchased** (i.e., acquiring a business for *more* than the fair value of its net assets).
    *   **Not Amortized**. Instead, tested annually for **Impairment** (reduction in value).
3.  **Brands:**
    *   Acquired brands: Capitalized.
    *   Internally generated brands (Advertising): Expensed immediately.
    *   Infinite life brands: Not amortized; tested for impairment.
4.  **Leased Assets (Right-to-Use):**
    *   Improvements to leased assets (Leasehold Improvements) are capitalized.
    *   Amortization Period: **Shorter** of the Asset Life or Lease Period.
5.  **R&D Costs:**
    *   **Research:** Always Expensed.
    *   **Development:** Capitalized only if commercial viability is established (e.g., Software Development); otherwise expensed.

# **Module 7: Reading Financial Statements**

## **1. Overview of Financial Statements**
The three primary financial statements used to assess business performance are:
1.  **Balance Sheet:** Statement of Wealth (Position at a specific point in time).
2.  **Statement of Profit and Loss:** Income Statement (Performance over a period).
3.  **Cash Flow Statement:** Summary of cash inflows and outflows.

---

## **2. The Balance Sheet**
*   **Definition:** A statement representing all assets owned by a company and the claims (liabilities and equity) against those assets at a particular date.
*   **Accounting Equation:** $$Assets = Liabilities + Equity$$
*   **Format:**
    *   **Consolidated Balance Sheet:** Combines the financials of the holding company and its subsidiaries (where stake > 50%). It includes a line item called **"Non-Controlling Interest"** (Minority Interest) representing the portion of subsidiaries owned by others.
    *   **Standalone Balance Sheet:** Financials of the legal entity alone.

### **A. Equity (Shareholders' Funds)**
Represents the owners' claim on the business.

**1. Share Capital:**
*   **Authorized Capital:** Maximum shares a company can issue.
*   **Issued & Subscribed Capital:** Shares actually issued to and bought by investors.
*   **Types:**
    *   *Equity Shares:* Voting rights, variable dividend, residual claim during liquidation.
    *   *Preference Shares:* Fixed dividend, priority over equity in liquidation/dividend payment.

**2. Corporate Actions affecting Share Capital:**
*   **Bonus Shares:**
    *   Free shares issued to existing shareholders.
    *   **Source:** Issued out of Retained Earnings or General Reserves.
    *   **Impact:** No change in Net Worth/Wealth. Increases number of shares; reduces price per share. Improves liquidity.
    *   **Entry:** Equity Share Capital (+), Reserves (-).
*   **Stock Split:**
    *   Reducing the face value of shares (e.g., Rs. 10 to Re. 1).
    *   **Impact:** Increases number of shares; no change in wealth. **No accounting entry required.**
*   **Share Buyback (Repurchase):**
    *   Company buys back its own shares using surplus cash.
    *   **Entry:** Cash (-), Share Capital (-), Reserves (-).

**3. Other Equity (Reserves & Surplus):**
*   **Retained Earnings:** Cumulative profits earned over years minus dividends distributed.
*   **General Reserve:** Created from profit; can be used for any purpose (including dividends).
*   **Capital Reserve:** Created from capital transactions (e.g., acquisition bargain purchase). **Cannot** be used to pay dividends.
*   **Other Comprehensive Income (OCI):** Unrealized gains/losses (e.g., Forex translation, change in fair value of investments).

### **B. Non-Current Liabilities**
Obligations payable after 12 months.
*   **Borrowings:** Long-term loans (Banks, Debentures).
*   **Lease Liabilities:** Present value of future lease payments (Ind AS 116).
*   **Provisions:** Estimated future liabilities (e.g., Gratuity, Pension).
*   **Deferred Tax Liability (DTL):**
    *   Arises due to temporary differences between Accounting Profit and Tax Profit (e.g., different depreciation rates).
    *   *Concept:* It is like an **interest-free loan** from the government effectively deferring tax payments to future years to encourage capital investment (CAPEX).

### **C. Current Liabilities**
Obligations payable within 12 months.
*   **Trade Payables:** Dues to suppliers (Material/Services).
*   **Short-term Borrowings:** Working capital loans.
*   **Unpaid Dividends:** Declared but not yet claimed by shareholders (Transferred to Investor Protection Fund after statutory period).

### **D. Non-Current Assets**
Assets held for long-term use, not for sale.
*   **Property, Plant & Equipment (PPE):** Tangible assets (Land, Building, Machines). Reported at **Net Block** (Gross Cost - Accumulated Depreciation).
*   **Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP):** Assets under construction. No depreciation charged until completed.
*   **Intangible Assets:** Goodwill, Patents, Software, Brands.
*   **Right-of-Use Asset:** Leased assets recognized on the Balance Sheet.

### **E. Current Assets**
Assets expected to be consumed or sold within 12 months.
*   **Inventories:** Raw Material, WIP, Finished Goods.
*   **Trade Receivables:** Amounts due from customers (Net of provision for doubtful debts).
*   **Cash & Cash Equivalents:** Cash in hand, Bank balance, Short-term liquid investments.
*   **Operating Cycle:** Cash $\rightarrow$ Raw Material $\rightarrow$ WIP $\rightarrow$ Finished Goods $\rightarrow$ Receivables $\rightarrow$ Cash.

---

## **3. Statement of Profit and Loss (Income Statement)**
Reports performance (Profit/Loss) over a specific period.

### **Components of Income**
1.  **Revenue from Operations:** Income from core business (Sale of products/services).
2.  **Other Income:** Non-core income.
    *   Interest/Dividend received.
    *   Profit on sale of assets.
    *   **Forex Gains:** Gain due to currency fluctuation on exports.

### **Components of Expenses**
1.  **Cost of Materials Consumed:** Opening Stock + Purchases - Closing Stock.
2.  **Employee Benefit Expenses:** Salaries, PF, Gratuity.
3.  **Finance Costs:** Interest paid on loans.
4.  **Depreciation & Amortization:** Non-cash expense allocating asset cost.
5.  **Other Expenses:** Freight (Production), Advertisement (Marketing), Travel (Admin), CSR activities.

### **Profit Hierarchy**
1.  **EBITDA / PBDIT:** Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortization (Operating efficiency metric).
2.  **PBIT:** Profit Before Interest and Tax.
3.  **PBT:** Profit Before Tax (After deducting interest).
4.  **PAT:** Profit After Tax (Bottom Line).

---

## **4. Cash Flow Statement**
Summarizes cash movements to assess solvency and liquidity. Helps check the "quality of profits" (Accrual vs. Cash).

| Activity Type | Description | Examples (Inflow/Outflow) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operating Activities** | Cash generated from core business operations. | (+) Cash from Sales <br> (-) Payment to Suppliers <br> (-) Salaries/Tax Paid |
| **Investing Activities** | Cash flows related to long-term assets and investments. | (-) Purchase of Fixed Assets (CAPEX) <br> (+) Sale of Assets <br> (+) **Interest/Dividend Received** |
| **Financing Activities** | Cash flows related to capital and debt. | (+) Issue of Shares/Debentures <br> (-) Repayment of Loans <br> (-) **Dividend/Interest Paid** |

### **Analysis of Cash Flow:**
*   **Ideal Scenario:**
    *   **Positive** Operating Cash Flow (Business is profitable).
    *   **Negative** Investing Cash Flow (Company is expanding/investing).
    *   **Positive/Negative** Financing Cash Flow (Depends on growth stage).
*   **Reliability Check:** Compare **Adjusted Accrual Profit (PAT + Non-cash items)** with **Operating Cash Flow**. A large unexplained gap indicates potential manipulation/aggressive accounting.

---

## **5. Key Concepts & Adjustments**
*   **Working Capital:**
    $$Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities$$
    (Capital required to run day-to-day operations).
*   **Minority Interest (Non-Controlling Interest):**
    *   In a consolidated balance sheet, if the parent company owns less than 100% of a subsidiary, the portion of Equity/Net Assets belonging to other shareholders is shown as Minority Interest.
*   **Changes in Inventory:**
    *   In P&L, if Closing Stock > Opening Stock $\rightarrow$ Add difference to profit (or deduct from expenses).
    *   If Opening Stock > Closing Stock $\rightarrow$ Deduct difference from profit (or add to expenses).

# **Module 8: Assessing Business Performance through Financial Statements**

## **1. Introduction to Financial Statement Analysis**
Financial statements (Balance Sheet, P&L, Cash Flow) provide data. Analysis converts this data into insights regarding **Profitability** and **Risk**.

Three major methods of analysis:
1.  **Common Size Analysis** (Vertical Analysis)
2.  **Trend Analysis** (Horizontal Analysis)
3.  **Ratio Analysis** (Relationship Analysis)

---

## **2. Common Size Analysis**
Useful for comparing companies of different sizes (Inter-firm) or changing composition over time (Intra-firm).

*   **Common Size Balance Sheet:**
    *   **Base:** Total Assets = 100.
    *   All assets, liabilities, and equity items are expressed as a **percentage of Total Assets**.
    *   *Insight:* Shows the composition of assets (Fixed vs. Current) and funding mix (Equity vs. Debt).
*   **Common Size Income Statement:**
    *   **Base:** Total Income (Sales) = 100.
    *   All expenses and profit figures are expressed as a **percentage of Total Income**.
    *   *Insight:* Shows cost structure and profit margins.

---

## **3. Trend Analysis**
Useful for assessing growth or decline over a period.

*   **Method:**
    *   Select a **Base Year** (e.g., 2011).
    *   Set Base Year values = 100.
    *   Express subsequent years' data relative to the base year.
*   **Formula:** $$\frac{\text{Current Year Value}}{\text{Base Year Value}} \times 100$$
*   *Insight:* If Sales Trend = 284 and Asset Trend = 400, it indicates assets grew faster than sales (potential under-utilization).

---

## **4. Ratio Analysis: Drivers of Profitability**
Profitability is driven by four key factors: **Asset Management, Cost Management, Leverage Management, and Tax Management.**

### **A. Return on Total Assets (ROTA)**
Measures the efficiency of assets in generating operating profit.
*   **Formula:**
    $$ROTA = \frac{\text{Profit Before Interest and Taxes (PBIT)}}{\text{Total Assets}}$$
*   **Decomposition:** ROTA is a product of Asset Productivity and Cost Efficiency.
    $$ROTA = \text{Asset Turnover Ratio} \times \text{Profit Margin}$$

### **B. Asset Management (Productivity)**
Measures how efficiently assets generate sales.

| Ratio | Formula | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asset Turnover (ATO)** | $\frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ | Revenue generated per rupee of asset. |
| **Fixed Asset Turnover** | $\frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Fixed Assets}}$ | Productivity of long-term assets. |
| **Current Asset Turnover** | $\frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Current Assets}}$ | Efficiency of working capital assets. |
| **Inventory Days** | $\frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{Avg Cost of Sales per day}}$ | How long inventory is held. Lower is better. |
| **Collection Days** | $\frac{\text{Receivables}}{\text{Avg Sales per day}}$ | How fast customers pay. Lower is better. |

### **C. Cost Management (Efficiency)**
Measures control over expenses.

| Ratio | Formula | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Profit Margin** | $\frac{\text{PBIT}}{\text{Sales}}$ | Operating profit per rupee of sales. |
| **Cost Ratios** | $\frac{\text{Specific Expense}}{\text{Sales}}$ | E.g., Material Cost %, Employee Cost %. Lower is better. |

## **5. Leverage Management**
Leverage involves using other people's money (Suppliers' credit and Debt) to increase return for shareholders.

### **Phase 1: Payables Leverage (Operating Liability)**
*   **Metric:** **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)**.
*   **Concept:** Total Assets are funded by Payables + Debt + Equity. If we remove Payables (non-interest bearing), we get Capital Employed.
*   **Formula:**
    $$ROCE = \frac{\text{PBIT}}{\text{Capital Employed}}$$
    Where **Capital Employed** = Total Assets - Non-interest bearing Current Liabilities (Payables).
*   **Analysis:** If **ROCE > ROTA**, the company is effectively using supplier credit to boost returns.

### **Phase 2: Debt Leverage (Financial Liability)**
*   **Metric:** Return on Equity (Pre-tax).
*   **Concept:** Using borrowed money (Debt) to earn more than the cost of interest.
*   **Key Components:**
    1.  **Cost of Debt (Interest Rate):** $\frac{\text{Interest Expense}}{\text{Loan Amount}}$.
    2.  **Spread:** $ROCE - \text{Interest Rate}$.
    3.  **Debt/Equity (D/E) Ratio:** $\frac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Equity}}$.
*   **Relationship Formula:**
    $$\text{Pre-tax ROE} = ROCE + [\text{Spread} \times \text{D/E Ratio}]$$
*   **Insight:**
    *   If Spread is **Positive**, Debt increases ROE.
    *   If Spread is **Negative**, Debt destroys ROE.

## **6. Tax Management**
*   **Objective:** To retain maximum profit after paying statutory dues.
*   **Metric:** **Effective Tax Rate**.
*   **Formula:**
    $$\text{Effective Rate} = \frac{\text{Tax Expense}}{\text{Profit Before Tax (PBT)}}$$
*   **Post-Tax ROE:**
    $$\frac{\text{Profit After Tax (PAT)}}{\text{Equity}}$$

## **7. Risk Analysis (Solvency)**

### **A. Short-Term Solvency (Liquidity Risk)**
Ability to meet obligations due within 1 year.

*   **Current Ratio:**
    $$\frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$
*   **Benchmark:** Generally, **> 2** is considered safe.
*   **Probability Interpretation:** Ratio of 2 implies a 50% probability of collection is sufficient to pay liabilities ($1/2$). Ratio of 1.1 implies 91% collection probability is needed.

### **B. Long-Term Solvency (Financial Risk)**
Ability to service long-term debt and interest.

1.  **Debt to Equity Ratio:**
    $$\frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Equity}}$$
    *   *Benchmark:* Traditionally 2:1, but 1:1 is preferred in modern contexts (industry dependent).
2.  **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR):**
    $$DSCR = \frac{\text{PBDIT} - \text{Tax}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Loan Repayment (Principal)}}$$
    *   *Benchmark:* Should be **> 1**. Indicates cash generation vs. debt obligations.

### **C. Altman Z-Score (Bankruptcy Prediction)**
A composite score using 5 weighted ratios to predict financial distress.
*   **Formula:** $Z = 1.2X_1 + 1.4X_2 + 3.3X_3 + 0.6X_4 + 1.0X_5$
    *   $X_1$: Working Capital / Total Assets
    *   $X_2$: Retained Earnings / Total Assets
    *   $X_3$: EBIT / Total Assets
    *   $X_4$: Equity / Total Debt
    *   $X_5$: Sales / Total Assets
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **Z > 2.675:** Safe / Good Solvency.
    *   **Z < 1.81:** High Risk / Distress zone.

## **8. Summary of Analysis Flow (The "River" Analogy)**
To understand **ROE (The main river)**, trace back the tributaries:
1.  **ROE** depends on $\rightarrow$ **ROCE** and **Debt Leverage**.
2.  **ROCE** depends on $\rightarrow$ **ROTA** and **Payables Leverage**.
3.  **ROTA** depends on $\rightarrow$ **Asset Turnover** and **Profit Margin**.
4.  **Asset Turnover** depends on $\rightarrow$ Fixed/Current Asset efficiency (Inventory/Collection days).
5.  **Profit Margin** depends on $\rightarrow$ Cost Control (Material/Employee costs).
