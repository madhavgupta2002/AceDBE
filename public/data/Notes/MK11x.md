## **Topic 1: Understanding Needs, Wants, and Markets**

### **1.1 Maslow’s Need Hierarchy Theory**
*   **Structure:** Represented as a triangle with five levels; needs move from lower to higher end.
*   **Level 1: Physiological Needs:** Basic survival necessities (Food, clothing, shelter).
*   **Level 2: Safety Needs:** Security of resources to ensure future survival (Jobs, savings, insurance, housing).
*   **Level 3: Social (Love/Belonging) Needs:** Need for connection, family, friends, and acceptance in society.
*   **Level 4: Esteem Needs:** Need for respect, recognition, leadership, and significance in society.
*   **Level 5: Self-Actualization:** Achieving full potential and pursuing passions (e.g., art, music, philanthropy) after other needs are met.
*   **Application:** Marketers must identify which need level their product satisfies. Products can satisfy multiple levels (e.g., a phone satisfies social needs; games satisfy relaxation/physiological needs).

### **1.2 Needs vs. Wants**
*   **Need:** A felt sense of deprivation of basic necessities (e.g., hunger, communication, safety). Marketers **cannot** create needs; they pre-exist.
*   **Want:** Specific satisfiers for needs, shaped by culture and personality (e.g., Need: Food $\rightarrow$ Want: Burger/Pizza). Marketers influence wants.
*   **Value:** Benefit divided by Cost. Value exists when Benefit > Cost.

### **1.3 Market Definitions and Types**
*   **Definition:** A physical or virtual place where buyers and sellers unite to exchange goods/services based on rules, regulations, and competition.
*   **Key Components:** Buyers, Sellers, Goods/Services, Rules/Regulations, Competition.
*   **Types of Markets (by Goods Traded):**
    *   **Commodity Market:** Unbranded bulk goods (grains, pulses, sugar).
    *   **FMCG Market:** Fast Moving Consumer Goods (toothpaste, soap); high frequency, low cost.
    *   **White Goods Market:** Durables (TV, Fridge); low frequency, higher cost.
    *   **Stock Market:** Trading of company equity/services.
*   **Types of Markets (by Transaction):**
    *   **B2C (Business to Consumer):** Retailer selling to end-user.
    *   **B2B (Business to Business):** Manufacturer $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler $\rightarrow$ Retailer.
*   **Types of Markets (by Geography):** Local, National, Global. (Note: Digital markets/platforms like Amazon defy strict geographic boundaries).

## **Topic 2: Introduction to Marketing Concepts**

### **2.1 Key Definitions**
*   **Peter Drucker:** The only valid purpose of a business is to **create a customer**. Marketing is the distinguishing, unique function of business.
*   **Philip Kotler (Social Definition):** A societal process by which individuals/groups obtain what they need/want through creating, offering, and **freely exchanging** products and services of value.
    *   **Free Exchange:** Buyer has freedom to buy; Seller has freedom to sell (Democracy of choice).
*   **Marketing Management (Managerial Definition):** The process of planning and executing the **conception, pricing, promotion, and distribution** of ideas, goods, and services to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organizational goals.
    *   **Ideas can be marketed:** e.g., "Donate Blood," "Save Water," Playlists (Spotify).

*   **Marketing:**
    *   Starts with the **Customer**.

### **2.3 Marketing Vocabulary**
*   **Target Market:** Specific segment the organization focuses on (e.g., youth, women). Opposite is **Mass Market** (one offer for everyone).

## **Topic 4: The Evolution of Marketing**

### **4.1 Historical Stages (Orientations)**
1.  **Production Concept (Industrial Revolution):**
    *   **Focus:** High production efficiency, low cost, mass distribution.
    *   **Strategy:** Availability and Affordability.
    *   **Assumption:** Consumers will buy products that are widely available and cheap.
2.  **Product Concept:**
    *   **Focus:** Product quality, performance, innovative features.
    *   **Risk:** **Marketing Myopia** (coined by Theodore Levitt). Focusing too much on the product/features and losing sight of the customer's actual need (Short-sightedness).
3.  **Selling Concept:**
    *   **Focus:** Aggressive selling and promotion.
    *   **Assumption:** Consumers won't buy enough unless forced/persuaded. Common in unsought goods or B2B.
4.  **Marketing Concept:**
    *   **Focus:** Identifying needs/wants of target markets and delivering satisfaction better than competitors.
    *   **Pillars:** Target market, Customer needs, Integrated marketing, Profitability.
5.  **Societal/Relationship Marketing:** (Modern Era) Focus on social welfare and long-term customer bonds.

## **Topic 6: Case Study: Coca-Cola**

### **6.1 History & Strategy (The Rise)**
*   **Origin:** Created by John Stith Pemberton (1886) as a headache remedy/syrup.
*   **Growth:** Asa Candler (1888) and **Robert Woodruff** (1923) drove expansion.
*   **Woodruff’s Strategy (Production Concept Era):**
    *   Standardized service/bottling quality.
    *   **Distribution:** "Place Coke within arm's reach of desire."
    *   **Promotion:** "The pause that refreshes" (1929).
    *   **Culture:** Invented the modern **Santa Claus** image (1931). Integrated into American culture (WWII soldiers got Coke for 5 cents globally).
*   **Value Proposition:** Functional (Taste/Refreshment) + Experiential/Social (Family, Happiness, Connection).

---

### **6.2 The Rivalry (Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi)**
*   **Coke’s Position:** Traditional, Family-oriented, Nostalgic, Market Leader.
*   **Pepsi’s Entry:** Targeted the **"Pepsi Generation"** (Baby Boomers/Youth).
    *   **Positioning:** Rebel, Wild, Vital, Active, "New Generation."
    *   **Strategy:** Influencers (Michael Jackson), **Pepsi Challenge** (Blind taste test proved Pepsi tasted better).
*   **Differentiation:** Same functional product, but different **Psychographics** (Traditional vs. Rebel).

---

### **6.3 The Decline (1970s - 1980s)**
*   **Market Context:**
    *   Aging population (Coke’s core "Baby Boomers" were aging and drinking less soda).
    *   Health concerns (sugar/diabetes).
*   **Coke’s Mistakes:**
    *   **Complacency:** Sacred cow syndrome (didn't test formula against competitors).
    *   **Diversification:** Lost focus by buying wine companies, plastic bags, water treatment (non-core).
    *   **Legal/Operations:** Antitrust suits, restrictive bottler practices, aging board of directors.
*   **Statistics:** By 1980, Pepsi beat Coke in supermarket share (29.3% vs 29%). Coke's growth slowed to 1-2%.

## **Topic 2: Market Segmentation Concepts**

### **2.1 Definition and Purpose**
*   **Definition:** The process of dividing a heterogeneous market into distinct subsets (segments) of consumers with common needs or characteristics.
*   **Goal:** To generate **Homogeneous groups within a Heterogeneous market**.
    *   **Homogeneous Within:** Members of a segment are similar.
    *   **Heterogeneous Between:** One segment is significantly different from another.
*   **Product vs. Customer:** **Customers are segmented**, not products. Products are created *based* on segmented customer needs.
    *   *Example:* Sunsilk creates different variants (Black Shine, Hair Fall Solution, Straight) because customers have different hair needs.

## **Topic 3: Bases of Segmentation**

### **3.1 Geographic Segmentation**
*   **Basis:** Location and physical environment.
*   **Variables:** Region (North vs. South), City Size (Tier 1 vs. Tier 3), Population Density, Climate (Hot vs. Cold).
*   *Example:* Dunzo/Swiggy focus on high-density cities; Woolens for cold climates.

### **3.2 Demographic Segmentation**
*   **Basis:** Vital and measurable statistics of the population (The "Who").
*   **Variables:** Age, Gender, Income, Occupation, Education, Family Size.
*   **Limitation:** Descriptive only; does not explain *why* a consumer buys (e.g., a 20-year-old and 60-year-old may buy the same jeans).

---

### **3.3 Psychographic Segmentation**
*   **Basis:** Internal self, psychology, and lifestyle (The "Why").
*   **Constituents:** Personality + Lifestyle.
*   **AIO Inventory:** Tool used to measure psychographics:
    *   **A**ctivities (Work, hobbies).
    *   **I**nterests (Food, fashion, family).
    *   **O**pinions (Social issues, politics).
*   **VALS Framework (Values and Lifestyle):**
    *   Developed by Strategic Business Insights (SBI).
    *   Classifies adults into 8 groups based on resources and motivation:
        1.  **Innovators:** Successful, take charge, high self-esteem.
        2.  **Thinkers:** Mature, reflective, value order/knowledge.
        3.  **Achievers:** Goal-oriented, conservative.
        4.  **Experiencers:** Young, enthusiastic, impulsive.
        5.  **Believers:** Traditional, family-oriented.
        6.  **Strivers:** Trendy, resource-constrained.
        7.  **Makers:** Practical, self-sufficient.
        8.  **Survivors:** Low resources, safety-focused.

---

### **3.4 Behavioral Segmentation**
*   **Basis:** Behavior of actual/current customers regarding the product.
*   **Variables:**
    *   **Decision Roles:** Initiator, Influencer, Decider, Buyer, User.
    *   **Occasions:** Regular vs. Special occasion (Festivals).
    *   **User Status:** Non-user, Ex-user, Potential user, First-time user, Regular user.
    *   **Usage Rate:** Light, Medium, Heavy.
    *   **Loyalty Status:** Hardcore (One brand), Split (2-3 brands), Shifting (Moving between brands), Switchers (No loyalty/Deal prone).
    *   **Buyer Readiness:** Unaware $\rightarrow$ Aware $\rightarrow$ Informed $\rightarrow$ Interested $\rightarrow$ Desirous $\rightarrow$ Intending to buy.

---

### **3.5 Technographic Segmentation**
*   **Basis:** Attitude towards technology and online shopping behavior (Concept by Mary Modahl/Forrester Research).
*   **Types:**
    *   **Tech Optimists:** Early adopters, willing to try new tech.
    *   **Tech Pessimists:** Fearful, avoiders, laggards.
*   *Application:* Segmenting based on Income + Tech Attitude (e.g., High Income + Tech Pessimist needs reassurance; High Income + Optimist needs innovation).
*   *Example:* Flipkart "Kids as Adults" campaign targeted mainstream parents (tech pessimists) to build trust in online shopping.

---

### **3.6 Criteria for Effective Segmentation**
1.  **Identifiable & Measurable:** Can you measure the size/purchasing power?
2.  **Substantial:** Is the segment large/profitable enough to serve?
3.  **Accessible:** Can the segment be reached (media/distribution)?
4.  **Differentiable:** Does the segment respond differently to marketing mix elements?
5.  **Actionable:** Does the company have resources to serve this segment?

## **Topic 4: Targeting Market**

### **4.1 Evaluation of Segments (Selection Criteria)**
Marketers analyze three blocks of parameters to select a target:
1.  **Segment Size and Growth:**
    *   Current demand potential.
    *   Future growth rate (Is the segment growing or stagnant?).
2.  **Structural Attractiveness (Porter’s 5 Forces):**
    *   **Threat of New Entrants:** Low entry barriers make a segment less attractive.
    *   **Rivalry:** Intense competition reduces attractiveness (Exit barriers).
    *   **Substitute Products:** Availability of substitutes limits price/profit.
    *   **Power of Buyers:** Strong buyers force prices down.
    *   **Power of Suppliers:** Strong suppliers raise costs.
    *   *Ideal Segment:* High entry barriers, low exit barriers, low rivalry, few substitutes, low buyer/supplier power.
3.  **Company Objectives and Resources:**
    *   Does the segment align with long-term goals (Profit vs. Share)?
    *   Does the firm have the skills/resources to succeed?

---

### **4.2 Targeting Strategies**
1.  **Single Segment Concentration:** Focusing on one specific segment with one product (Niche).
2.  **Selective Specialization:** Selecting multiple segments that are attractive; no synergy necessary between them (Diversification of risk).
3.  **Product Specialization:** Making one product and selling it to different segments (e.g., Microscopes for universities, labs, and government).
4.  **Market Specialization:** Serving many needs of a specific customer group (e.g., Selling stationary, books, and uniforms specifically to schools).
5.  **Full Market Coverage:** Serving all customer groups with all products they might need.
    *   **Undifferentiated:** Mass marketing (One offer).
    *   **Differentiated:** Different products for different segments (e.g., Coca-Cola, GM, Microsoft).

### **2.3 POP and POD**
*   **Points of Parity (POP):** Attribute associations that are **not unique** to the brand but are shared with other brands. Necessary to be considered a legitimate player in the category (e.g., a refrigerator must cool).
*   **Points of Difference (POD):** Attributes or benefits consumers strongly associate with a brand, positively evaluate, and believe they cannot find to the same extent with a competitor.
    *   **Selection Criteria for POD:** Desirable to consumer, Deliverable by company, Distinctive from competitor.
    *   **Rule of Thumb:** Focus on 1-2 key differences to avoid confusion.

### **2.4 Positioning Statement**

*   **Available Market:** Consumers with **Interest + Income + Access**.

*   **Penetrated Market:** Set of consumers actually buying the product.

### **3.2 Demand Concepts**
*   **Market Demand:** Total volume bought by a customer group in a specific area, time, and marketing environment.
*   **Market Forecast:** Expected market demand corresponding to a specific level of industry marketing expenditure.
*   **Market Minimum:** Sales that would take place without any marketing expenditure.

## **Topic 6: Case Study: Coca-Cola (New Coke)**
### **6.1 The Problem**
*   **Market Context (1970s-80s):** Coke was losing market share.
*   **Competitor Action:** Pepsi launched the "Pepsi Challenge" (Blind taste test showing people preferred Pepsi) and targeted the youth ("Pepsi Generation").
*   **Coke’s Diagnosis:** Believed the product (taste) was the problem based on Pepsi's data.
*   **Coke's Action:** Changed the formula to be sweeter (New Coke) to beat Pepsi in taste tests.

### **6.2 The Failure Analysis (5Cs Application)**
*   **Company:** Ignored its core strength—Heritage and Emotional bond. Focused only on product function (taste).
*   **Customer:** Misunderstood the customer.
    *   **Demographic Shift:** The "Coke generation" (WWII era) was aging (consuming less sugar).
    *   **Psychographic Miss:** Customers valued the *brand/nostalgia* more than the *taste*.
*   **Competitor:** Coke reactively followed Pepsi’s strategy (Taste) instead of playing to its own strengths.
*   **Context:** Failed to recognize the aging population trend.
*   **Conclusion:** The failure was due to a **Strategy mismatch** (solving a relationship/brand problem with a product/formula solution). Coke failed to connect with the changing youth demographic while alienating loyalists.

# **Module 5: Business Buying Behaviour and Strategy**

## **Topic 1: Introduction to B2B Markets**
### **1.1 Concept & Definition**
*   **B2B (Business-to-Business):** Transactions where goods/services are bought to be used in the production of other goods/services, or for resale/renting.
*   **The Chain:** Supplier (B2B) $\rightarrow$ Manufacturer (B2B) $\rightarrow$ Retailer (B2B) $\rightarrow$ Consumer (B2C).
    *   *Example:* Tisco sells steel to Maruti (B2B) $\rightarrow$ Maruti sells cars to Dealers (B2B) $\rightarrow$ Dealer sells to You (B2C).
*   **Scope:** Includes raw materials (steel, rubber), equipment (CT scanners, trucks), services (consulting, software), and agricultural produce sold to retailers.

### **1.2 B2B vs. B2C Markets (Comparative Analysis)**
| Feature | B2B Market | B2C Market |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Customers** | Few (Select large buyers) | Many (Mass population) |
| **Transaction Value** | High (Bulk/Millions) | Low (Individual units) |
| **Product Nature** | Customized/Technical | Standardized/Mass produced |
| **Price** | Negotiated (Bidding/Tenders) | Fixed (MRP) |
| **Buying Process** | Complex, Long, Formal | Simple, Short, Impulsive |
| **Decision Maker** | Buying Center (Multiple people) | Individual or Family |
| **Demand Type** | **Derived Demand** | Direct Demand |
| **Relationship** | Long-term (Partnership) | Transactional |

*   **Derived Demand:** Demand for B2B goods depends on demand for the final consumer product.
    *   *Example:* Demand for steel depends on the market demand for cars/apartments.

---

## **Topic 2: Value Proposition in Business Markets**
### **2.1 Three Types of Value Propositions**
1.  **All Benefits:**
    *   **Strategy:** Listing all possible features/benefits of the product.
    *   **Flaw:** "Benefit Assertion" – claiming advantages that customers don't actually care about. Least effective; ignores competitor offerings.
2.  **Favorable Points of Difference (POD):**
    *   **Strategy:** explicitly comparing features against competitors to show superiority.
    *   **Flaw:** Assuming the difference is valuable to the customer. (e.g., *Case:* IC chip maker reduced price needlessly because they focused on service differentiation without knowing the customer didn't value service).
3.  **Resonating Focus (Gold Standard):**
    *   **Strategy:** Focuses on 1 or 2 **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)** that matter most to the customer’s business model.
    *   **Concept:** B2B2C – Helping the customer serve *their* customers better.
    *   **Example (Sonoco):** Redesigned packaging to improve the client’s manufacturing efficiency (5-day shift vs. 7-day shift), reducing labor costs.

---

## **Topic 3: Segmentation in Business Markets**
### **3.1 Difference from B2C**
*   **B2C Goal:** Identify *who* the customer is.
*   **B2B Goal:** The customer is known; the goal is to identify *how to solve their specific problem*.

### **3.2 Segmentation Variables**
1.  **Geographic:** Country, region, centralized vs. decentralized locations.
2.  **Firmographics (Demographics of Firms):**
    *   **Industry:** Construction, Pharma, IT, etc.
    *   **Size:** Revenue, number of employees.
    *   **Ownership:** Government (Tenders/L1), Private, NGO.
3.  **Psychographic (Culture):**
    *   **Risk Attitude:** Risk-taking vs. Risk-averse.
    *   **Importance:** Strategic purchase (High involvement) vs. Routine consumable.
4.  **Behavioral:** Volume, purchase frequency, loyalty status.
5.  **Buying Approach:** Centralized purchasing vs. Local autonomy; Sealed bids vs. Negotiation.

---

## **Topic 4: Buying Benefits and Strategy**
### **4.1 Classification of Benefits**
1.  **Tangible Financial:**
    *   Measurable and monetary (e.g., Horsepower, Fuel efficiency, Price).
    *   *Strategy:* Easy to communicate; standard verify.
2.  **Non-Tangible Financial:**
    *   Monetary impact exists but is hard to measure (e.g., Big Data software increasing future revenue).
    *   *Strategy:* **"Tangibilize the Intangible"** – Use case studies, Excel sheets, and ROI calculators to prove value.
3.  **Tangible Non-Financial:**
    *   Measurable but psychological (e.g., Brand reputation, Global presence).
    *   *Strategy:* Emphasize reliability and risk reduction.
4.  **Non-Tangible Non-Financial:**
    *   Hard to measure, non-monetary (e.g., Trust, 24/7 support during holidays, Relationship).
    *   *Strategy:* Build long-term trust; hard to sell initially but creates "Partnership" status.

---

## **Topic 5: Purchase Process and Buying Center**
### **5.1 The Buying Center (Decision Making Unit)**
A group of individuals within an organization who participate in the purchasing decision.
*   **Initiator:** Starts the process/identifies need.
*   **User:** Actually uses the product (e.g., Radiologist). Focuses on performance/ease.
*   **Influencer:** Defines specs/provides info (e.g., Physicist). Focuses on technical data.
*   **Decider:** Has power to finalize the vendor.
*   **Approver:** Authorizes the action (e.g., Management/Board).
*   **Buyer:** Formal authority to execute paperwork/negotiation (e.g., Procurement Manager). Focuses on price/contract.
*   **Gatekeeper:** Controls flow of information (e.g., Secretary, Purchasing Agent). *Crucial role for access.*

### **5.2 Buying Situations (Buy Classes)**
1.  **Straight Rebuy:** Routine reorder, no changes (e.g., Office paper). Low involvement.
2.  **Modified Rebuy:** Wants to change product specs, price, or terms. Moderate involvement.
3.  **New Task:** Buying for the first time. High risk, complex decision, involves full Buying Center. (e.g., Installing a new ERP system).

### **5.3 The 8-Step Buying Process**
1.  Problem Recognition.
2.  General Need Description.
3.  Product Specification.
4.  Supplier Search.
5.  Proposal Solicitation (Tenders).
6.  Supplier Selection (Technical + Financial Bidding).
7.  Order-Routine Specification.
8.  Performance Review.

## **Topic 6: Types of Buyers in Business Markets**
1.  **Commodity Buyers:**
    *   View products as undifferentiated.
    *   Focus: **Price**. Force vendors to strip value-added services.
    *   *Strategy:* Only serve if you have high volume/scale economy.
2.  **Underperformers:**
    *   Industries with high fixed costs (e.g., Steel, Pharma).
    *   Demand free services/low price; not loyal.
    *   *Strategy:* Avoid. They consume resources without profitability.
3.  **Partners:**
    *   Expensive to serve but loyal.
    *   Want customized, turnkey solutions.
    *   *Strategy:* Invest in long-term relationships.
4.  **Most Valuable Customers (MVCs):**
    *   Loyal and efficient to serve.
    *   Buyers assume some supplier functions (efficiency).
    *   *Strategy:* Retain at all costs; collaborate for mutual growth.
5.  **Institutional Markets:**
    *   Schools, Hospitals, Prisons.
    *   Characteristics: Low budgets, captive clientele.
    *   *Strategy:* Cost minimization (L1 bidder usually wins).

# **Module 6: Strategizing Products and Services**

## **Topic 1: Product Concept**
### **1.1 Definition and Levels of Product**
*   **Definition:** Anything offered to a market to satisfy a need or want.
*   **The Five Product Levels (Customer Value Hierarchy):**
    1.  **Core Benefit:** The fundamental service/benefit the customer is really buying (e.g., Hotel = Sleep/Rest).
    2.  **Basic Product:** Converting the core benefit into a physical product (e.g., Bed, bathroom, towels).
    3.  **Expected Product:** Attributes buyers normally expect (e.g., Clean sheets, working lights).
    4.  **Augmented Product:** Features that exceed expectations and differentiate from competitors (e.g., Free high-speed WiFi, smart TV). *Note: Today’s augmentation becomes tomorrow’s expectation.*
    5.  **Potential Product:** All future transformations/augmentations the product might undergo (e.g., VR conference facility in room).

---

### **1.2 Classification of Consumer Goods**
1.  **Convenience Goods:** Frequent purchase, little effort, low price (e.g., Soap, Newspaper). Includes **Staples** (Rice), **Impulse** (Chocolates), **Emergency** (Umbrella during rain). Strategy: Widespread availability.
2.  **Shopping Goods:** Less frequent, consumer compares suitability, quality, price, and style (e.g., Furniture, Clothing). Strategy: Differentiation.
3.  **Specialty Goods:** Unique characteristics/brand identification; buyer makes special purchase effort (e.g., Luxury cars, Designer suits). Strategy: Exclusive distribution.
4.  **Unsought Goods:** Consumer does not know about or does not normally think of buying (e.g., Life Insurance, Encyclopedias, Vaccines). Strategy: Aggressive selling/awareness.

---

## **Topic 2: Product Line Management**
### **2.1 Product Hierarchy Definitions**
*   **Product Item (SKU):** A distinct unit within a brand or product line (e.g., iPhone 15 Pro Max 256GB).
*   **Product Line:** A group of related products that function similarly or are sold to the same customer group (e.g., Apple’s line of MacBooks).
*   **Product Mix (Assortment):** The set of all products and items a seller offers.

### **2.2 Dimensions of Product Mix**
*   **Width (Breadth):** Number of different product lines (e.g., ITC has Cigarettes, Hotels, FMCG, Paper).
*   **Length:** Total number of items in the mix.
*   **Depth:** Number of variants offered of each product in the line (e.g., Lux soap in 3 sizes and 4 scents = Depth of 12).
*   **Consistency:** How closely related the various product lines are in end-use, production, or distribution.

### **2.3 Line Management Strategies**
*   **Line Stretching:** Lengthening the product line beyond current range.
    *   **Down-market Stretch:** Introducing lower-priced items (e.g., Apple launching iPhone SE).
    *   **Up-market Stretch:** Entering high-end segments (e.g., Maruti launching Nexa/Grand Vitara).
    *   **Two-way Stretch:** Expanding both ways.
*   **Line Filling:** Adding more items within the present range to plug gaps and keep competitors out.
*   **Line Modernization:** Updating product style/technology (e.g., Car facelifts).
*   **Line Pruning:** Removing unprofitable products (Deadwood).
*   **Line Featuring:** Selecting specific items to promote/showcase (e.g., Flagship phones).

## **Topic 3: Branding**
### **3.1 Concept and Role**
*   **Definition:** A name, term, sign, symbol, or design identifying the maker and differentiating them from competitors.
*   **Brand Promise:** The seller’s promise to deliver a specific set of features/benefits consistently.
*   **Role:** Identifies maker, legal protection (Trademark), signals quality, creates barriers to entry, allows price premiums.

---

### **3.2 Building Strong Brands**
*   **Brand Culture:** The story/myth around a brand (e.g., Harley Davidson = Freedom/Rebellion; Saffola = Heart Health).
*   **Brand Equity:** The added value endowed to products/services.
    *   **Constituents:** Brand Awareness, Perceived Quality, Brand Associations, Brand Loyalty.
    *   **CBBE Model (Customer-Based Brand Equity):** Steps: Identity (Who are you?) $\rightarrow$ Meaning (What are you?) $\rightarrow$ Response (Feelings) $\rightarrow$ Relationships (Resonance/Loyalty).
*   **Brand Value:** The financial worth of the brand (Quantitative measure). Models by Interbrand (Financial performance + Role in purchase + Brand strength).

## **Topic 4: Product Life Cycle (PLC)**
### **4.1 The Concept**
*   Products have a limited life, passing through distinct stages: Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline.
*   **Curve Shape:** Typically S-shaped (Sales vs. Time).

### **4.2 Stages and Strategies**
1.  **Introduction Phase:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Low sales, negative/low profits, high cost per customer, innovators buy.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Rapid Skimming:** High Price, High Promotion (e.g., New tech).
        *   **Slow Skimming:** High Price, Low Promotion (e.g., Luxury niche).
        *   **Rapid Penetration:** Low Price, High Promotion (e.g., FMCG mass market).
        *   **Slow Penetration:** Low Price, Low Promotion.
2.  **Growth Phase:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Rapid sales climb, early adopters + early majority, profits peak, competition enters.
    *   **Strategies:** Improve quality, add new features/models (Flankers), enter new segments, lower price slightly to attract price-sensitive buyers.
3.  **Maturity Phase:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Sales peak/flatten, low cost per customer, competition stabilizes, profits decline due to price wars.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Market Modification:** Increase consumption (convert non-users, increase usage rate).
        *   **Product Modification:** Quality, feature, or style improvement.
        *   **Mix Modification:** Adjusting Price, Distribution, or Advertising.
4.  **Decline Phase:**
    *   **Characteristics:** Declining sales, profits erode, laggards buy.
    *   **Strategies:**
        *   **Harvest:** Reduce costs, milk remaining value.
        *   **Divest:** Sell the brand/business.
        *   **Drop:** Liquidate.

## **Topic 5: Service Concept**
### **5.1 Defining Services**
*   **Definition:** Any act/performance that is essentially intangible and does not result in the ownership of anything.
*   **Service Mix Continuum:**
    1.  **Pure Tangible Good:** Soap, Salt.
    2.  **Tangible Good + Service:** Car + Warranty/Repair.
    3.  **Hybrid:** Restaurant (Food + Ambience).
    4.  **Major Service + Goods:** Airline travel (Transport + Food).
    5.  **Pure Service:** Consulting, Massage.

### **5.2 Characteristics of Services (IHIP)**
1.  **Intangibility:** Cannot be seen, tasted, felt, or smelled before buying.
    *   *Strategy:* "Tangibilize the intangible" (Use symbols, evidence, testimonials).
2.  **Inseparability:** Produced and consumed simultaneously.
    *   *Strategy:* Managing provider-client interaction; Training staff.
3.  **Variability:** Quality depends on who provides it, when, where, and how.
    *   *Strategy:* Standardization (SOPs), Training, Automation.
4.  **Perishability:** Cannot be stored for later sale (e.g., Empty airline seat).
    *   *Strategy:* Demand management (Differential pricing for peak/off-peak), Appointment systems.

### **5.3 Classification by Evaluation**
*   **Search Qualities:** Easy to evaluate before purchase (Clothing). High in goods.
*   **Experience Qualities:** Evaluate after consumption (Haircut, Restaurant). High in mixed services.
*   **Credence Qualities:** Hard to evaluate even after consumption (Medical diagnosis, Legal advice). High in pure services.

# **Module 7: Strategizing Pricing and Distribution**

## **Topic 1: Price Mix and Concepts**
### **1.1 Pricing Vocabulary & Framework**
*   **Product Price:** Generally based on Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). Must cover manufacturing and distribution costs.
*   **Perceived Value:** What the customer thinks the product is worth (influenced by marketing/brand).
*   **Customer Incentive to Buy:** Perceived Value minus Product Price.
*   **Firm’s Incentive to Sell:** Product Price minus COGS.
*   **True Economic Value (TEV):** Cost of the *Next Best Alternative* + Value of Performance Differential.
    *   *Strategy:* TEV should ideally be higher than Perceived Value to demonstrate savings/benefit.

### **1.2 Price-Quality Matrix**
*   **Premium Strategy:** High Price + High Quality (e.g., Apple).
*   **High Value Strategy:** Medium Price + High Quality.
*   **Super Value Strategy:** Low Price + High Quality.
*   **Overcharging Strategy:** High Price + Medium Quality.
*   **Rip-off Strategy:** High Price + Low Quality.
*   **Economy Strategy:** Low Price + Low Quality.
*   *Note:* "Quality" is defined by the customer segment, not just the manufacturer.

## **Topic 2: Price Setting Policy**
### **2.1 Six Steps of Price Setting**
1.  **Select Pricing Objective:**
    *   **Survival:** Low price to stay in business.
    *   **Maximum Current Profit (ROI):** Focus on financial return.
    *   **Market Share (Penetration):** Low price to capture volume/growth.
    *   **Market Skimming:** High price to harvest value from innovators/early adopters.
    *   **Product-Quality Leadership:** High price to cover R&D and high quality.
2.  **Determine Demand:**
    *   Understand price sensitivity (Elasticity).
    *   Understand the "Funnel": Need $\rightarrow$ Ability $\rightarrow$ Willingness.
3.  **Estimate Costs:**
    *   **Fixed Costs (Overheads):** Rent, salaries, interest.
    *   **Variable Costs:** Raw materials, wages.
    *   **Total Cost:** Floor for pricing.
4.  **Analyze Competitors:** Compare against competitor costs, prices, and offers.
5.  **Select Pricing Method:** (See Section 2.2).
6.  **Select Final Price:** Determine the specific number.

### **2.2 Pricing Methods**
*   **Cost-Based (The Floor):**
    *   **Markup Pricing:** Cost + Desired Margin % (Common in retail).
    *   **Target Return Pricing:** Price set to achieve a specific ROI on invested capital.
*   **Value-Based (The Ceiling):**
    *   **Perceived Value Pricing:** Based on buyer's perception (e.g., DuPont, Caterpillar).
    *   **Value Pricing:** Low price for high quality (e.g., EDLP - Everyday Low Pricing by Walmart).
*   **Competition-Based:**
    *   **Going-Rate Pricing:** Following market leaders/oligopoly pricing (B2C).
    *   **Sealed-Bid Pricing:** Bidding lower than competitors to win contracts (B2B/Tenders).

### **2.3 Pricing Tactics and Strategies**
*   **Psychological Pricing:**
    *   **Odd Pricing:** Ending in 9 (e.g., 199 vs 200). Signals discount.
    *   **Reference Pricing:** Placing expensive item next to cheaper one to make the latter look good.
*   **Promotional Pricing:**
    *   **Loss Leader:** Selling a well-known brand below cost to drive store footfall.
    *   **Special Event Pricing:** Diwali/Festival sales.
    *   **Low-Interest Financing:** EMIs to reduce purchase pain.
    *   **Warranties/Service Contracts:** Adding value.
*   **Product Mix Pricing:**
    *   **Product Line Pricing:** Price steps between line items (e.g., Surf vs. Surf Excel).
    *   **Optional Feature Pricing:** Base price + Accessories (e.g., Car + Sunroof).
    *   **Captive Product Pricing:** Low price main product, high price consumables (e.g., Razor + Blades; Printer + Ink).
    *   **Two-Part Pricing:** Fixed fee + Variable fee (e.g., Electricity bill, Amusement parks).
    *   **Product Bundling:** Discount for buying items together.
*   **Price Discrimination:** Charging different prices for the same product based on:
    *   Customer Segment (Student/Senior citizen discounts).
    *   Time (Peak vs. Off-peak).
    *   Location (Airport vs. Supermarket).
    *   Channel (Online vs. Offline).

## **Topic 4: Distribution Channel Decisions**
### **4.1 Channel Design Decisions**
*   **1. Channel Length (Levels):**
    *   **Level 0 (Direct Marketing):** Mfr $\rightarrow$ Consumer (Online, Door-to-door). Max control, Min market coverage.
    *   **Level 1:** Mfr $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer (Modern Trade).
    *   **Level 2:** Mfr $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer (General Trade/FMCG).
    *   **Level 3:** Mfr $\rightarrow$ Agent $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer. Min control, Max market coverage.
    *   **Direct Marketing vs. Direct Selling:**
        *   *Direct Marketing:* Non-personal media (Mail, TV, Apps) initiates contact.
        *   *Direct Selling:* Personal contact (Face-to-face) initiates contact.
*   **2. Channel Breadth (Intensity):**
    *   **Exclusive Distribution:** Few outlets. For **Specialty Goods** (e.g., Luxury cars, Bose). High control/service.
    *   **Selective Distribution:** Some outlets. For **Shopping Goods** (e.g., Apparel, Appliances). Balanced approach.
    *   **Intensive Distribution:** As many outlets as possible. For **Convenience Goods** (e.g., Soap, Soft drinks). High volume.
*   **3. Channel Member Selection:**
    *   Based on financial strength, reputation, service capability, and solvency.
*   **4. Channel Management:**
    *   Training, Motivating, Evaluating members.
    *   **Channel Conflict:**
        *   *Vertical:* Conflict between different levels (e.g., Mfr vs. Retailer).
        *   *Horizontal:* Conflict between members at same level (e.g., Retailer A vs. Retailer B price war).

## **Topic 6: Case Study: D'Light**
### **6.1 Background and Product**
*   **Company:** Solar light startup targeting rural poor (founded 2007).
*   **Products:**
    *   **S10:** Small, affordable solar lantern (Breakeven requires high volume: ~1.54 lakh units).
    *   **S250:** Larger, charges mobile phones (Breakeven requires lower volume: ~50k units).
*   **Context:** Target market (Rural India) uses Kerosene. Low trust in new brands. Income is seasonal (harvest) and low. Kerosene expenditure is high (~Rs 250/month), but "savings" is an intangible future concept.

### **6.2 Distribution Strategy Analysis**
*   **Challenge:** Reaching dispersed rural villages efficiently.
*   **Channel Options Evaluated:**
    1.  **Rural Entrepreneurs:** High trust, but low capital/stock capacity.
    2.  **Village Retailers:** High access, but low incentive to push new product/demo.
    3.  **Centralized Shops:** Good service, but customers must travel far.
    4.  **NGOs/Self-Help Groups:** Trustworthy, but lack technical/sales skills.
    5.  **Corporate Partnerships (e.g., Petrol bunks):** Scale, but low product focus.
*   **Solution/Conclusion:** No single channel is perfect.
    *   **Strategy:** **Hybrid Channel Design**. Use a combination of channels to balance trust, reach, and service.
    *   **Product Focus:** Better to sell **S250** (despite higher price) due to better margins/lower breakeven volume compared to S10.

# **Module 8: Strategizing Promotions**

## **Topic 1: Introduction to Promotion and Communication**
### **1.1 Role of Promotion**
*   **Definition:** The means by which firms attempt to **inform, persuade, and remind** consumers about the products and brands they sell.
*   **Connection to PLC (Product Life Cycle):**
    *   **Introduction Stage:** Goal is to **Inform** (Build awareness).
    *   **Growth Stage:** Goal is to **Persuade** (Why buy us vs. competitors?).
    *   **Maturity Stage:** Goal is to **Remind** (Value proposition still holds).
    *   **Decline Stage:** Minimal promotion.

**_QnA:_**

**_Q1. Which of the following is a key objective of promotional strategies in marketing?_**  
**_A1. Inform, persuade, and remind customers_**

### **1.2 The Macro Model of Communication**
*   **Sender:** The company/marketer.
*   **Encoding:** Converting the idea/message into a symbolic form (Ad/Story).
*   **Message/Media:** The actual ad transmitted via TV, Print, Digital, etc.
*   **Decoding:** The receiver interprets the message.
*   **Receiver:** The target consumer.
*   **Feedback:** Consumer’s response (Sales, Sentiment).
*   **Noise:** Distractions/clutter that distort the message.
*   **Key Insight:** Effective communication requires the decoded message to match the encoded message.
    *   *Example (Center Fresh):* A funny ad about a "Human ATM" must clearly decode to the core message: "The gum is tasty/yummy." If the humor overpowers the product message, communication fails.

## **Topic 2: The Marketing Communications Mix (The Bouquet)**
1.  **Advertising:**
    *   **Definition:** Paid form of **non-personal** presentation of ideas, goods, or services by an **identified sponsor**.

    *   **Goal:** Accelerate sales/purchase decision.

5.  **Personal Selling:**
    *   **Definition:** Face-to-face interaction with prospective buyers.
    *   **Characteristics:** Customized, relationship-oriented, immediate feedback. Best for complex/B2B products.
6.  **Direct Marketing:**
    *   **Tools:** Email, Telemarketing, Catalogs, SMS.
    *   **Characteristics:** Customized, up-to-date, interactive.

## **Topic 4: Budgeting**
### **4.1 Methods of Budgeting**

2.  **Percentage of Sales:** Budget is a fixed % of current or projected sales (Most common).

## **Topic 7: Digital Marketing: An Overview**
### **7.1 Outbound Marketing**
